# 📄 Syllabus.txt Explanation

Each subject folder contains a `Syllabus.txt` file with the **complete syllabus for that subject**, organized unit-wise.

For example:

[`Final_Year/Notes/2_Indian_Writings_in_English/Syllabus.txt`](https://github.com/madhurimarawat/MA-English-Notes/blob/main/Final_Year/Notes/2_Indian_Writings_in_English/Syllabus.txt)

The file contains the complete list of authors, texts, poems, novels, plays, and other prescribed works, along with their **study status** and **exam relevance**.

---

## 🚀 How to Use

> [!NOTE]
>
> 1. Open the `Syllabus.txt` file inside the subject folder.
> 2. Check the syllabus unit by unit.
> 3. Look at the abbreviation given after each text:
>
>    * `D` = Detailed Study
>    * `ND` = Non-Detailed Study
>    * `S` = Studied & Notes Completed
> 4. Use the file to understand **what is included in each unit and how the texts are treated in the exam**.
> 5. For detailed information about the examination pattern, refer to the appropriate exam-format document:
>
>    * **Previous Year:** [`MA_Syllabus_Exam_Format_Previous_Year.md`](https://github.com/madhurimarawat/MA-English-Notes/blob/main/Previous_Year/Notes/MA_Syllabus_Exam_Format_Previous_Year.md)
>    * **Final Year:** [`MA_Syllabus_Exam_Format_Final_Year.md`](https://github.com/madhurimarawat/MA-English-Notes/blob/main/Final_Year/Notes/MA_Syllabus_Exam_Format_Final_Year.md)
>
> These documents explain how the syllabus is organized for the examination, including how **Detailed Study (D)** and **Non-Detailed Study (ND)** portions are used in the question paper.

## 📚 What the File Contains

The `Syllabus.txt` file contains the **whole syllabus in a simple, readable format**, divided into five units.

Each unit lists the prescribed authors and their works.

For example:

```txt
INDIAN WRITINGS IN ENGLISH – SYLLABUS

==================================================

UNIT I

Toru Dutt
Sita - D
The Lotus - D
Our Casuarina Tree - D

Rabindranath Tagore - S
Gitanjali - Songs 1 to 15 - D

Sarojini Naidu
Indian Dancers - D
Love and Death - D
The Old Woman - ND

========================================

UNIT II

Jayanta Mahapatra
Indian Summer - ND
A Missing Person - ND
The Logic - ND

Kamala Das
The Freaks - ND
A Hot Noon in Malabar - ND
The Looking Glass - ND
The Sunshine Cat - ND

Nissim Ezekiel - S
Enterprise - D
Poet, Lover, Birdwatcher - D
Night of the Scorpion - D

========================================
```

This makes it easy to see:

* Which authors are included
* Which texts belong to each unit
* Which works are **Detailed Study (D)**
* Which works are **Non-Detailed Study (ND)**
* Which authors/texts have already been studied and have notes completed

---

## 📝 What D and ND Mean for the Exam

The `D` and `ND` labels are important because they indicate **how the prescribed texts are used in the examination**.

### 🔹 D = Detailed Study

`D` means **Detailed Study**.

These are texts that are studied in depth and are used for **extract/excerpt-based questions** in the examination.

For example:

```txt
Toru Dutt
Sita - D
The Lotus - D
Our Casuarina Tree - D
```

Questions may provide an **excerpt or passage from one of these detailed-study texts**, and you may be required to identify, explain, interpret, or discuss the given extract according to the examination pattern.

---

### 🔹 ND = Non-Detailed Study

`ND` means **Non-Detailed Study**.

These works are not treated in the same detailed extract-based manner as the `D` texts.

Instead, they are used for **separate unit-wise questions**, according to the examination pattern described in:

`MA_Syllabus_Exam_Format_Final_Year.md`

For example:

```txt
Jayanta Mahapatra
Indian Summer - ND
A Missing Person - ND
The Logic - ND
```

These works form part of the prescribed syllabus and can be examined through the **ND portions of the relevant units**.

---

### 🔹 S = Studied & Notes Completed

`S` means **Studied & Notes Completed**.

This is a progress/status marker used in the notes repository.

For example:

```txt
Rabindranath Tagore - S
Nissim Ezekiel - S
A. P. J. Abdul Kalam - S
```

It indicates that the author's relevant material has been **studied and the notes have been completed** in the repository.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> `S` is **not an examination category** like `D` or `ND`.
>
> It simply indicates the **study/notes completion status**.

---

## 📖 Example

A section such as:

```txt
UNIT IV

Girish Karnad - S
Nagamandala - D

Shashi Deshpande
The Dark Holds No Terrors - ND

Mahesh Dattani
Final Solutions - ND
```

can be understood as:

* **Girish Karnad** → notes/study completed
* **Nagamandala** → Detailed Study → relevant for detailed/extract-based examination
* **The Dark Holds No Terrors** → Non-Detailed Study
* **Final Solutions** → Non-Detailed Study

The `S` marker tells you about the **progress of the notes**, while `D` and `ND` tell you about the **study/examination category**.

---

## ➖ Unit Separators

The `====` lines are used as **visual separators between each syllabus unit**.

For example:

```txt
UNIT I

Toru Dutt
Sita - D
The Lotus - D
Our Casuarina Tree - D

========================================

UNIT II

Jayanta Mahapatra
Indian Summer - ND
A Missing Person - ND
The Logic - ND
```

The `========================================` line simply indicates that **Unit I has ended and Unit II begins**.

It does **not** represent a text, topic, question, or examination category.

This makes the plain-text syllabus easier to read and helps clearly distinguish the five units:

```txt
UNIT I
========================================
UNIT II
========================================
UNIT III
========================================
UNIT IV
========================================
UNIT V
```

> [!TIP]
>
> Think of `========================================` as a **page/section divider**. It is only used to visually separate one unit from the next.

## 🗂️ Why Every Subject Folder Has a Syllabus.txt

Each subject has its own `Syllabus.txt` so that you can understand the contents of that subject **without having to search through all the notes**.

The structure is:

```txt
Subject Folder/
│
├── Syllabus.txt
├── Notes/
├── ...
```

The `Syllabus.txt` acts as a quick reference for the entire subject.

You can open it first to understand:

**What is in the subject → Which unit it belongs to → D or ND → Whether notes are completed.**

## 📌 In Short

| Marker | Meaning                   | Purpose                                                                  |
| ------ | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `D`    | Detailed Study            | Text studied in detail; excerpts/extracts may be used in the examination |
| `ND`   | Non-Detailed Study        | Text included in the syllabus and examined through the ND/unit pattern   |
| `S`    | Studied & Notes Completed | Shows that notes/study for that author or section are completed          |

So, **`Syllabus.txt` is the complete syllabus map for each subject**, while the exam-format document explains **how those syllabus portions are used in the actual examination**.
