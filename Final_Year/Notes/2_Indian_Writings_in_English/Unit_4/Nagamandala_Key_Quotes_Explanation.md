## 🔥 Naga-Mandala — memorable quotes + meaning

| Quote / key line                                              | What it shows                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **“I am king of the underworld, but a beggar at your door.”** | **Naga’s love for Rani** — despite his supernatural power, he becomes helpless before his desire for her.                             |
| **“I can take any form I want.”**                             | **Naga’s supernatural identity** — he can cross the boundaries between human and animal worlds.                                       |
| **“A woman’s life is a closed room.”**                        | **Rani’s oppression** — her marriage has isolated her and denied her freedom, companionship and desire.                               |
| **“You are my wife.”**                                        | **Possession and marriage** — both Appanna and Naga claim Rani, raising the question of who has the right to define her relationship. |
| **“I have been waiting for you.”**                            | **Longing and desire** — Naga’s relationship with Rani is driven by emotional and physical longing.                                   |
| **“What does a woman want?”**                                 | **Female desire and identity** — the play questions whether women are allowed to have desires of their own.                           |
| **“She became a goddess.”**                                   | **Rani’s transformation** — the woman who was powerless inside her marriage becomes socially powerful through the snake ordeal.       |
| **“The serpent is the lover.”**                               | **Myth + sexuality** — the Naga represents the love and sexual fulfilment missing from Rani’s marriage.                               |
| **“Stories have their own lives.”**                           | **Power of storytelling** — the play itself is framed through stories, showing how stories can shape reality.                         |
| **“The flames are alive.”**                                   | **Folklore and magic** — the personified flames establish the magical, imaginative world of the play.                                 |
| **“She is pure.”**                                            | **The snake ordeal** — Rani’s innocence is publicly established despite the ambiguity surrounding her relationships.                  |
| **“He doesn't touch me.”**                                    | **Rani’s unhappy marriage** — Appanna’s neglect makes her emotional isolation clear.                                                  |

### 🐍 Especially useful for **Naga**

**“I am king of the underworld, but a beggar at your door.”**

This is probably your **BEST quote** for Naga. The contrast between **“king”** and **“beggar”** is what makes it powerful. Naga possesses supernatural authority, yet love makes him vulnerable and dependent on Rani.

### 👩 For **Rani**

**“A woman’s life is a closed room.”**

Use this when discussing **patriarchy, marriage, isolation, or female oppression**. Rani literally lives in a confined domestic space, but the “closed room” also represents the restrictions placed on women by society.

### 🐍❤️ For **Naga + Rani**

**“I can take any form I want.”**

This is great for discussing **identity and transformation**. Naga changes his physical form, but the play also makes us question whether identity is determined by appearance, social role, or emotional relationship.

### 👑 For Rani's ending

**“She became a goddess.”**

This is excellent for an answer on **Rani’s transformation**. At the beginning, she is treated almost like property; by the end, the same community that could have condemned her instead turns her into a sacred figure.

**Exam hack:** Don't just dump the quote. Do:

> **“I am king of the underworld, but a beggar at your door.”** The paradox of “king” and “beggar” captures how love strips Naga of his supernatural power and makes him emotionally dependent on Rani.
