# 🌙 *Nagamandala* — Characters in Detail

## 1. RANI — The Protagonist

### 👤 Appearance / Identity

Rani is a **young, newly married woman**. She comes from a traditional background and becomes Appanna's wife. Her name literally means **"queen"**, which is ironic because she is treated more like a servant than a queen.

She is physically young and attractive, but more importantly, she is initially **innocent, inexperienced and emotionally vulnerable**.

### 🧠 Personality & Qualities

* **Innocent** — She enters marriage believing that a husband should love and protect his wife.
* **Lonely** — Appanna completely neglects her.
* **Imaginative** — Her dreams and fantasies become important ways of escaping her unhappy reality.
* **Emotional** — She longs for affection and companionship.
* **Adaptable** — She gradually learns how to survive within the oppressive social system.
* **Courageous** — By the end, she successfully handles a situation that could have destroyed her.
* **Intelligent** — She learns to use society's expectations to protect herself.
* **Complex** — She is neither simply a victim nor simply a rebellious woman.

### 🔥 Transformation

Rani's character changes dramatically:

**Beginning:**

> Innocent → frightened → lonely → submissive

**Middle:**

> Curious → emotionally awakened → secretly involved with Naga

**End:**

> Confident → respected → powerful → almost mythical

At the beginning, she has little control over her life. At the end, **she becomes the centre of her household and community.**

### 🐍 Symbolism

Rani represents:

* **The oppressed Indian woman**
* **Female sexuality and desire**
* **The search for love**
* **Transformation**
* **The conflict between social morality and personal happiness**

Her relationship with Naga also challenges the traditional idea that a wife can only have one kind of identity: **"faithful wife."**

### 👑 Why is she called Rani?

Her name means **queen**, but initially she has no power.

This creates an important irony:

**Rani = Queen in name → powerless woman in reality → Queen in the end**

By the conclusion, she actually acquires the status suggested by her name.

### ⭐ Exam point

Rani's journey can be interpreted as a movement from **patriarchal oppression to female agency**.

---

# 2. APPANNA — Rani's Husband

### 👤 Appearance / Identity

Appanna is Rani's husband. He is an ordinary village man but behaves like the **master of the household**.

He spends most of his time outside the house and returns mainly to eat and sleep.

He is married to Rani but does not behave like a loving husband.

### 🧠 Personality & Qualities

* **Authoritarian**
* **Self-centred**
* **Emotionally cold**
* **Controlling**
* **Insensitive**
* **Patriarchal**
* **Possessive**
* **Hypocritical**

The biggest contradiction in Appanna is that:

> He gives Rani almost no love, but expects complete loyalty from her.

### 💔 Relationship with Rani

Appanna treats marriage as a form of **ownership rather than companionship**.

He expects:

* obedience
* sexual fidelity
* domestic service

But he does not provide:

* emotional intimacy
* companionship
* affection

He even openly visits another woman.

### 🧩 Symbolism

Appanna represents:

**Patriarchy + male authority + hypocrisy**

He embodies a social system in which:

> A man can violate social rules while demanding that a woman obey them.

### 🔥 Important contrast

**Appanna = biological husband**

**Naga = emotional/romantic lover**

This creates one of the central conflicts of the play.

### 👤 Appearance / Identity

Naga is a **male cobra** who falls in love with Rani.

Through magical means, he is able to assume **Appanna's physical appearance**.

Therefore, Rani believes she is meeting her husband.

This creates the central magical ambiguity of the play.

### 🧠 Personality

Naga is:

* Romantic
* Passionate
* Sensitive
* Imaginative
* Jealous
* Possessive
* Protective
* Vulnerable

Unlike Appanna, Naga gives Rani the emotional attention she desperately wants.

### ❤️ Relationship with Rani

Naga gives Rani:

* companionship
* affection
* intimacy
* emotional connection
* sexual awakening

For Rani, Naga becomes the **husband she emotionally wanted**, even though he is biologically a snake.

### 🐍 Symbolism

Naga is the most symbolically complicated character.

He can represent:

### 1. Desire

The snake becomes an embodiment of Rani's suppressed sexual and emotional desires.

### 2. Nature

Naga represents the natural world invading a rigid social world.

### 3. Transformation

He constantly changes his form.

### 4. Male fantasy

He becomes the idealised version of Appanna that Rani desires.

### 5. Forbidden sexuality

He represents a relationship outside conventional marriage.

### 6. Divine / supernatural power

The snake is also connected with Indian folklore and serpent worship.

# 4. KURUDAVVA — The Old Blind Woman

### 👤 Appearance / Identity

Kurudavva is an **old blind woman** in the village.

She is one of the most interesting characters because despite her blindness, she often understands people's problems better than those who can see.

She has magical roots that allow her to help Rani.

### 🧠 Personality & Qualities

* Kind
* Compassionate
* Practical
* Clever
* Experienced
* Helpful
* Resourceful
* Superstitious
* Maternal

She acts almost like a **mother figure** to Rani.

### ❤️ Relationship with Rani

Kurudavva recognises Rani's loneliness and wants to help her.

She gives Rani the magical root that eventually leads to her relationship with Naga.

Ironically, her attempt to help Rani creates the central supernatural situation of the play.

### 👁️ Symbolism of Blindness

Kurudavva's blindness is highly symbolic.

She **cannot physically see**, but she understands human suffering.

This creates an important irony:

> **The blind woman sees the truth better than the sighted people.**

Her blindness may represent:

* wisdom beyond physical vision
* intuitive understanding
* social marginalisation
* women's shared experience

### 👵 Representation

Kurudavva represents the **older generation of women**, especially women who have learned to survive within patriarchal society.

Unlike Rani, she understands how the social world operates.

---

# 5. KAPPANNA — Kurudavva's Son

### 👤 Identity

Kappanna is Kurudavva's son.

He is a relatively minor character but is important in establishing Kurudavva's social and family context.

### 🧠 Qualities

* Dutiful
* Practical
* Protective
* Ordinary
* Connected to village life

He helps his mother and participates in the everyday social world surrounding Rani.

### 🧩 Symbolism

Kappanna mainly represents:

* ordinary rural society
* family relationships
* traditional village life

He is not as symbolically complex as Rani, Naga or Appanna.

---

# 6. THE OLD MAN

### 👤 Identity

The Old Man is one of the villagers who becomes involved when Rani's strange story becomes a public matter.

He represents the **traditional male authority of the village**.

### 🧠 Qualities

* Conservative
* Practical
* Socially authoritative
* Traditional

### 🧩 Representation

He represents the community's tendency to:

* judge individuals
* enforce social norms
* decide what is acceptable

He is important because Rani's private life eventually becomes a **public/social issue**.

---

# 7. THE SECOND OLD MAN

There are several older village figures involved in the public discussion surrounding Rani.

They collectively represent:

> **The conservative village community and its social judgment.**

Their importance is less about individual personality and more about what they represent collectively.

### Symbolism

They represent:

* tradition
* social morality
* patriarchy
* collective judgment
* superstition

---

# 8. THE VILLAGERS

The villagers are essentially a **collective character**.

They observe, gossip about and judge Rani.

When Rani's pregnancy becomes known, the villagers become particularly important.

### 🧠 Qualities

As a group, they are:

* curious
* judgmental
* superstitious
* traditional
* easily influenced
* fascinated by the supernatural

### 🧩 Symbolism

They represent **society itself**.

The villagers demonstrate how society can control a woman's life through:

**gossip → suspicion → judgment → punishment/acceptance**

---

# 9. THE CHILD

The child born to Rani is also significant.

The child is biologically ambiguous because of the circumstances surrounding Rani's relationship with Naga.

### 🧩 Symbolism

The child represents:

* reconciliation
* continuation of life
* union of opposing worlds
* acceptance
* transformation

The child also complicates the distinction between:

**human / animal**

and

**husband / lover**

---

# 10. THE MANGOOSE

The mongoose appears in relation to Naga's world and is important because of the traditional association between **mongoose and snake**.

### 🐍 vs 🦦 Symbolism

Snake and mongoose traditionally represent enemies.

Therefore, the mongoose contributes to the larger theme of:

> **Conflict between opposing forces.**

It also reinforces the connection between the play's supernatural world and Indian folk traditions.

---

# 11. THE SNAKES

The snakes are not merely animals.

They form part of the **mythical and folkloric universe** of the play.

### 🐍 Symbolism

Snakes traditionally carry multiple meanings in Indian culture:

* fertility
* sexuality
* rebirth
* immortality
* supernatural power
* danger
* mystery

In *Nagamandala*, the snake becomes particularly connected with **sexuality, transformation and desire**.

---

# 12. THE STORY / FLAME / ANIMATE STORY

This is an important part of Karnad's dramatic structure.

The play begins with a **Story** and a **Flame**, who are personified.

They are not ordinary human characters but are part of the frame narrative.

### 🔥 Flame

The Flame is connected with:

* light
* knowledge
* storytelling
* life
* supernatural imagination

### 📖 Story

The Story is effectively a **personified storyteller**.

It demonstrates Karnad's use of **folk theatre traditions**.

The Story becomes something that can move, speak and interact with the world.

### 🎭 Why is this important?

Karnad does not simply tell a folk tale.

He makes the process of **storytelling itself part of the drama**.

So there are effectively multiple levels:

**Storyteller → Story → Rani's story → audience**

This creates a play-within-a-play feeling.

---

# 13. THE ANIMATE FLAMES

The flames that come alive after listening to stories are another example of Karnad's magical realism.

They represent:

* imagination
* storytelling
* oral tradition
* the power of narrative
* the boundary between reality and fantasy

The idea is basically:

> **Stories have life.**

# 🧠 THE MOST IMPORTANT CHARACTER RELATIONSHIPS

### Rani ↔ Appanna

**Legal marriage**

But:

> No emotional intimacy.

Represents **marriage without love**.

---

### Rani ↔ Naga

**Emotional + sexual relationship**

Represents:

> **Desire, fantasy and emotional fulfilment.**

---

### Rani ↔ Kurudavva

**Young woman ↔ older woman**

Represents:

> **Female solidarity and transmission of women's knowledge.**

---

### Appanna ↔ Naga

They are almost **opposite versions of masculinity**.

| Appanna       | Naga                 |
| ------------- | -------------------- |
| Human         | Snake                |
| Legal husband | Secret lover         |
| Cruel         | Loving               |
| Cold          | Passionate           |
| Reality       | Fantasy              |
| Patriarchal   | Romantic             |
| Neglects Rani | Gives Rani attention |

This contrast is **VERY important for exams**.

# 🌟 RANI'S CHARACTER ARC

You can write this in an exam:

**Rani → Lonely wife → Curious woman → Lover → Pregnant woman → Accused woman → Socially tested woman → Respected mother → Powerful figure**

Her transformation is the **heart of the play**.

# 🎯 One-line exam interpretations

**Rani:**

> Rani is a symbol of the oppressed woman who gradually discovers her own agency within a patriarchal society.

**Appanna:**

> Appanna represents the hypocrisy and authoritarianism of patriarchy.

**Naga:**

> Naga represents the mysterious intersection of desire, sexuality, fantasy and transformation.

**Kurudavva:**

> Kurudavva represents the wisdom and resilience of women who survive within traditional society.

**Villagers:**

> The villagers represent society's tendency to judge women according to rigid moral codes.

**Story:**

> The Story represents the power of folk narrative and imagination.

**Flame:**

> The Flame symbolises the vitality of storytelling and the transformation of ordinary reality into art.

### 💡 The BIG idea connecting everyone

The whole play revolves around the tension between:

**Reality ↔ Fantasy**
**Human ↔ Animal**
**Husband ↔ Lover**
**Patriarchy ↔ Female desire**
**Tradition ↔ Individual freedom**
**Society ↔ Individual**
**Story ↔ Reality**

And **Rani stands at the centre of all these conflicts**.
