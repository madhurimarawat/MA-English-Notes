# 📖 *The English Teacher* — R. K. Narayan

## All Characters in Detail

# 1. KRISHNA — The English Teacher / Protagonist

### 👤 Appearance / Identity

Krishna is the **narrator and protagonist** of the novel.

He is an English lecturer at **Albert Mission College in Malgudi**. He is married to Susila and later becomes the father of their daughter, Leela.

He initially lives a fairly ordinary, respectable middle-class life.

### 🧠 Personality & Qualities

Krishna is:

* Educated
* Sensitive
* Intelligent
* Romantic
* Imaginative
* Introspective
* Occasionally lazy
* Frustrated with institutional education
* Deeply attached to his family
* Spiritually curious

He is not presented as a perfect hero. He is a **very ordinary man experiencing extraordinary emotional and spiritual changes**.

---

## 📚 Krishna as a Teacher

Krishna becomes increasingly dissatisfied with the education system.

He feels that formal education often:

* suppresses creativity
* forces students into rigid systems
* values examinations over genuine learning
* turns teachers into mechanical workers

He wants education to become more **natural, creative and humane**.

This becomes especially important when he meets the **Headmaster of the school**.

---

## ❤️ Krishna as a Husband

His relationship with Susila is the emotional centre of the novel.

At first, Krishna is happy to have her back in his life after their separation.

He enjoys:

* talking with her
* walking with her
* sharing domestic life
* planning their future

He is deeply affected by her death.

---

## 💔 Krishna after Susila's Death

This is where his character changes dramatically.

Initially:

**Krishna = ordinary husband + teacher**

After Susila's death:

**Krishna = grieving husband → seeker → spiritual explorer**

He becomes interested in communicating with Susila's spirit.

Eventually, he experiences a sense of spiritual communication with her.

---

## 🧩 Symbolism

Krishna represents:

### 1. The modern educated Indian

He is educated but dissatisfied with the institutions around him.

### 2. The individual's search for meaning

His life moves from ordinary material concerns toward spiritual understanding.

### 3. The conflict between institutional education and true learning

He teaches English but increasingly questions the educational system.

### 4. Human grief

His suffering after Susila's death is central to his transformation.

### 5. Spiritual awakening

His journey ultimately becomes:

> **Education → Love → Loss → Grief → Spiritual discovery**

---

# ⭐ Krishna's Character Arc

Memorise this:

**Teacher → Husband → Father → Grieving widower → Spiritual seeker → More peaceful individual**

This is probably the **most important character development in the novel**.

---

# 2. SUSILA — Krishna's Wife

### 👤 Appearance / Identity

Susila is Krishna's wife and one of the most important characters even though she dies relatively early in the novel.

She is:

* young
* attractive
* intelligent
* affectionate
* domestic
* emotionally sensitive

She represents the emotional centre of Krishna's life.

---

# ❤️ Personality & Qualities

Susila is:

* Loving
* Caring
* Practical
* Sensitive
* Intelligent
* Independent-minded
* Affectionate
* Responsible

She is not merely a stereotypical wife.

She has her own opinions and personality.

---

# 🏠 Susila as a Wife

She and Krishna develop a warm domestic relationship.

Their relationship is based on:

**companionship + affection + everyday intimacy**

Narayan deliberately focuses on ordinary moments rather than dramatic romance.

That makes their relationship feel realistic.

---

# 💔 Susila's Death

Susila becomes ill after visiting a house that is poorly maintained and unhygienic.

She eventually dies.

Her death completely changes Krishna.

This is one of the most important events in the novel.

---

# 👻 Susila after Death

Although physically absent, Susila continues to influence Krishna.

Krishna believes that he can communicate with her spirit.

Therefore, Susila becomes:

> **physically absent but spiritually present.**

This is crucial to the novel's spiritual dimension.

---

# 🧩 Symbolism

Susila represents:

* Love
* Emotional intimacy
* Domestic happiness
* Memory
* Spiritual continuity
* The possibility that love survives physical death

Her role demonstrates that:

> **Death can end physical presence without necessarily ending emotional connection.**

---

# ⭐ Exam point

Susila is not simply a dead wife.

She becomes the **spiritual presence that transforms Krishna's understanding of life and death**.

---

# 3. LEELA — Krishna and Susila's Daughter

### 👧 Appearance / Identity

Leela is Krishna and Susila's young daughter.

She is a lively, innocent child.

She becomes particularly important after Susila's death.

---

# 🧠 Personality & Qualities

Leela is:

* Innocent
* Playful
* Curious
* Energetic
* Affectionate
* Imaginative
* Spontaneous

Her behaviour is very different from the rigid educational environment surrounding Krishna.

---

# 🧒 Leela and Education

Leela's presence helps Krishna question conventional education.

She learns naturally through:

* play
* curiosity
* observation
* experience

This connects directly to Krishna's criticism of formal education.

---

# 🧩 Symbolism

Leela represents:

### 1. Childhood

Natural curiosity and freedom.

### 2. Natural education

Children learn naturally before institutions impose rigid structures.

### 3. Hope

After Susila's death, Leela gives Krishna a reason to continue living.

### 4. The future

She represents a new generation that might experience a better form of education.

---

# ⭐ Important contrast

**School education:**

> Rules → examinations → discipline → memorisation

**Leela's learning:**

> Curiosity → play → experience → freedom

This contrast is central to Narayan's educational philosophy.

---

# 4. THE HEADMASTER

### 👤 Identity

The Headmaster runs the school where Krishna eventually becomes associated with a different educational philosophy.

He is one of the most important secondary characters.

---

# 🧠 Personality & Qualities

The Headmaster is:

* Intelligent
* Progressive
* Idealistic
* Calm
* Practical
* Compassionate
* Strongly opposed to conventional education

He believes children should be educated in a more natural environment.

---

# 📚 Educational Philosophy

The Headmaster believes:

> Children should be allowed to learn naturally rather than being crushed by excessive discipline and examinations.

He dislikes:

* rote learning
* rigid discipline
* unnecessary examinations
* excessive authority

---

# 🧩 Symbolism

The Headmaster represents:

**Alternative education + freedom + natural learning**

He is essentially the opposite of the traditional educational institution Krishna works within.

---

# 🔥 Headmaster vs Albert Mission College

| Albert Mission College | Headmaster's philosophy |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Formal                 | Natural                 |
| Rigid                  | Flexible                |
| Examination-centred    | Child-centred           |
| Institutional          | Individual              |
| Mechanical             | Creative                |
| Authority              | Freedom                 |

This contrast is **very useful in exam answers**.

---

# 5. KRISHNA'S FATHER-IN-LAW

### 👤 Identity

He is Susila's father and therefore Krishna's father-in-law.

He becomes particularly important after Susila's death.

---

# 🧠 Qualities

He is:

* Practical
* Caring
* Traditional
* Concerned about family
* Emotionally affected by Susila's death

He represents the wider family structure surrounding Krishna and Susila.

---

# 🧩 Symbolism

He represents:

* traditional family values
* familial responsibility
* social support
* continuity after death

---

# 6. KRISHNA'S MOTHER-IN-LAW

She is Susila's mother.

She is emotionally affected by her daughter's death and is concerned about Leela.

### Qualities

* Loving
* Maternal
* Traditional
* Protective
* Emotionally sensitive

### Symbolism

She represents:

> **Motherhood, family attachment and grief.**

---

# 7. KRISHNA'S FATHER

Krishna's father is part of the traditional family environment.

He is less central to the plot but contributes to the portrayal of the Indian middle-class family.

### Represents:

* tradition
* family authority
* conventional values
* generational expectations

---

# 8. THE SWAMI / SPIRITUAL MEDIUM

The spiritual figure/medium becomes important when Krishna searches for a way to communicate with Susila after her death.

He provides Krishna with a framework through which he can understand spiritual communication.

### 🧠 Represents

* spirituality
* faith
* the supernatural
* the possibility of life beyond physical existence

However, Narayan deliberately leaves some ambiguity about whether Krishna's experiences are genuinely supernatural or psychological.

That ambiguity is **VERY important**.

---

# 9. THE AYAH

The **ayah** helps take care of Leela.

She becomes part of the domestic environment after Susila's death.

### Qualities

* Caring
* Practical
* Responsible
* Maternal

### Representation

She represents:

> **The invisible labour of women who maintain households and care for children.**

---

# 10. THE DOCTOR

The doctor is connected with Susila's illness and treatment.

### Representation

He represents:

* modern medicine
* the limitations of human knowledge
* society's attempt to control illness and death

Despite medical intervention, Susila dies.

This reinforces one of the novel's central questions:

> **Can human beings really control life and death?**

---

# 11. ALBERT MISSION COLLEGE AUTHORITIES

These are not necessarily developed as individual personalities, but collectively they are important.

They represent:

* colonial educational structures
* bureaucracy
* institutional authority
* rigid academic systems

Krishna's frustration with them reflects his dissatisfaction with conventional education.

---

# 12. KRISHNA'S COLLEAGUES

Krishna's fellow teachers provide the background of academic life.

They represent:

* conventional teachers
* institutional routine
* academic competition
* the mechanical nature of formal education

They help highlight Krishna's growing dissatisfaction.

---

# 13. THE SCHOOL CHILDREN

The children in the Headmaster's school are important collectively.

They represent:

* innocence
* natural curiosity
* freedom
* creativity
* the possibility of educational reform

They support the novel's central argument that **children should learn naturally rather than merely memorise information**.

# 🔥 THE BIGGEST THEMES THROUGH THE CHARACTERS

### 1. Education

Krishna + Headmaster + Children

↓

**What should education actually do?**

Narayan criticises education that only produces examination results.

---

### 2. Love and Marriage

Krishna + Susila

↓

**Marriage should be based on companionship and emotional connection.**

---

### 3. Death

Susila's death + Krishna's grief

↓

**Does death completely destroy a relationship?**

The novel suggests that emotional/spiritual bonds can continue.

---

### 4. Spirituality

Krishna + Susila's spirit + spiritual medium

↓

**The novel moves from rational everyday life toward spiritual possibility.**

---

### 5. Individual Freedom

Krishna's dissatisfaction with college

↓

**The individual should not become a machine controlled by institutions.**

---

# ⭐ KRISHNA'S COMPLETE CHARACTER ARC

This is the one to memorise for your exam:

> **Krishna begins as a conventional English teacher who is dissatisfied with his profession. His marriage to Susila gives him emotional fulfilment, but her sudden death plunges him into grief. Through his suffering and attempts to communicate with Susila's spirit, he develops a deeper spiritual understanding of life and death. His association with the Headmaster and his concern for Leela also transform his understanding of education. Thus, Krishna's journey is from conventionality to emotional maturity and spiritual awareness.**
