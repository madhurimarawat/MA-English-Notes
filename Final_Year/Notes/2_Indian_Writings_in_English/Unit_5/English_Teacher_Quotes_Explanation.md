## 📖 *The English Teacher* — Most Important Quotes

### 1. “I could no longer stuff Shakespeare and Elizabethan metre and Romantic poetry…”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** Krishna becomes frustrated with teaching English literature mechanically. He feels that students are forced to memorise literary facts instead of developing their minds.

**Use for:**

* Criticism of colonial education
* Krishna's dissatisfaction with his job
* Education vs rote learning
* Colonialism

The passage continues with Krishna describing such education as feeding students the “dead mutton” of literary analysis.

**⭐ Exam idea:**

> Krishna rejects an education system that values memorisation over genuine intellectual development.

---

### 2. “This education had reduced us to a nation of morons…”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** Krishna strongly criticises the colonial educational system because it has disconnected Indians from their own culture while making them imitate another culture.

**Use for:**

* Colonialism
* Education
* Cultural alienation
* National identity

He describes Indians as being strangers to their own culture and “camp followers of another culture.”

**🔥 VERY IMPORTANT QUOTE.**

---

### 3. “I am up against the system, the whole method and approach of a system of education…”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** Krishna isn't simply complaining about being a teacher. He is questioning the **entire philosophy of the educational system**.

He believes the system produces efficient clerks rather than independent thinkers.

**Use for:**

* Educational reform
* Colonial education
* Krishna's character
* Theme of freedom

This is one of the strongest quotations for an essay on education.

---

### 4. “Don’t worry so much about these things – they are trash…”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** Krishna tells a student that examinations and academic details are ultimately insignificant compared with the much larger questions of **life and death**.

The important continuation is essentially:

> We are forced to study these things while the fundamental problem of living and dying remains before us.

**Use for:**

* Krishna's changing philosophy
* Education
* Life and death
* Existential concerns

This quotation is specifically highlighted in an official Pearson examination mark scheme.

---

### 5. “Living like a cow.”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** At the beginning, Krishna describes his monotonous existence through this image.

His life is:

**routine → teaching → eating → sleeping → repeating**

He feels that something is missing.

**Use for:**

* Krishna's initial character
* Boredom
* Monotony
* Meaninglessness of institutional life

The examination material identifies this as part of Krishna's early sense of dissatisfaction.

---

### 6. “I have the feeling of a crow flying in a storm.”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** This expresses Krishna's sense of **confusion, instability and helplessness**.

The image of the crow struggling through a storm captures his emotional condition.

**Use for:**

* Krishna's mental state
* Grief
* Confusion
* Symbolism

This quotation is among the commonly cited passages from the novel.

---

### 7. “A continuous sense of something missing.”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** Before Susila arrives, Krishna has everything that should make an educated man comfortable, but he still feels incomplete.

This becomes important because Susila initially fills that emotional emptiness.

**Use for:**

* Krishna before marriage
* Loneliness
* Search for meaning
* Character development

The phrase is cited in examination material discussing Krishna's initial life.

---

### 8. “The boundaries of our personalities suddenly dissolved.”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**, describing his final spiritual experience with Susila.

**Meaning:** Krishna feels that the separation between himself and Susila disappears.

It represents the ultimate union of their consciousness.

**Use for:**

* Spirituality
* Love beyond death
* Ending of the novel
* Krishna and Susila's relationship

This is **one of the BEST quotations for the ending**.

---

### 9. “It was a moment of rare, immutable joy.”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** After his spiritual experience with Susila, Krishna experiences a profound peace and happiness that is beyond ordinary physical experience.

**“Immutable”** means **unchanging / permanent**.

So the phrase suggests that Krishna has reached a kind of spiritual peace.

**Use for:**

* Ending
* Spiritual enlightenment
* Acceptance of death
* Krishna's transformation

The ending is closely associated with Krishna's feeling of being grateful to **“life and death.”** 

---

### 10. “For you as ever.”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**

**Meaning:** Krishna offers the garland to Susila's spirit as an expression of continuing love.

Even though Susila has died physically, Krishna's love for her remains unchanged.

**Use for:**

* Love
* Death
* Memory
* Spiritual connection
* Ending

This is a beautiful short quote because you can easily insert it into an answer about Susila.

---

# 👩 SUSILA — Important Quote

### 11. “House-keeping was a grand affair for her.”

**Who says it:** **Krishna**, describing Susila.

**Meaning:** Susila takes great pride and satisfaction in managing the household.

Her domestic responsibilities are not presented merely as chores; they become an important part of her identity.

**Use for:**

* Susila's character
* Indian domestic life
* Marriage
* Womanhood

The longer passage describes the satisfaction she gets from running an orderly household.

---

# 👨‍🏫 THE HEADMASTER — Important Quotes

### 12. “Leave them alone.”

**Who says it:** **The Headmaster**

**Meaning:** This is essentially the Headmaster's educational philosophy.

Instead of controlling children through excessive discipline, he believes they should be given freedom to **play, explore and learn naturally**.

**Use for:**

* Alternative education
* Headmaster's character
* Child psychology
* Freedom
* Contrast with Albert Mission College

The Headmaster's philosophy is specifically described as rejecting spoon-feeding and excessive discipline.

---

### 13. “Who can see past, present and future as one…”

**Who says it:** **The Headmaster**, referring to the astrologer.

**Meaning:** The Headmaster believes the astrologer can see the entire course of life — past, present and future.

But the irony is that the prediction about his own death **doesn't come true**.

**Use for:**

* Fate
* Astrology
* Irony
* Human inability to predict the future
* East vs West systems of knowledge

The novel deliberately parallels this with the doctor's confidence about Susila's illness.

---

# 🩺 THE DOCTOR — Important Quote

### 14. “It is the one fever which goes strictly by its own rules.”

**Who says it:** **The Doctor**

**Meaning:** The doctor confidently believes that Susila's typhoid will follow a predictable medical pattern.

But Susila dies anyway.

This creates **dramatic irony**.

**Use for:**

* Science
* Fate
* Death
* Human limitations
* Uncertainty

The doctor even describes it as an “absolutely normal course” and “a perfect typhoid run,” making Susila's death even more ironic.
