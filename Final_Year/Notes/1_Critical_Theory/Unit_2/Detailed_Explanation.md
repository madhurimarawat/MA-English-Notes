# Unit II – Detailed Explanation

# 1. An Apology for Poetry – Philip Sidney

## 📚 What is it?

*An Apology for Poetry* (also called *The Defence of Poesy*) is one of the most important works of English literary criticism.

Sidney wrote it during the Renaissance to defend poetry against critics who claimed that poetry was:

* Useless
* Immoral
* A waste of time

Sidney argues that poetry is actually the **highest form of learning and teaching**.

---

## 🎯 Main Idea

Sidney believes that poetry:

### Teaches and Delights

Poetry combines:

* Knowledge (teaching)
* Pleasure (entertainment)

Therefore, people learn moral lessons more effectively through poetry than through philosophy or history.

### Famous Idea

> "Poetry teaches and delights."

---

## 🌟 Why Does Sidney Defend Poetry?

At that time, some Puritans attacked poetry because they thought it encouraged lies and immoral behavior.

Sidney replies:

### 1. Poet Never Lies

A poet does not claim facts.

Instead, he creates imaginative possibilities.

Therefore:

> The poet never affirmeth, therefore never lieth.

This is Sidney's most famous argument.

---

### 2. Poetry Improves Human Beings

Poetry inspires people to become:

* Virtuous
* Brave
* Noble

Unlike philosophy, poetry makes moral lessons attractive.

---

### 3. Poet as Creator

Sidney calls the poet a creator.

The poet creates a better world than the ordinary world.

The poet's imagination can surpass nature itself.

---

## 👤 Sidney's View of the Poet

According to Sidney, a poet is:

* Visionary
* Teacher
* Creator
* Moral guide

The poet stands above historians and philosophers.

---

## 📖 Poetry vs History vs Philosophy

| Philosophy      | History            | Poetry                      |
| --------------- | ------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Teaches ideas   | Gives facts        | Teaches through imagination |
| Difficult       | Limited to reality | Universal                   |
| Less attractive | Less inspiring     | Delightful and effective    |

Sidney concludes:

### Poetry is superior.

---

## 🎭 Types of Poetry Mentioned

Sidney discusses:

* Religious Poetry
* Philosophical Poetry
* Pastoral Poetry
* Tragedy
* Comedy
* Epic Poetry

He especially admires heroic poetry.

---

## 🌟 Significance

### Literary Significance

* First major English defense of poetry.
* Foundation of Renaissance criticism.
* Elevated the status of poets.

### Historical Significance

Reflects Renaissance humanism:

* Faith in education
* Faith in imagination
* Faith in human potential

---

## 📝 Important Quotes

> "The poet never affirmeth, therefore never lieth."

> "Poetry is an art of imitation."

> "To teach and delight."

> "Nature never set forth the earth in so rich tapestry as divers poets have done."

---

## 📌 Examination Point

Remember:

**An Apology for Poetry = Defense of Poetry + Poet as Creator + Teach and Delight**

# 2. An Essay of Dramatic Poesy – John Dryden

## 📚 What is it?

*An Essay of Dramatic Poesy* (1668) is the most important critical work of Dryden.

It is written as a dialogue among four friends discussing:

* Drama
* Theatre
* Ancient vs Modern writers
* French vs English drama

The work attempts to determine what makes drama successful.

---

## 🎯 Main Idea

Dryden argues that drama should:

* Delight audiences
* Represent human life realistically
* Follow artistic principles without becoming rigid

He defends English drama while acknowledging the strengths of classical and French drama.

---

## 👥 Main Speakers (Characters)

Unlike Sidney's essay, this work contains fictional discussants.

### 1. Crites

Represents:

* Ancient/Classical critics

Believes ancient Greek and Roman dramatists are superior.

---

### 2. Eugenius

Represents:

* Modern critics

Argues modern writers have improved upon ancient writers.

---

### 3. Lisideius

Represents:

* French drama

Praises French playwrights for order and discipline.

---

### 4. Neander

Represents Dryden himself.

He supports English drama and presents Dryden's views.

### Significance

Neander is the most important speaker because he voices Dryden's opinions.

---

## ⚔️ Major Debates

### Ancient vs Modern

Crites:
Ancients are perfect.

Eugenius:
Moderns are better because knowledge advances.

Dryden's position:
Respect the ancients but learn from modern improvements.

---

### French vs English Drama

French Drama:

* More orderly
* Follows strict rules

English Drama:

* More natural
* More lively
* Better characterization

Dryden ultimately favors English drama.

---

### Rhyme vs Blank Verse

A major issue discussed is dramatic language.

Dryden initially supports rhyme in heroic drama because it adds elegance and grandeur.

---

## 🎭 View of Drama

Dryden defines drama as:

> "A just and lively image of human nature."

This is one of the most famous definitions in literary criticism.

Drama should show:

* Human passions
* Human actions
* Human conflicts

---

## 🌟 Significance

### Literary Significance

* Landmark of Restoration criticism.
* Established Dryden as England's first great critic.
* Balanced classical rules with creative freedom.

### Historical Significance

Shows the literary debates of the Restoration Age.

---

## 📝 Important Quotes

> "A just and lively image of human nature."

> "Delight is the chief, if not the only, end of poesy."

> "Imitation of nature."

> "The ancients may be revered, but not blindly followed."

(Last statement summarizes Dryden's position.)

---

## 🎯 Themes

* Nature and Art
* Drama and Reality
* Ancient vs Modern
* French vs English Drama
* Entertainment and Instruction

---

## 📌 Examination Point

Remember:

**Essay of Dramatic Poesy = Defense of English Drama + Dialogue Form + Ancient vs Modern + French vs English Debate**
