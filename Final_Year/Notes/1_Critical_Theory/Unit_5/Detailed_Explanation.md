# Unit V – Detailed Explanation

# 1. Feminist Criticism in the Wilderness – Elaine Showalter

## 📚 What is it?

Published in 1981, *Feminist Criticism in the Wilderness* is one of the most important essays in feminist literary criticism.

Showalter argues that feminist criticism should develop its own theories instead of depending entirely on male-centered literary theories.

The essay attempts to answer:

> How should women be studied in literature?

---

## 🎯 Main Idea

Showalter believes feminist criticism has been too dependent on theories created by men.

She argues that women need:

* Their own literary tradition
* Their own critical methods
* Their own theoretical framework

The essay seeks to establish an independent female literary criticism.

---

# 🌟 Two Types of Feminist Criticism

## 1. Feminist Critique

This approach studies:

* Images of women in literature
* Stereotypes about women
* Male representations of women

Questions asked include:

* How are women portrayed?
* Are they oppressed?
* Are they treated fairly?

### Focus

**Woman as Reader**

---

## 2. Gynocriticism

This is Showalter's most famous contribution.

Gynocriticism studies:

* Women writers
* Women's experiences
* Female creativity
* Female literary traditions

### Focus

**Woman as Writer**

---

## 📖 Meaning of Gynocriticism

The term comes from:

* "Gyne" = woman
* "Criticism" = literary study

Thus:

### Gynocriticism = Study of women's literature by women.

Instead of examining women through male theories, it studies women's writing on its own terms.

---

# 🌸 Four Models of Gynocriticism

Showalter proposes four approaches.

## 1. Biological Model

Examines:

* Female body
* Female experiences
* Physical aspects influencing writing

---

## 2. Linguistic Model

Studies:

* Women's language
* Female expression
* Writing style

---

## 3. Psychoanalytic Model

Studies:

* Female psyche
* Female consciousness
* Identity formation

---

## 4. Cultural Model

Most important model.

Studies:

* Women's culture
* Social experiences
* Historical conditions

Showalter prefers this approach because it combines all others.

---

# 👩 Women's Literary Tradition

Showalter argues that women writers have often been ignored by literary history.

She calls for the recovery of neglected women authors and the creation of a female literary tradition.

---

## 🌟 Significance

### Literary Significance

* Established feminist criticism as a major discipline.
* Introduced Gynocriticism.
* Encouraged study of women's writing.

### Historical Significance

Helped create modern feminist literary theory.

---

## 📝 Important Quotes

> "The programme of gynocritics is to construct a female framework for the analysis of women's literature."

> "Woman as reader."

> "Woman as writer."

---

## 📌 Examination Point

Remember:

**Feminist Criticism in the Wilderness = Feminist Critique + Gynocriticism + Female Literary Tradition**

# 2. The Function of Criticism – Northrop Frye

## 📚 What is it?

Northrop Frye's essay *The Function of Criticism* discusses the purpose and nature of literary criticism.

Frye believed criticism should be a systematic and scientific study of literature.

He opposed criticism based only on personal likes and dislikes.

---

## 🎯 Main Idea

Criticism should not merely express opinions such as:

* "I like this poem."
* "I dislike this novel."

Instead, criticism should develop objective principles for understanding literature.

---

# 📖 What is Criticism?

For Frye:

Criticism is an organized study of literature.

It should:

* Analyze literary structures
* Discover patterns
* Identify recurring myths and symbols

---

## 🌟 Criticism as a Science

One of Frye's most important ideas:

### Criticism should be systematic.

Just as science studies nature, criticism should study literature objectively.

The critic should search for:

* Literary patterns
* Genres
* Archetypes
* Myths

---

# ⭐ Archetypes

A central concept in Frye's criticism.

## Meaning

An archetype is a recurring image, symbol, character, or pattern found in literature.

Examples:

* Hero
* Quest
* Journey
* Wise old man
* Heaven and Hell imagery

These patterns appear repeatedly across cultures.

---

## 🌍 Literature as a Unified System

Frye argues that literature is interconnected.

Every literary work belongs to a larger system of stories and myths.

A critic should understand these relationships.

---

## 👤 Role of the Critic

The critic should:

* Explain literature
* Discover literary structures
* Classify literary forms

The critic should not merely judge whether a work is good or bad.

---

## 🌟 Significance

### Literary Significance

* Foundation of archetypal criticism.
* Made criticism more systematic.
* Influenced structuralist criticism.

### Historical Significance

Shifted criticism from personal evaluation to literary analysis.

---

## 📝 Important Quotes

> "Criticism is a structure of thought and knowledge in its own right."

> "The function of criticism is not merely evaluation but understanding."

> "Literature forms an order of words."

(Famous Fryean ideas associated with the essay.)

---

## 📌 Examination Point

Remember:

**The Function of Criticism = Scientific Criticism + Archetypes + Literary Structure**
