# Unit I – Detailed Explanation

# 1. Poetics – Aristotle

## 📚 What is it?

*Poetics* is the earliest and most influential work of literary criticism in Western literature.

Aristotle wrote it around the 4th century BCE to explain:

* What literature is
* How poetry works
* Why tragedy affects audiences
* What makes a good plot and character

The work mainly discusses **tragedy**, though it also briefly mentions epic poetry.

---

## 🎯 Main Idea

Aristotle argues that literature is **mimesis** (imitation of life).

Writers imitate human actions, emotions, and experiences through art.

According to Aristotle:

> "Art imitates life."

The purpose of tragedy is not merely entertainment but to create emotional and moral understanding.

---

## ⭐ Important Concepts

### 1. Mimesis (Imitation)

Literature imitates human life and actions.

Example:
A tragic hero's fall reflects real human struggles.

---

### 2. Catharsis

The most famous idea in *Poetics*.

Catharsis means:

**Purification or cleansing of emotions.**

When audiences watch tragedy, they feel:

* Pity
* Fear

These emotions are released, leaving the audience emotionally purified.

### Famous Quote

> "Through pity and fear effecting the proper catharsis of these emotions."

---

### 3. Tragic Hero

A tragic hero is:

* Noble
* Virtuous
* Not perfect

They possess a flaw called **Hamartia**.

Examples:

* Oedipus
* Hamlet
* Macbeth

---

### 4. Hamartia

Means:

**Error in judgment or tragic flaw.**

This flaw causes the hero's downfall.

---

### 5. Peripeteia

Means:

**Reversal of fortune.**

The hero suddenly moves from success to disaster.

---

### 6. Anagnorisis

Means:

**Recognition or discovery.**

The hero realizes a hidden truth.

Example:

Oedipus discovers he killed his father.

---

## 🎭 Six Elements of Tragedy

Aristotle identifies six parts:

| Rank | Element   | Meaning             |
| ---- | --------- | ------------------- |
| 1    | Plot      | Sequence of events  |
| 2    | Character | People in the story |
| 3    | Thought   | Ideas and themes    |
| 4    | Diction   | Language            |
| 5    | Melody    | Music               |
| 6    | Spectacle | Visual effects      |

### Most Important:

**Plot**

Aristotle says:

> "Plot is the soul of tragedy."

---

## 👤 Character (Appearance & Significance)

Aristotle is not describing fictional characters here.

Instead, he explains how characters should be written.

A good character should be:

* Good
* Appropriate
* Realistic
* Consistent

Character exists to support the plot.

---

## 🌟 Significance of *Poetics*

### Literary Significance

* Foundation of literary criticism.
* Influenced drama for over 2000 years.
* Introduced concepts like catharsis and tragic hero.

### Academic Significance

Almost every modern literary theory begins by responding to Aristotle.

---

## 📝 Important Quotes

> "Plot is the soul of tragedy."

> "Art is imitation."

> "A man does not become a hero until he can see the root of his own downfall."

(Paraphrased Aristotelian idea.)

> "Through pity and fear effecting the proper catharsis of these emotions."

---

## 📌 Examination Point

Remember:

**Poetics = Tragedy + Mimesis + Catharsis + Tragic Hero**

# 2. On the Sublime – Longinus

## 📚 What is it?

*On the Sublime* is a famous work of literary criticism written by Longinus (probably 1st century CE).

While Aristotle asks:

> "What makes literature effective?"

Longinus asks:

> "What makes literature great?"

---

## 🎯 Main Idea

True literature should not merely persuade.

It should elevate the reader's soul.

The highest form of writing creates:

### Sublimity

Meaning:

**Grandeur, magnificence, awe, and spiritual elevation.**

The reader feels transported beyond ordinary experience.

---

## 🌄 Meaning of "Sublime"

The word "sublime" means:

Something so great, powerful, beautiful, or overwhelming that it inspires awe.

Examples:

* Vast oceans
* Mountains
* Thunderstorms
* Great speeches
* Powerful poetry

The sublime produces wonder rather than simple pleasure.

---

## ⭐ Five Sources of the Sublime

Longinus says great writing comes from five sources.

### 1. Grandeur of Thought

A writer must think noble and elevated thoughts.

Great minds create great literature.

---

### 2. Strong Emotion

Powerful feelings create powerful writing.

Genuine passion affects readers.

---

### 3. Figures of Speech

Skillful use of:

* Metaphor
* Hyperbole
* Rhetorical questions

enhances sublimity.

---

### 4. Noble Diction

Choice of majestic and beautiful words.

Language should be elevated.

---

### 5. Dignified Composition

Arrangement of words and sentences should create grandeur.

---

## 🌟 Characteristics of Sublime Writing

Sublime writing:

* Inspires awe
* Elevates the soul
* Creates wonder
* Stays in memory
* Transcends ordinary language

Longinus says great literature strikes like lightning.

### Famous Quote

> "The sublime is the echo of a great soul."

---

## 👤 Character (Appearance & Significance)

Unlike drama, *On the Sublime* has no characters.

It is a critical essay.

The central focus is:

* Great writers
* Great language
* Great emotional power

---

## ⚡ Significance of *On the Sublime*

### Literary Significance

* One of the greatest works of criticism.
* Influenced Romantic writers.
* Shifted attention from rules to imagination and emotion.

### Historical Significance

Longinus emphasized:

* Creativity
* Emotional power
* Individual genius

This later inspired Romantic literature.

---

## 📝 Important Quotes

> "The sublime is the echo of a great soul."

> "Great writing does not persuade; it transports."

> "Sublimity raises us toward greatness."

(Paraphrased Longinian ideas.)

---

## 📌 Examination Point

Remember:

**On the Sublime = Grandeur + Awe + Emotional Elevation + Great Writing**
