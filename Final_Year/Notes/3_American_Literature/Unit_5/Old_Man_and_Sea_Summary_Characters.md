# *The Old Man and the Sea* — Ernest Hemingway

* **Author:** Ernest Hemingway
* **Published:** 1952
* **Genre:** Novella
* **Setting:** Cuba / the Gulf Stream
* **Main Character:** Santiago
* **Central Conflict:** An old fisherman struggles against nature, the sea, the marlin, sharks, and his own physical limitations.

---

# 1. Detailed Summary

## Beginning: Santiago's bad luck

The story begins with **Santiago**, an old Cuban fisherman.

He has gone **84 days without catching a fish**.

People in the village consider him unlucky.

His young friend **Manolin** used to fish with him, but Manolin's parents have forced him to fish with another, more successful fisherman.

However, Manolin still loves and respects Santiago.

He continues to help him by bringing food, fishing equipment and newspapers.

Santiago's body is old and worn:

* his hands are scarred
* his face is wrinkled
* his eyes are described as having the color of the sea
* but his spirit remains strong

Santiago decides to go farther into the sea than usual.

---

# 2. Santiago goes far into the sea

On the **85th day**, Santiago goes out alone.

He sails far into the Gulf Stream.

He sets his fishing lines carefully.

Eventually, a huge **marlin** takes his bait.

The fish is extraordinarily large and powerful.

Santiago immediately recognizes that this is the greatest fish he has ever encountered.

---

# 3. The struggle with the marlin

The marlin pulls Santiago's boat.

For nearly **three days**, Santiago struggles with the fish.

He is physically exhausted.

His hands become:

* cut
* bleeding
* cramped

But he refuses to give up.

He respects the marlin enormously.

He thinks of the fish almost as a **brother**.

This is important because Santiago is not simply trying to destroy his enemy.

He admires its strength and courage.

---

# 4. Santiago's thoughts during the struggle

While fighting the marlin, Santiago thinks about:

* Manolin
* baseball
* Joe DiMaggio
* lions from his youth
* his own physical strength
* his past
* his relationship with nature

He remembers **Joe DiMaggio**, who played baseball despite physical pain.

Santiago uses DiMaggio as an example of endurance.

He tells himself that if DiMaggio can continue playing despite his injury, he too can continue fighting.

---

# 5. Santiago kills the marlin

Eventually, Santiago succeeds.

He kills the marlin with his harpoon.

The fish is so large that it cannot fit inside the boat.

Santiago ties it alongside the boat.

He feels proud because he has proved his ability as a fisherman.

But his victory is not complete.

The journey home becomes another struggle.

---

# 6. The sharks attack

The marlin's blood attracts sharks.

First, a large **mako shark** attacks.

Santiago kills it with his harpoon, but the shark eats part of the marlin.

Later, more sharks arrive.

Santiago fights them with whatever weapons he has:

* harpoon
* knife
* oar
* club

But he cannot prevent them from eating the fish.

One by one, the sharks destroy the marlin.

---

# 7. Santiago returns

Santiago finally reaches shore.

Almost nothing remains of the marlin except its:

**skeleton, head and tail.**

Santiago is physically exhausted.

He carries the mast back to his shack like a man carrying a cross.

He collapses and sleeps.

---

# 8. Manolin returns

Manolin sees Santiago's condition and becomes emotional.

He promises to fish with Santiago again.

He tells Santiago that he still has much to learn from him.

The other fishermen see the enormous skeleton and realize that Santiago has achieved something remarkable.

Even though Santiago lost the flesh of the fish, he has gained **respect, dignity and spiritual victory**.

The story ends with Santiago dreaming again about the **lions of Africa**.

---

# 9. Meaning of the Ending

The ending is deliberately not simply:

**"Santiago lost."**

He lost the physical body of the fish.

But he did not lose his courage.

His real victory is **spiritual rather than material**.

This connects to Hemingway's famous idea:

> **“A man can be destroyed but not defeated.”**

This is one of the central ideas of the entire novella.

---

# 2. Major Themes

## 1. Man vs Nature

This is the most obvious theme.

Santiago struggles against:

* the sea
* the marlin
* sharks
* weather
* hunger
* physical exhaustion

But Hemingway does not present nature as simply evil.

Santiago **respects nature**.

He loves the sea and admires the marlin.

### Meaning

Humans cannot completely control nature.

But they can demonstrate courage and dignity while facing it.

---

# 2. Perseverance and Endurance

Santiago has gone **84 days without catching a fish**, but he refuses to give up.

He continues fishing.

Even after catching the marlin, he must fight sharks.

Even after losing the fish, he returns home.

### Message

Success is not always measured by the final material result.

Sometimes **the act of continuing despite failure is itself a victory**.

---

# 3. Human Dignity

Santiago loses the physical prize but maintains his dignity.

He is poor.

He is old.

He is physically exhausted.

But he refuses to surrender his pride.

His dignity comes from **how he fights**, not what he possesses.

---

# 4. Courage

Santiago demonstrates extraordinary courage.

He knows the marlin is stronger than him, yet he continues fighting.

He also knows the sharks may destroy his catch, but he fights them anyway.

### Important idea

Courage does not mean being unafraid.

It means **continuing despite pain, fear and exhaustion.**

---

# 5. Isolation and Loneliness

Santiago spends much of the story alone.

He has lost his wife.

Manolin no longer fishes with him regularly.

He has little money.

His only companions are:

* the sea
* the fish
* birds
* his memories

However, his relationship with Manolin shows that he is not completely emotionally alone.

---

# 6. Friendship and Love

The relationship between **Santiago and Manolin** is extremely important.

Manolin respects Santiago despite his bad luck.

He:

* brings him food
* helps carry equipment
* worries about him
* listens to him
* wants to fish with him again

Their relationship represents **loyalty, affection and the transmission of knowledge between generations**.

---

# 7. Pride

Santiago is proud of his fishing ability.

His pride motivates him to go farther into the sea.

This pride is both:

### Positive

It gives him courage and determination.

### Dangerous

It may encourage him to push himself beyond his physical limits.

Thus Hemingway presents pride as complicated rather than completely good or bad.

---

# 8. Defeat vs Victory

The novella asks:

**What does it actually mean to win?**

Materially, Santiago loses.

The sharks eat the marlin.

But spiritually, Santiago wins because he proves his courage and endurance.

Therefore:

**Physical defeat ≠ spiritual defeat.**

---

# 9. Respect for the Opponent

Santiago respects the marlin.

He repeatedly thinks of it as a noble creature.

He doesn't hate it.

Instead, he admires its strength.

This creates an unusual relationship:

**Santiago vs marlin**

but also

**Santiago + marlin as fellow creatures struggling for survival.**

---

# 10. Aging

Santiago is an old man, but he refuses to believe that old age has destroyed his abilities.

His body is weak, but his mind and spirit remain powerful.

The story therefore explores the difference between:

**physical weakness**

and

**spiritual strength.**

---

# 3. Major Symbols

## 1. The Sea

The sea is the most important symbol.

Santiago calls the sea **“la mar”**, a feminine term.

He sees the sea as something that can:

* give life
* provide food
* be beautiful
* be dangerous
* be unpredictable

### Representation

**The sea = nature, life, mystery and the struggle of existence.**

---

# 2. The Marlin

The marlin is Santiago's greatest challenge.

It represents:

* strength
* dignity
* courage
* nature
* the ideal opponent
* Santiago's personal challenge

The marlin is not merely a fish.

It becomes a reflection of Santiago himself.

Both are:

* strong
* determined
* solitary
* struggling
* unwilling to surrender

### Representation

**Marlin = noble struggle and the challenge that gives life meaning.**

---

# 3. Sharks

The sharks represent destructive forces that attack success.

They destroy the physical reward Santiago has earned.

They can symbolize:

* destruction
* bad luck
* unavoidable suffering
* forces beyond human control

The sharks also show that achieving something does not mean being able to keep it.

---

# 4. The Lions

Santiago repeatedly dreams of **lions on the beaches of Africa**.

The lions are associated with:

* youth
* strength
* vitality
* freedom
* hope
* courage

When Santiago was young, he saw lions in Africa.

Even as an old man, he dreams about them.

### Meaning

The lions represent the **youth and strength still alive inside Santiago**.

They also symbolize hope.

---

# 5. The Mast

At the end, Santiago carries the mast on his shoulders.

This strongly resembles the image of **Christ carrying the cross**.

### Meaning

The mast can represent:

* suffering
* sacrifice
* endurance
* spiritual struggle
* martyr-like suffering

Santiago becomes a kind of **Christ-like suffering figure**.

---

# 6. Santiago's Hands

His hands are scarred and injured.

They represent:

* hard work
* experience
* suffering
* endurance
* the physical cost of survival

His hands show that his success has come through struggle.

---

# 7. Baseball / Joe DiMaggio

Joe DiMaggio is Santiago's sporting hero.

He represents:

* strength
* endurance
* discipline
* determination
* excellence

Santiago thinks about DiMaggio when he is suffering.

DiMaggio becomes psychological inspiration for him.

---

# 8. The Number 84 / 85

Santiago has gone **84 days without catching a fish**.

He catches the marlin on the **85th day**.

The number 85 therefore represents:

* hope
* perseverance
* a turning point
* renewal

His failure does not continue forever.

---

# 9. The Marlin's Skeleton

At the end, only the skeleton remains.

The skeleton represents the **difference between material loss and spiritual victory**.

The villagers see the enormous skeleton and understand the greatness of Santiago's achievement.

---

# 4. Characters — Appearance, Qualities & Representation

# Santiago

### Appearance

* Very old fisherman
* Thin and lean
* Deep wrinkles
* Scarred hands
* Worn body
* Bright eyes described as resembling the sea

Hemingway emphasizes that although Santiago's body is old, his **eyes remain strong**.

### Qualities

* Courageous
* Patient
* Determined
* Experienced
* Humble
* Proud
* Independent
* Resilient
* Intelligent
* Respectful toward nature
* Spiritually strong

### Representation

Santiago represents:

**The human spirit's ability to endure suffering and defeat without surrendering its dignity.**

He is the central embodiment of:

> **“A man can be destroyed but not defeated.”**

### Important relationship

**Santiago + Marlin**

They are opponents, but Santiago respects the marlin.

**Santiago + Manolin**

They represent love, friendship and the passing of knowledge from one generation to another.

---

# Manolin

### Appearance / Background

* Young boy
* Santiago's former fishing companion
* Comes from a poor fishing community
* His parents have made him fish with another fisherman because Santiago is considered unlucky.

### Qualities

* Loyal
* Loving
* Respectful
* Compassionate
* Helpful
* Devoted
* Hopeful
* Admiring of Santiago

### Representation

Manolin represents:

**Youth, hope, loyalty and continuity.**

He ensures that Santiago's knowledge and values will survive.

### Important point

Although other people consider Santiago unlucky, Manolin continues to believe in him.

---

# The Marlin

The marlin is not a human character, but it functions almost like one.

### Qualities

* Powerful
* Noble
* Determined
* Patient
* Strong
* Enduring

### Representation

The marlin represents:

**Santiago's ideal opponent and a reflection of his own courage.**

Santiago respects the fish because he sees its struggle as similar to his own.

---

# The Sharks

### Qualities

* Aggressive
* Destructive
* Opportunistic
* Relentless

### Representation

The sharks represent:

**destruction, unavoidable suffering and forces that consume human achievements.**

---

# Perico

### Role

Perico is associated with the local community and provides Santiago with newspapers, particularly information about baseball.

### Representation

He helps connect Santiago to the **outside world**, especially his interest in baseball.

---

# Martin

### Role

Martin is the owner of a local café/restaurant who helps Santiago by giving him food.

### Qualities

* Generous
* Kind
* Supportive

### Representation

Martin represents the **community's quiet kindness** toward Santiago.

---

# Santiago's Wife

She is already dead before the main events.

### Representation

Her absence emphasizes:

* Santiago's loneliness
* aging
* loss
* his isolated life

He keeps a photograph of her but eventually turns it face down because it makes him feel lonely.

---

# Joe DiMaggio

He is a real-life baseball player mentioned throughout the novella.

### Representation

He represents:

* physical excellence
* perseverance
* courage
* Santiago's ideal of masculinity and endurance

Santiago uses DiMaggio's ability to play despite pain as motivation during his own struggle.

# 6. Important Quotes — Who Said It + Explanation

## ⭐ 1. “But man is not made for defeat.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

Santiago refuses to accept defeat as the final definition of a human being.

A person may lose physically, but the human spirit can remain undefeated.

### Theme

**Perseverance + human dignity**

---

# ⭐ 2. “A man can be destroyed but not defeated.”

**Speaker:** Santiago / narrator presenting Santiago's thought

### Meaning

This is probably the **most important quotation in the entire novella**.

Santiago may be physically destroyed:

* his hands are injured
* he is exhausted
* his fish is eaten

But his courage remains.

### Theme

**Endurance, courage and spiritual victory**

### Exam use

Use this quote whenever discussing:

* Santiago
* the ending
* Hemingway's philosophy
* victory vs defeat
* human dignity

---

# ⭐ 3. “Everything about him was old except his eyes and they were the same color as the sea and were cheerful and undefeated.”

**Narrator describing Santiago**

### Meaning

Santiago's body is old, but his eyes show that his spirit remains young and strong.

The word **“undefeated”** is extremely important.

### Symbolism

His eyes being the color of the sea connects Santiago spiritually with nature.

---

# ⭐ 4. “He was a very old man who fished alone in a skiff in the Gulf Stream.”

**Narrator**

### Meaning

This introduces Santiago as an isolated old fisherman.

The word **“alone”** immediately establishes one of the novella's major themes:

**loneliness and isolation.**

---

# ⭐ 5. “He is a great fish and I must convince him that I am a great fisherman.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

Santiago sees the marlin as worthy of respect.

He does not simply want to kill it.

He wants to prove himself worthy of fighting such a powerful creature.

### Theme

**Pride, courage and respect for nature.**

---

# ⭐ 6. “Fish, I love you and respect you very much.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

This is a fascinating statement because Santiago is trying to kill the fish, yet he loves and respects it.

It shows Hemingway's idea that humans and nature are connected.

### Theme

**Man vs nature + respect for life**

---

# ⭐ 7. “Now is no time to think of baseball.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

Santiago knows that he needs to concentrate completely on his struggle.

But baseball remains an important source of psychological strength throughout the story.

### Theme

**Determination and concentration**

---

# ⭐ 8. “I may not be as strong as I think, but I know many tricks and I have resolution.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

Santiago knows that he is physically old.

But he compensates with:

* experience
* intelligence
* technique
* determination

### Theme

**Experience vs physical strength**

---

# ⭐ 9. “Why do old men wake so early? Is it to have one longer day?”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

This reflects Santiago's awareness of his old age and limited time.

It also shows his desire to make every remaining day meaningful.

### Theme

**Aging and mortality**

---

# ⭐ 10. “He was a fish to kill and he was a fish to eat.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

Santiago understands the practical reality of fishing.

The marlin is both:

* a living creature he respects
* a source of food and survival

This captures the complicated relationship between humans and nature.

---

# ⭐ 11. “The fish is my friend too.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

The marlin becomes Santiago's companion during his isolation.

It emphasizes the strange emotional bond between hunter and hunted.

### Theme

**Friendship, nature and solitude**

---

# ⭐ 12. “Pain does not matter to a man.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

Santiago believes that suffering should not stop a person from doing what must be done.

It reflects his extremely strong attitude toward endurance.

### Theme

**Courage and perseverance**

---

# ⭐ 13. “Every day above earth is a good day.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

Despite his poverty, loneliness and suffering, Santiago values life.

The quotation expresses **hope and gratitude**.

---

# ⭐ 14. “I wish I had the boy.”

**Speaker:** Santiago

### Meaning

During his struggle, Santiago repeatedly wishes Manolin were with him.

This reveals how much Santiago depends emotionally on the boy.

It also shows that beneath his independent exterior, Santiago needs human companionship.

### Theme

**Love, friendship and loneliness**
