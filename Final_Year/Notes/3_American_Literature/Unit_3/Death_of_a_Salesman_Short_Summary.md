*Death of a Salesman* by Arthur Miller is the story of Willy Loman, an ageing salesman who has spent his life believing that success comes from being popular, attractive, and financially successful. He dreams of a better future for his two sons, Biff and Happy, especially Biff, whom he believes is destined for greatness. However, Biff has failed to achieve the success Willy expects, and Willy himself is struggling with his career, money, and declining confidence. Unable to accept reality, Willy often escapes into memories of the past and his dreams of success.

As the play progresses, Biff realizes that his father's idea of success is false and that they have both been living in illusions. He tries to make Willy accept the truth and live honestly. Willy, however, cannot accept his failures and decides that his death will provide his family with life-insurance money. He deliberately causes a car accident and dies. At his funeral, very few people attend, showing that Willy's belief that being well-liked would bring success was an illusion. The play therefore presents Willy's tragedy as a criticism of the false promises of the American Dream and the pressure to measure human worth through money and success.
