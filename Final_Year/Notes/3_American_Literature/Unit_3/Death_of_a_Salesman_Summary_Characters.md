# *Death of a Salesman* — Arthur Miller

* **Author:** Arthur Miller
* **First Performed:** 1949
* **Genre:** Tragedy / Modern Tragedy / Social Drama
* **Setting:** Brooklyn, New York, and Willy Loman’s memories/flashbacks
* **Main Character:** Willy Loman, an aging salesman
* **Central Conflict:** Willy’s false idea of the American Dream clashes with economic reality and his family’s actual abilities.

---

# 1. Detailed Summary

## Act I

The play begins with **Willy Loman**, a 63-year-old traveling salesman, returning home exhausted from a sales trip.

Willy is worried because he is getting older, his sales are declining, and he can no longer travel as successfully as before. His wife, **Linda Loman**, lovingly supports him.

Willy has two sons:

* **Biff Loman** — 34, formerly a high-school football star
* **Happy Loman** — 32, successful-looking but emotionally empty

Willy constantly remembers Biff's high-school years. Biff was extremely popular and talented, and Willy believed that being **well-liked** would guarantee success.

Willy's philosophy is essentially:

> If you are attractive, confident and well-liked, you will succeed.

He therefore does not encourage Biff to work hard academically. Instead, he celebrates Biff's popularity and athletic ability.

### Biff's failure

As an adult, Biff has not achieved the success Willy imagined.

Biff has moved from job to job and feels lost. He realizes that he does not actually want the business career his father dreamed about for him.

Willy cannot accept this reality.

He continues to believe that Biff is destined for greatness.

---

## Willy's memories

The present constantly mixes with Willy's memories.

One important memory is of **Ben**, Willy's older brother.

Ben became rich after going into the African jungle and discovering diamonds.

Willy admires Ben enormously because Ben represents the kind of success Willy wants.

Ben repeatedly symbolizes the possibility of becoming rich through boldness and luck.

---

# 2. The Biff–Willy relationship

Willy has enormous expectations for Biff.

When Biff was young, Willy believed:

**Biff + popularity + confidence = success.**

But Biff's life did not turn out that way.

Biff eventually realizes that his father's philosophy is false.

He wants to live honestly and independently rather than pretending to be a successful businessman.

This creates the central conflict between father and son.

### Biff's realization

Biff finally understands that:

* he is not a businessman
* he does not want Willy's dream
* he has been living according to someone else's expectations
* his father has been living in a fantasy

---

# 3. The Boston revelation

One of the most important events in the play happens in Willy's memories.

When Biff was young, he went to Boston to visit Willy.

There, Biff discovered that Willy was having an affair with **The Woman**.

Willy had bought The Woman stockings, while Linda was at home carefully repairing old stockings because the family could not afford new ones.

This discovery destroys Biff's idealized image of his father.

Before Boston, Biff sees Willy as almost heroic.

After Boston, Biff loses respect for him.

This helps explain why Biff's life begins to fall apart.

---

# 4. The restaurant scene

Later, Willy meets Biff and Happy at a restaurant.

Biff tries to tell Willy the truth: he is not going to become the great businessman Willy imagines.

But Willy refuses to listen.

Happy is more concerned with women and superficial pleasures than with helping his father.

Biff becomes increasingly frustrated and tells Willy that they must stop pretending.

Willy escapes into his memories.

---

# 5. The final confrontation

Near the end, Biff finally confronts Willy emotionally.

He tells his father that they are ordinary people and that they should stop pretending to be something they are not.

Biff wants freedom from Willy's expectations.

Willy interprets Biff's emotional reaction as proof that Biff loves him and believes Biff will become successful.

Willy still cannot understand the truth.

---

# 6. Willy's suicide

Willy decides that his death can benefit Biff.

He believes that if he dies, his family will receive **life insurance money**, and Biff will finally have the financial opportunity to become successful.

This is a tragic final expression of Willy's distorted version of the American Dream.

He dies in a car crash.

---

# 7. The Requiem

The final section is called **"Requiem."**

At Willy's funeral, very few people attend.

This is deeply ironic.

Willy believed he was extremely popular and had many friends.

But almost nobody comes to his funeral.

Linda is devastated.

She says:

> **"We're free."**

This is one of the most important lines in the play.

The house has finally been paid off, but Willy is dead.

His dream of financial security arrives only after his death.

Biff understands that Willy was chasing the wrong dream.

Happy, however, refuses to learn the lesson and decides to continue pursuing Willy's dream.

---

# Major Themes

## 1. The American Dream

This is the **central theme**.

Willy believes that success comes from:

* popularity
* physical attractiveness
* personality
* connections
* being "well-liked"

He does **not** believe strongly enough in:

* hard work
* self-knowledge
* honesty
* realistic goals

Miller criticizes a society where people are valued mainly according to their **economic success**.

### Willy's tragedy

Willy doesn't simply fail economically.

His deeper tragedy is that **he cannot distinguish between reality and his dream of success**.

---

# 2. Reality vs Illusion

Willy constantly escapes into memories and fantasies.

He remembers:

* Biff's youth
* Ben's success
* his affair
* old conversations
* imagined conversations with successful people

The boundaries between past and present become blurred.

### Meaning

Willy uses illusion because reality is painful.

Reality tells him:

**"You are an aging salesman whose career is failing."**

His fantasy tells him:

**"You are important and Biff will become successful."**

He chooses the second version.

---

# 3. Father-Son Conflict

The relationship between **Willy and Biff** is central.

Willy wants Biff to become what **Willy wants him to be**.

Biff wants to discover who **he actually is**.

Therefore:

**Willy = expectation**

**Biff = self-discovery**

Biff's eventual rejection of Willy's dream is actually a movement toward honesty.

---

# 4. Identity and Self-Deception

Willy does not know who he really is.

He wants to be:

* successful
* admired
* wealthy
* remembered

But he isn't satisfied with being an ordinary man.

His inability to accept himself causes enormous psychological suffering.

Biff eventually realizes that accepting oneself is more important than pretending to be successful.

---

# 5. Materialism

The Lomans constantly discuss:

* money
* cars
* houses
* refrigerators
* appliances
* insurance
* jobs
* business

Material possessions become measures of success.

The play asks:

**Can money actually provide happiness and human dignity?**

Miller's answer is largely **no**.

---

# 6. Masculinity

Willy's ideas about being a successful man are strongly connected to traditional masculinity.

He believes a successful man should be:

* financially successful
* admired by other men
* attractive to women
* independent
* powerful

Biff's failure therefore feels to Willy like a failure of manhood.

---

# 7. Betrayal

Betrayal appears in several forms.

### Willy betrays Linda

His affair with The Woman is a betrayal of his marriage.

### Biff feels betrayed by Willy

Biff discovers that Willy is not the perfect father he imagined.

### Willy feels betrayed by Biff

Willy believes Biff has failed to fulfill his potential.

This creates emotional distance between them.

---

# 8. Aging and Death

Willy is afraid of becoming irrelevant.

He feels that society has no use for an old salesman.

Death becomes connected with:

* failure
* escape
* financial security
* recognition
* sacrifice

Ironically, Willy believes death will make him valuable to his family.

---

# 9. Nature

Nature represents a healthier and more authentic life.

Biff loves working outdoors and dreams of working with his hands.

Willy, meanwhile, is trapped in a city environment filled with:

* buildings
* business
* competition
* financial pressure

Nature therefore contrasts with the artificial world of Willy's business dream.

---

# Important Symbols

## 1. Seeds

Willy plants seeds near the end of the play.

### Symbolism

Seeds represent:

* growth
* hope
* legacy
* Willy's desire to leave something behind

Willy wants to know that his life has produced something meaningful.

But his seeds struggle to grow.

This reflects his own life.

He has worked for years, yet he feels he has produced nothing lasting.

---

# 2. The Stockings

Stockings symbolize:

* poverty
* guilt
* betrayal
* Willy's affair

Linda repairs old stockings because money is tight.

Willy gives new stockings to The Woman.

Therefore, whenever Willy sees Linda repairing stockings, he remembers his betrayal.

---

# 3. The Rubber Hose

The rubber hose suggests Willy's suicidal intentions.

Linda discovers it and realizes that Willy may be planning to kill himself.

### Symbolism

It represents:

* despair
* depression
* death
* Willy's inability to cope with reality

---

# 4. Willy's Car

The car represents:

* Willy's job
* mobility
* economic independence
* his eventual death

Willy travels constantly as a salesman.

But the same car eventually becomes the instrument of his suicide.

Therefore, the object that represents his working life also causes his death.

---

# 5. The House

The Loman house represents:

* family
* security
* the American Dream
* confinement

Willy has spent years paying for the house.

Ironically, once the mortgage is finally paid, Willy is dead.

---

# 6. Ben's Diamonds

Diamonds represent:

* wealth
* success
* luck
* the dream of sudden prosperity

Ben's success fascinates Willy.

Willy sees Ben as proof that a man can become rich quickly.

But Ben's success is exceptional and unrealistic for Willy.

---

# 7. Flute / Music

The flute music is associated with:

* Willy's father
* nature
* memory
* an older, simpler life

It also creates a contrast with the modern commercial world Willy inhabits.

---

# 8. The Jungle

The jungle represents the unknown world where wealth and opportunity seem possible.

Ben says:

> **"The jungle is dark but full of diamonds, Willy."**

The jungle therefore symbolizes:

* danger
* opportunity
* wealth
* uncertainty

---

# Important Quotes — Speaker + Explanation

## 1. "Attention, attention must be finally paid to such a person."

**Speaker:** Linda

**Meaning:** Linda argues that ordinary people deserve dignity and respect.

This is one of the most important statements of Miller's social message.

Willy may not be rich or successful, but his suffering matters.

---

## 2. "Be liked and you will never want."

**Speaker:** Willy

**Meaning:**

Willy believes popularity guarantees success.

This represents his mistaken philosophy.

Miller exposes the weakness of a society where personal charm is valued more than genuine ability.

---

## 3. "The man who makes an appearance in the business world, the man who creates personal interest, is the man who gets ahead"

**Speaker:** Willy Loman

**Meaning:** This line reflects the theme of self-deception and the importance of personal interest in achieving success in business. Willy Loman, the character, believes that being liked and creating a memorable impression are more crucial than actual ability or knowledge, which ultimately leads to his downfall.

The theme of self-deception is illustrated in Willy’s advice to his sons that the person who is well liked is the one to success best in business.

---

## 4. **"I'm the New England man. I'm vital in New England."**

**Speaker:** Willy Loman

**Meaning:** Willy takes pride in his identity as a travelling salesman and believes that his importance comes from his business connections.

The quote shows Willy's **desire to feel important and successful**, even though his actual professional situation is declining.

---

## 5. **"The jungle is dark but full of diamonds, Willy."**

**Speaker:** Ben Loman

**Meaning:** Ben describes the world as dangerous and uncertain, but full of opportunities for wealth and success.

The **jungle** represents the harsh world, while the **diamonds** represent wealth and success. It also reflects Willy's obsession with achieving financial success.

---

## 6. **"I'm gonna get a job. I don't want your thirty dollars a week!"**

**Speaker:** Biff Loman

**Meaning:** Biff rejects the idea of depending on Happy or living according to Willy's definition of success.

He wants to become **independent and honest about who he really is**, rather than pretending to be successful.

---

## 7. **"I am not a leader of men, Willy, and neither are you."**

**Speaker:** Biff Loman

**Meaning:** Biff finally confronts the truth about himself and his father.

He recognizes that neither of them is the great, successful businessman Willy imagined them to be. This represents **Biff's rejection of Willy's false dreams**.

---

## 8. **"We never told the truth for ten minutes in this house!"**

**Speaker:** Biff Loman

**Meaning:** Biff realizes that the Loman family has been living through **lies, illusions, and false hopes**.

This is a major turning point because Biff wants to face reality instead of continuing Willy's fantasy.

---

## 9. **"We’re free."**

**Speaker:** Biff Loman

**Meaning:** Biff says this after Willy's death.

He believes that Willy is finally free from the pressures and failures that troubled him throughout his life.

The line is **deeply ironic** because Willy has died believing that his suicide will provide financial security for his family, while Biff understands that true freedom comes from **accepting reality rather than chasing false dreams**.

---

## 10. **"Nothing's planted. I don't have a thing in the ground."**

**Speaker:** Willy Loman

**Meaning:** Willy realizes that he has left behind no meaningful achievement or lasting legacy.

The imagery of **planting** represents creating something that will grow and survive. Willy's desire to plant seeds reflects his desperate wish to leave something behind.

---

## 11. **"After all the highways, and the trains, and the appointments, and the years, you end up worth more dead than alive."**

**Speaker:** Willy Loman

**Meaning:** Willy realizes that his life insurance will provide more money to his family after his death than he could earn while alive.

The quote exposes the **cruel materialism** at the heart of Willy's idea of success.

---

## 12. **"Biff is a wonderful man. He’s got spirit, personality..."**

**Speaker:** Willy Loman

**Meaning:** Willy continues to idealize Biff and believes that his son's personal charm will eventually make him successful.

It shows Willy's inability to accept **Biff's real personality and ambitions**.

---

## 13. **"You can't just come to see me, because I love him."**

**Speaker:** Linda Loman

**Meaning:** Linda tells Biff that Willy is still his father and deserves his love and respect.

It demonstrates Linda's **loyalty, compassion, and understanding** toward Willy despite his many flaws.

---

## 14. **"I can't cry. I can't cry. I don't know what it is, but I can't cry."**

**Speaker:** Biff Loman

**Meaning:** Biff struggles to express his emotions after Willy's death.

His inability to cry suggests that he has already emotionally separated himself from Willy's false dreams and understands that Willy's death was the tragic result of those illusions.

---

## 15. **"The man knew what he wanted and went out and got it!"**

**Speaker:** Willy Loman, speaking about Ben

**Meaning:** Willy admires his brother Ben because Ben achieved wealth and success through adventure and risk-taking.

Ben becomes a **symbol of the success Willy desperately wants**, even though Willy's admiration for him is based largely on an idealized image.

---

# Characters in *Death of a Salesman*

## 1. Willy Loman

### Appearance / Background

* **Age:** 63
* An aging **traveling salesman**
* Husband of Linda
* Father of Biff and Happy
* Lives in Brooklyn, New York.
* Physically and mentally exhausted from years of traveling.
* His name, **“Loman,”** ironically suggests an ordinary or “low man.”

### Qualities

* Ambitious
* Loving but misguided father
* Proud
* Insecure
* Self-deceiving
* Materialistic
* Obsessed with popularity
* Emotionally unstable
* Stubborn
* Unable to accept reality
* Deeply concerned about his sons' success

### What does Willy represent?

**Willy represents the tragic failure of the American Dream.**

He believes that success comes from being:

> **“well liked”**

rather than from hard work, ability, or self-knowledge.

He represents ordinary working people who are pressured to measure their worth through **money, status and professional success**.

### Relationship with Biff

Willy sees Biff as his great hope.

He believes Biff will become rich and successful.

But Biff wants to live honestly and independently.

This creates the main father-son conflict.

### Relationship with Linda

Linda loves and protects Willy.

She understands that he is suffering, but she sometimes protects his illusions rather than confronting him with reality.

### Tragic flaw

Willy's biggest flaw is **his inability to accept reality**.

He keeps believing that his dream will eventually come true.

### Final significance

Willy kills himself believing that his insurance money will help Biff become successful.

His death demonstrates how completely he has misunderstood the meaning of success.

---

# 2. Linda Loman

### Appearance / Background

* Willy's wife
* Mother of Biff and Happy
* A middle-aged/older woman devoted to her family
* Lives with Willy in Brooklyn.

### Qualities

* Loving
* Patient
* Loyal
* Compassionate
* Protective
* Practical
* Emotionally strong
* Sometimes overly submissive toward Willy

### What does Linda represent?

Linda represents:

**Love, loyalty, family devotion and emotional support.**

She is one of the few people who genuinely values Willy as a human being rather than judging him by his financial success.

### Important point

Linda understands that Willy is suffering.

She tells her sons:

> **“Attention, attention must be finally paid to such a person.”**

She believes Willy deserves dignity even though he has failed financially.

### Relationship with Willy

She supports Willy almost unconditionally.

However, her protection can also contribute to his self-deception because she rarely challenges his fantasies directly.

### At the end

After Willy's funeral, Linda says:

> **“We’re free.”**

This is extremely ironic.

The house is finally paid off, but Willy is dead.

### Representation

**Linda = unconditional love and family loyalty.**

---

# 3. Biff Loman

### Appearance / Background

* **Age:** 34
* Willy's elder son
* Former high-school football star
* Handsome and physically strong
* Has moved between different jobs.
* He does not have a stable career.

### Qualities

* Strong
* Attractive
* Independent
* Honest underneath his confusion
* Frustrated
* Rebellious
* Emotional
* Self-aware
* Disillusioned
* Wants freedom

### What does Biff represent?

Biff represents:

**Self-discovery and rejection of false dreams.**

He is arguably the character who comes closest to understanding the truth.

He realizes that he does not want to become a businessman simply because his father wants it.

### Biff's major conflict

Biff wants:

**Reality**

Willy wants:

**The Dream**

This is the central conflict between them.

### Boston incident

Biff discovers Willy's affair with **The Woman** in Boston.

This destroys Biff's idealized image of his father.

After this, Biff's relationship with Willy changes dramatically.

### Biff's realization

Biff eventually accepts that he is not destined to become a great businessman.

He wants to work outdoors and live according to his own nature.

### Representation

**Biff = truth, self-knowledge, freedom and rejection of materialistic success.**

---

# 4. Happy Loman

### Appearance / Background

* **Age:** 32
* Willy's younger son
* Works in business
* Lives independently.
* Has material success but lacks emotional fulfillment.

### Qualities

* Confident
* Womanizer
* Competitive
* Materialistic
* Superficial
* Insecure
* Attention-seeking
* Self-centered
* Ambitious

### What does Happy represent?

Happy represents:

**A person who has accepted Willy's false American Dream without questioning it.**

He appears successful, but internally he is empty.

### Relationship with Biff

Happy admires Biff and wants his approval.

But he also competes with him.

Their relationship shows how Willy's ideas about success create competition between brothers.

### Relationship with women

Happy frequently boasts about women.

This demonstrates his desire to prove his masculinity and importance.

### At the end

Happy refuses to learn from Willy's death.

He says he will continue Willy's dream.

This contrasts sharply with Biff.

### Representation

**Happy = materialism, superficial success and continuation of Willy's false dream.**

---

# 5. Ben Loman

### Appearance / Background

* Willy's older brother
* Became wealthy after entering the diamond business in Africa.
* He died before the main action of the play.
* Appears mainly in Willy's memories and hallucinations.

### Qualities

* Adventurous
* Confident
* Wealthy
* Opportunistic
* Mysterious
* Successful
* Risk-taking

### What does Ben represent?

Ben represents:

**The fantasy of sudden wealth and success.**

He is everything Willy wishes he could be.

Willy repeatedly looks to Ben for confirmation of his philosophy.

### Famous quote

> **“When I walked into the jungle, I was seventeen. When I walked out I was twenty-one. And, by God, I was rich!”**

This represents Willy's fascination with **quick success**.

### Representation

**Ben = wealth, adventure, luck and the seductive fantasy of the American Dream.**

---

# 6. Charley

### Appearance / Background

* Willy's neighbor
* Successful businessman
* Father of Bernard
* A practical and financially successful man
* Regularly helps Willy with money.

### Qualities

* Practical
* Kind
* Patient
* Generous
* Rational
* Successful
* Humble

### What does Charley represent?

Charley represents a **more realistic version of success**.

Unlike Willy, Charley doesn't constantly boast about being successful.

He quietly works and achieves financial stability.

### Relationship with Willy

Willy is jealous of Charley because Charley has achieved the success Willy wants.

Yet Charley continues to help him.

### Important contrast

**Willy:** talks about success.

**Charley:** actually achieves it.

### Representation

**Charley = practical success, generosity and realism.**

---

# 7. Bernard

### Appearance / Background

* Charley's son
* Biff's former schoolmate
* Was academically serious as a young man.
* Later becomes a successful lawyer.

### Qualities

* Intelligent
* Hard-working
* Quiet
* Responsible
* Serious
* Successful
* Modest

### What does Bernard represent?

Bernard represents:

**Hard work, education, discipline and genuine achievement.**

When young, Willy considers Bernard inferior to Biff because Bernard isn't as popular.

But as adults, Bernard becomes successful while Biff struggles.

This proves Willy's philosophy was wrong.

### Important contrast

| Biff                | Bernard                 |
| ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Popular             | Studious                |
| Athletic            | Academic                |
| Loved by Willy      | Underestimated by Willy |
| Struggles as adult  | Becomes successful      |
| Represents charisma | Represents hard work    |

### Representation

**Bernard = genuine achievement through effort and education.**

---

# 8. The Woman

### Appearance / Background

* A woman Willy meets through his work.
* Willy has an affair with her.
* She appears in Willy's memories.

### Qualities

* Flirtatious
* Materialistic
* Wants attention
* Uses her relationship with Willy partly for business/social benefits

### What does she represent?

She represents:

* Willy's betrayal of Linda
* His desire for recognition
* His sexual/male ego
* The emptiness behind his public image

### Important symbolism

Willy gives her **stockings**.

At the same time, Linda is repairing old stockings at home.

This creates a powerful symbol of **guilt and betrayal**.

### Representation

**The Woman = infidelity, guilt and Willy's hidden life.**

---

# 9. Howard Wagner

### Appearance / Background

* Willy's employer
* Son of Willy's former boss, Frank Wagner.
* Owns/runs the company.

### Qualities

* Business-minded
* Self-interested
* Detached
* Powerful
* Unsympathetic toward Willy

### What does he represent?

Howard represents the **impersonal capitalist business system**.

Willy has spent decades working for the company, but Howard ultimately fires him when he is no longer useful.

### Important point

Willy believes his long service should earn him respect.

Howard sees him primarily as an employee who is no longer productive.

### Representation

**Howard = capitalism, corporate indifference and disposability of workers.**

---

# 10. Uncle Frank / Willy's Father

Willy's father is mostly absent from his life and is remembered as a man who made and sold flutes.

### What does he represent?

Willy's father represents:

* the lost past
* craftsmanship
* nature
* a simpler economic life
* Willy's childhood
* absence of parental guidance

The flute music associated with him gives the play a nostalgic atmosphere.

### Representation

**Willy's father = the lost past and an older, simpler world.**

---

# 11. Jenny

### Appearance / Role

* Charley's secretary.
* Has only a small role.

### Function

She helps establish Charley's professional environment and reinforces the contrast between Charley's orderly working life and Willy's increasingly chaotic existence.

---

# 12. Stanley

### Appearance / Role

* Waiter at the restaurant where Willy meets Biff and Happy.

### Function

His role is small, but the restaurant scene becomes important because Willy's family relationships collapse there.

---

# 13. Miss Forsythe

### Appearance / Role

* Woman Happy meets at the restaurant.

### Qualities

* Attractive
* Social
* Flirtatious

### Representation

She contributes to the portrayal of Happy's **superficial lifestyle and sexual competitiveness**.

---

# 14. Letta

### Appearance / Role

* Friend of Miss Forsythe
* Meets Happy at the restaurant.

### Representation

Like Miss Forsythe, she helps demonstrate Happy's obsession with women, status and appearances.
