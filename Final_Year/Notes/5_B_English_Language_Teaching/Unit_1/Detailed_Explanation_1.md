# 1️⃣ WHAT LANGUAGE TEACHING IS ABOUT

## 🗣️ What is language teaching?

**Language teaching** means the organized process of helping someone learn to **understand, use, produce and communicate** in a language.

It is not simply:

> ❌ teaching grammar rules
> ❌ making students memorize vocabulary
> ❌ translating sentences

It is much broader.

A good language teacher tries to develop the learner's ability to **actually use the language**.

### Example 👇

Suppose I teach you:

> “I am going to the market.”

If you only memorize this sentence, you haven't necessarily learned English.

But if you can use the pattern:

> I am going to college.
> I am going to Delhi.
> I am going to the library.
> I am going to the doctor.

then you are beginning to **use the language system**.

---

# 🎯 Main aims of language teaching

| Aim                  | Simple meaning                                     |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 🗣️ Speaking           | Communicate orally                                 |
| 👂 Listening          | Understand spoken language                         |
| 📖 Reading            | Understand written texts                           |
| ✍️ Writing            | Express ideas in writing                           |
| 🧠 Grammar            | Understand how sentences are formed                |
| 📚 Vocabulary         | Learn words and expressions                        |
| 🔊 Pronunciation      | Produce understandable sounds                      |
| 💬 Communication      | Use language appropriately                         |
| 🌎 Cultural awareness | Understand language in its social/cultural context |

The four traditional major skills are:

### **Listening + Speaking + Reading + Writing**

These are often divided into:

**Receptive skills** 👂📖
→ Listening + Reading

**Productive skills** 🗣️✍️
→ Speaking + Writing

# 🧩 Language teaching has several components

## 1. Language

The teacher needs to understand what is being taught:

* sounds
* words
* grammar
* meaning
* sentence structure
* discourse
* vocabulary
* pronunciation

This is where **linguistics** becomes important.

---

## 2. Learner 👨‍🎓

Every learner is different.

Learners differ in:

* age
* motivation
* intelligence/cognitive abilities
* personality
* previous language knowledge
* learning style
* cultural background
* exposure to the language
* opportunities to use the language

Therefore:

> **There is no single teaching method that is perfect for every learner.**

---

## 3. Teacher 👩‍🏫

A teacher is not merely someone who gives information.

A teacher can act as:

* instructor
* facilitator
* guide
* motivator
* evaluator
* organizer
* language model

---

## 4. Learning environment 🏫

Language learning can happen:

* in classrooms
* at home
* through books
* through conversation
* through media
* online
* through interaction with native/non-native speakers
* through immersion

Modern research emphasizes that the **environment and nature of input** matter greatly in L2 learning. 

---

# ⭐ Most important idea

## Language teaching ≠ grammar teaching

Grammar is **part** of language teaching.

But language teaching includes:

> Grammar + vocabulary + pronunciation + four skills + communication + meaning + social/cultural use.

### Example

Knowing:

> “Could you open the window?”

is grammar knowledge.

Knowing **when and why** to say it is communicative competence.

# 2️⃣ DISTINCTION BETWEEN L1 AND L2

This is **VERY IMPORTANT for exams.** ⭐⭐⭐

## What is L1?

**L1 = First Language**

It is generally the language a person acquires first, especially in early childhood.

Example:

A child grows up in a Hindi-speaking family.

👶 → Hindi

Hindi may therefore be the child's **L1**.

---

# What is L2?

**L2 = Second Language**

L2 means a language learned/acquired **after the first language**.

For example:

Hindi → L1
English → L2

But there is an important point:

### ⚠️ “Second” does not necessarily mean literally the second language learned.

In language studies, **L2 is often used broadly for an additional language**, even when the learner knows more than one language already.

So:

Hindi → L1
English → L2
French → L3

But researchers may still use **L2** broadly to refer to additional-language learning.

---

# 🆚 L1 vs L2

| L1                                              | L2                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First language                                  | Additional/second language                                     |
| Usually begins in early childhood               | May begin at any age                                           |
| Usually learned naturally                       | May be learned naturally or formally                           |
| Usually extensive exposure                      | Exposure may be limited                                        |
| No formal instruction is normally necessary     | Classroom instruction is often involved                        |
| Learner usually has no previous language system | Learner already knows another language                         |
| Family/community often provides input           | School, media, society or other environments may provide input |

---

# 🧠 The BIG difference: L2 learner already knows a language

Imagine two children.

### 👶 Child A — L1

A baby learning Hindi:

> No previous language system.

### 👩 Student B — L2

A Hindi speaker learning English:

> Already knows Hindi.

Therefore:

**L2 learner starts with existing linguistic knowledge.**

That existing knowledge can influence L2 learning.

This is called:

# 🔄 LANGUAGE TRANSFER / CROSS-LINGUISTIC INFLUENCE

---

# Example of L1 influence

Suppose Hindi is someone's L1 and English is L2.

Hindi and English have different sound systems and sentence structures.

The learner may pronounce an English sound according to the patterns of Hindi.

This is an example of **L1 influence on L2**.

Modern SLA research recognizes that learners develop an evolving linguistic system and that their existing language can influence the new language. 

---

# 🌟 Important: L1 influence isn't always bad

This is a common misunderstanding.

L1 can sometimes:

### ❌ Cause difficulty

when the two languages differ.

But L1 can also:

### ✅ Help learning

when the languages have similarities.

This is sometimes described as:

**Positive transfer** → similarity helps.

**Negative transfer/interference** → differences cause difficulty.

---

# 3️⃣ SECOND LANGUAGE LEARNING AND BILINGUALISM

Now we move one step further.

## What is second language learning?

It is the process by which a person develops knowledge and ability in a language other than their first language.

This can happen through:

🏫 classroom instruction
👨‍👩‍👧 family
🌎 living in another country
🗣️ interaction
📺 media
📱 technology
📚 self-study
💬 communication

---

# 🧠 Learning vs Acquisition

These terms are sometimes distinguished in language-learning theories.

### Learning

Usually refers to **conscious/formal knowledge**.

Example:

> “I learned that English past tense is often formed with -ed.”

### Acquisition

Usually refers to developing language ability through **exposure, use and interaction**, often without consciously learning every rule.

Example:

A child naturally develops grammatical patterns without studying a grammar textbook.

⚠️ Different theories use these terms differently, so don't treat the distinction as universally agreed.

---

# 👥 What is bilingualism?

**Bilingualism = ability to use two languages.**

Simple definition:

> A bilingual person has knowledge/use of two languages.

But bilingualism is **not simply “speaking two languages perfectly.”**

This is VERY important.

---

# ❌ Myth

> “A bilingual person must speak both languages exactly like a native speaker.”

Not necessarily.

A bilingual person may:

* speak one language better
* read another better
* use one at home
* use another at work
* mix languages
* have different vocabulary in each language

Bilingualism is therefore **complex and multidimensional**, involving linguistic, psychological and social factors. 

---

# 🧩 Types of bilingualism

There are several ways scholars classify bilingualism.

### 1. Individual bilingualism

One individual knows/uses two languages.

### 2. Societal bilingualism

A society/community uses two languages.

Example:

A country or region where two languages are regularly used.

---

## Based on proficiency

### Balanced bilingual

The person has relatively similar ability in both languages.

### Dominant bilingual

One language is stronger.

Example:

Hindi: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
English: ⭐⭐⭐

---

# Based on age of learning

### Simultaneous bilingualism 👶

Two languages are acquired from very early childhood.

### Sequential bilingualism

One language develops first and another is introduced later.

---

# 🔥 Bilingualism and L2 learning are connected

Think:

**L1**

↓

Learner begins learning another language

↓

**L2 learning**

↓

Learner can develop ability in both languages

↓

**Bilingualism**

But:

> Not every L2 learner becomes highly bilingual.

And:

> A bilingual person does not necessarily have equal proficiency in both languages.

---

# 💡 Modern idea: bilingual's languages interact

Older approaches sometimes imagined:

🧠 Language 1 | Language 2

as two completely separate boxes.

Modern perspectives increasingly emphasize that bilinguals use their linguistic resources dynamically.

For example, **translanguaging** describes how multilingual speakers can draw on their whole linguistic repertoire to make meaning rather than treating named languages as completely isolated systems.

---

# 4️⃣ SECOND LANGUAGE vs FOREIGN LANGUAGE

This is another **VERY IMPORTANT distinction.** ⭐⭐⭐

Students often confuse:

> Second language
> Foreign language

They aren't exactly the same.

---

# 🌍 Second Language

A **second language** is generally learned/used in an environment where that language has an important social or institutional role.

### Example

Suppose:

Hindi is someone's L1.

They live in a multilingual environment where English is widely used in:

* education
* administration
* employment
* public communication

English may function as an **L2**.

---

# ✈️ Foreign Language

A **foreign language** is generally learned where the target language does **not have a major role in the surrounding society**.

Example:

A student in India studying Japanese as a school subject.

Japanese:

📚 studied in class
but

🏠 not normally used in the surrounding community.

So Japanese is a **foreign language** in that context.

---

# 🆚 Second Language vs Foreign Language

| Second Language                               | Foreign Language                                     |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Used in wider society                         | Limited use in surrounding society                   |
| Learner may encounter it outside classroom    | Often mainly encountered in classroom                |
| Greater opportunities for natural interaction | Fewer natural opportunities                          |
| May be needed for education/work/social life  | Often studied for education, travel, interest, exams |
| Natural exposure may be high                  | Classroom exposure may dominate                      |

### Simple memory trick 🧠

> **L2 = language is around you 🌎**

> **Foreign language = language is mainly in the classroom 📚**

But remember:

⚠️ These categories are **context-dependent**, and scholars don't always use the labels in exactly the same way.

---

# 5️⃣ SECOND LANGUAGE LEARNING vs SECOND LANGUAGE ACQUISITION

You may see:

### SLA = Second Language Acquisition

This is the academic field studying how people develop another language.

It asks questions like:

❓ How does L2 learning happen?

❓ Why do some learners become highly proficient?

❓ Why do learners make certain errors?

❓ Does age matter?

❓ How does L1 influence L2?

❓ How important is input?

❓ How important is interaction?

❓ What role does motivation play?

❓ How does instruction affect learning?

---

# 🧠 Major factors affecting L2 learning

| Factor                   | What it means                     |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| 👶 Age                    | When learning begins              |
| 🗣️ Input                  | Language the learner hears/reads  |
| 💬 Interaction            | Communication with others         |
| 🧠 Cognition              | Mental processes                  |
| ❤️ Motivation             | Desire to learn                   |
| 🌎 Environment            | Social/language environment       |
| 🏠 L1                     | Influence of previous language    |
| 👩‍🏫 Instruction            | Teaching and classroom methods    |
| 👤 Individual differences | Aptitude, personality, strategies |
| ⏳ Exposure               | Amount and quality of contact     |

Modern research also challenges overly simple claims that adult learners are simply incapable of acquiring languages well; evidence emphasizes factors such as input, experience and learning context.

# 6.1 📜 TRADITIONAL GRAMMAR

Traditional grammar is one of the oldest approaches to studying language.

It focused heavily on:

* rules
* correctness
* parts of speech
* sentence analysis
* written language
* classical languages, especially Latin and Greek

### Example

Teacher says:

> “Do not say this. This is grammatically incorrect.”

The emphasis is:

**correct rule → correct sentence**

---

## Effect on teaching

This contributed to:

# 📖 Grammar-Translation Method

Students often:

* learned grammar rules
* memorized vocabulary
* translated sentences
* translated literary texts
* studied written language

### Main goal:

📚 Reading and translating.

---

# 6.2 🔬 STRUCTURAL LINGUISTICS

Then linguistics began moving toward a more **scientific/descriptive** approach.

Important figures include:

### Ferdinand de Saussure 🇨🇭

and

### Leonard Bloomfield 🇺🇸

Structural linguistics focused on language as a **system of interconnected structures**.

---

# What did structuralists study?

They paid strong attention to:

🔊 Phonology
🧩 Morphology
📝 Syntax
🔤 observable language patterns

---

# Key idea

Instead of simply asking:

> “What is the correct grammar rule?”

the structuralist asks:

> “How is this language actually structured and used?”

---

# 🎧 Effect on language teaching

Structural linguistics influenced:

# Audio-Lingual Method

The method emphasized:

👂 Listening
🗣️ Speaking
🔁 Repetition
🧩 Sentence patterns
🎯 Habit formation

Example:

Teacher:

> I am going.

Students:

> I am going.

Teacher:

> He...

Students:

> He is going.

Teacher:

> They...

Students:

> They are going.

This is **pattern practice**.

---

# 6.3 🧠 BEHAVIOURISM

Behaviourism strongly influenced early language-learning theory.

Important name:

### B. F. Skinner

The basic idea was:

> Learning happens through stimulus → response → reinforcement.

### Example:

Teacher:

> What is this?

Student:

> A book.

Teacher:

> Good! 👍

The correct response is reinforced.

---

# Behaviourist view of language learning

**Stimulus**
⬇️
**Response**
⬇️
**Reinforcement**
⬇️
**Habit formation**

This strongly influenced the Audio-Lingual Method.

---

# ❗ Problem with pure behaviourism

Human language is much more creative than simple habit formation.

For example, a child can produce a sentence they have **never heard before**.

So researchers began looking for explanations involving the mind and underlying knowledge.

---

# 6.4 🧠 TRANSFORMATIONAL-GENERATIVE GRAMMAR

Now comes:

# Noam Chomsky

Chomsky challenged the idea that language is simply a collection of learned habits.

He emphasized:

🧠 mental knowledge
🧠 creativity
🧠 underlying grammatical system

---

# Very simple example

A child hears a limited number of sentences.

But the child can produce **new sentences**.

Therefore:

> Language is not simply memorizing sentences.

There must be some underlying mental system that allows humans to generate sentences.

---

# Important concepts

### Competence

The speaker's underlying knowledge of language.

### Performance

Actual language use.

A person may know a grammatical structure but make a mistake while speaking because of:

* tiredness
* distraction
* pressure
* memory limitations

---

# Why was Chomsky important for language teaching?

He helped shift attention toward:

🧠 mental processes
🧠 internal knowledge
🧠 creativity

rather than seeing language only as:

🔁 habit formation.

---

# 6.5 🧠 COGNITIVE APPROACH

Language learning increasingly came to be viewed as a **mental/cognitive process**.

The learner is not simply:

> stimulus → response machine 🤖

Instead, the learner:

* notices
* thinks
* organizes
* remembers
* understands
* processes
* produces
* solves problems

---

# Example

Suppose you see:

> I went yesterday.

You may notice:

> “Oh, go → went.”

You begin to form an internal understanding of the language.

That is a cognitive process.

---

# 6.6 👥 SOCIOLINGUISTICS

Then researchers increasingly recognized:

> Language doesn't exist only inside the brain.

Language exists in **society**.

Sociolinguistics studies the relationship between:

**Language ↔ Society**

It examines:

* dialects
* accents
* social class
* gender
* identity
* language variation
* multilingualism
* language attitudes
* language choice
* language change

---

# Example

You might speak differently:

🏠 at home
🎓 at university
👩‍💼 in an interview
👯 with friends

Same person.

Different context.

Therefore:

> Language use depends partly on social situation.

---

# 6.7 💬 FUNCTIONAL / COMMUNICATIVE APPROACHES

Language is not merely a grammatical structure.

Language also performs **functions**.

We use language to:

* request
* apologize
* greet
* invite
* complain
* persuade
* suggest
* refuse
* agree
* disagree
* express feelings

---

# Example

Sentence:

> “Can you open the door?”

Grammatically, it looks like a question about ability.

But in real communication, it is often a:

👉 **request**

So language teaching must teach:

> **What does the sentence DO?**

not only:

> **How is the sentence formed?**

---

# 🗣️ COMMUNICATIVE LANGUAGE TEACHING

This led toward approaches emphasizing:

### Communication

rather than only:

### Grammar accuracy.

Students should have opportunities to:

💬 communicate
🤝 interact
🎭 role-play
🧩 solve problems
🗣️ negotiate meaning

# 📝 EXAM DEFINITIONS — MEMORIZE THESE

### Language Teaching

> Language teaching is the systematic process of helping learners develop the knowledge and skills necessary to understand and use a language effectively.

### L1

> L1 is a person's first or primary language acquired during early development.

### L2

> L2 refers to a language learned or acquired after the first language and is often used broadly for an additional language.

### Bilingualism

> Bilingualism is the ability to use or have knowledge of two languages, although proficiency may differ between the languages.

### Second Language Acquisition

> Second Language Acquisition is the study and process of how learners develop knowledge and ability in a language other than their first language.

### Foreign Language

> A foreign language is a language learned in an environment where it does not normally function as a major language of everyday social communication.

### Linguistic Theory

> A linguistic theory is a systematic explanation of the nature, structure, use or acquisition of language.

---

# ⭐ VERY IMPORTANT DIFFERENCES

## L1 vs L2

**L1:** first language
**L2:** additional/second language

---

## L2 vs Foreign Language

**L2:** target language has a meaningful role in the surrounding environment.

**Foreign language:** target language generally has limited use in the surrounding environment.

---

## Language Learning vs Bilingualism

**Language learning:** process of developing another language.

**Bilingualism:** condition/ability involving use or knowledge of two languages.

---

## Linguistics vs Language Teaching

**Linguistics:** studies language scientifically.

**Language teaching:** applies knowledge and teaching principles to help people learn/use a language.
