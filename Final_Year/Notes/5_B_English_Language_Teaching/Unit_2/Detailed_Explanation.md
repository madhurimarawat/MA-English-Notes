# 📚 UNIT–II: LANGUAGE TEACHING THEORIES

The unit is basically showing us **how ideas about language learning changed over time**.

The methods move roughly like this:

**Grammar & Translation → Speaking → Reading → Habit Formation → Audio-Visual Learning → Mental/Cognitive Learning**

### 🧠 Easy memory line

> **GRAMMAR → SPEAK → READ → REPEAT → SEE & HEAR → THINK**

Language Teaching Theories Definition

Language teaching theories are the ideas and principles that explain how a language can be taught and learned effectively. They guide teachers in choosing appropriate teaching methods, classroom activities, materials, and techniques.

In simple words:

> Language teaching theories are different ideas about how students learn a language and how teachers should teach it.


Exam definition:

> Language teaching theories are systematic principles and beliefs about how languages are learned and how they should be taught, which help teachers select suitable methods and classroom practices.

---

# 1️⃣ Grammar-Translation Method

### Also called: **Traditional Method**

## 🌟 What is it?

The **Grammar-Translation Method (GTM)** is one of the oldest formal methods of language teaching.

Its main purpose was traditionally to help learners:

* understand grammar
* translate texts
* read literature
* learn vocabulary
* develop accuracy

It gives **very little importance to natural conversation**.

### In simple words:

> **Learn grammar rules → learn vocabulary → translate sentences → read texts.**

---

# 🧠 Example

Suppose students are learning the past tense.

The teacher explains:

> Simple Past is used for completed actions in the past.

Then gives:

> I went to school yesterday.

Students translate it into their first language.

Then they may be asked to translate:

> She visited her grandmother.

> They played football.

So the emphasis is on **grammar + translation**.

---

# ⭐ Main Features

### 1. Grammar is central

Students learn grammatical rules directly.

Example:

> Present Perfect = has/have + past participle

---

### 2. Translation is important

Students translate:

**L1 → L2**

and

**L2 → L1**

---

### 3. Reading is more important than speaking

The method focuses heavily on written language.

---

### 4. Vocabulary is often learned through lists

For example:

| English   | Meaning |
| --------- | ------- |
| Beautiful | सुंदर     |
| Difficult | कठिन     |
| Important | महत्वपूर्ण |
| Courage   | साहस     |

---

### 5. Mother tongue is used

The teacher can explain grammar and vocabulary through the learners' first language.

---

### 6. Literature is important

Classical/literary texts are often used as teaching material.

---

### 7. Accuracy is emphasized

Students are expected to produce grammatically correct sentences.

---

# 👩‍🏫 Role of Teacher

The teacher is usually:

* authority
* grammar explainer
* source of correct answers
* evaluator

Students mainly:

* listen
* memorize
* translate
* learn rules
* complete exercises

---

# 🎯 Main Skills

| Skill      | Importance |
| ---------- | ---------- |
| Reading    | ⭐⭐⭐        |
| Writing    | ⭐⭐⭐        |
| Grammar    | ⭐⭐⭐        |
| Vocabulary | ⭐⭐         |
| Speaking   | ⭐          |
| Listening  | ⭐          |

---

# 👍 Advantages

* Grammar is clearly explained.
* Students develop reading ability.
* Useful for understanding difficult written texts.
* Translation develops awareness of differences between languages.
* Easy to organize in large classes.
* Tests are relatively easy to prepare.

---

# 👎 Disadvantages

* Speaking ability may remain weak.
* Listening gets little attention.
* Students may know grammar but struggle to communicate.
* Too much memorization.
* Translation can become mechanical.
* Language may be treated as rules rather than communication.

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**GTM = GRAMMAR + TRANSLATION + READING**

---

# 2️⃣ The Direct Method

The **Direct Method** developed partly as a reaction against the Grammar-Translation Method.

## 🌟 Basic idea

> **Teach the second/foreign language directly without depending on translation into the mother tongue.**

So instead of:

**English → Mother tongue → Meaning**

we try:

**English → Meaning**

---

# 🗣️ Example

Teacher holds up a book and says:

> "This is a book."

Students see the object and understand the meaning.

Teacher does not necessarily say the equivalent word in their mother tongue.

Then:

Teacher:

> "Is this a book?"

Students:

> "Yes, it is."

---

# ⭐ Main Features

### 1. No/very little translation

The target language is used directly.

---

### 2. Speaking is important

Students are encouraged to communicate orally.

---

### 3. Everyday vocabulary

Common words and expressions are taught.

Examples:

* Good morning.
* How are you?
* Where do you live?
* What is this?

---

### 4. Grammar is taught inductively

This is **VERY IMPORTANT**. ⭐⭐⭐

Instead of giving the rule first, students see examples and discover the rule.

### Deductive:

**Rule → Examples**

### Inductive:

**Examples → Rule**

---

### Example

Teacher gives:

> I am reading.
> She is reading.
> They are reading.

Students gradually understand the pattern.

---

### 5. Demonstration is important

Teachers can use:

* objects
* pictures
* actions
* gestures
* examples

---

### 6. Pronunciation is emphasized

Learners practise speaking and pronunciation.

---

# 👩‍🏫 Role of Teacher

The teacher is:

* model speaker
* demonstrator
* questioner
* conversation organizer

Students are expected to:

* speak
* answer questions
* participate
* observe
* imitate appropriate language

---

# 👍 Advantages

* Develops speaking ability.
* Improves pronunciation.
* Encourages thinking in the target language.
* Makes classroom interaction active.
* Connects language with real situations.

# 👎 Disadvantages

* Difficult in very large classes.
* Requires skilled teachers.
* Difficult to explain some abstract concepts without translation.
* Can be time-consuming.
* Not always easy to use in classrooms with limited resources.

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**DIRECT = NO TRANSLATION → DIRECTLY TO MEANING**

---

# 3️⃣ The Reading Method

## 🌟 What is it?

The **Reading Method** gives primary importance to **reading ability**.

The method developed partly because educators recognized that many learners did not actually need fluent spoken language. They mainly needed to **read books, academic material, technical texts, or professional documents**.

### Simple idea:

> **If your main purpose is reading, teach reading efficiently.**

---

# 📖 Example

Suppose students are studying English mainly to understand academic books.

The teacher focuses on:

* vocabulary
* reading comprehension
* understanding passages
* recognizing important grammatical structures
* using dictionaries
* identifying main ideas

Speaking practice becomes less important.

---

# ⭐ Main Features

### 1. Reading is the primary skill

This is the biggest feature.

---

### 2. Vocabulary is important

Students need enough vocabulary to understand texts.

---

### 3. Grammar supports reading

Grammar is taught mainly when it helps students understand sentences.

---

### 4. Translation may be used

Unlike the Direct Method, translation is not necessarily rejected.

---

### 5. Comprehension is important

Students answer questions such as:

> What is the main idea?

> Who is the speaker?

> Why did the character leave?

---

# 🎯 Example Classroom Activity

Teacher gives a passage:

> "The Industrial Revolution changed the economic and social life of Europe..."

Students:

1. Read the passage.
2. Identify difficult words.
3. Find the main idea.
4. Answer comprehension questions.
5. Translate difficult sentences if necessary.

---

# 👍 Advantages

* Useful for academic purposes.
* Develops reading comprehension.
* Helps learners access books and written information.
* Practical when speaking is not the main need.

# 👎 Disadvantages

* Speaking may remain weak.
* Listening may receive little attention.
* Students may become dependent on written language.
* Does not fully prepare learners for everyday communication.

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**READING METHOD = LANGUAGE FOR READING**

---

# 4️⃣ Audio-Lingual Method

Now we come to a **VERY IMPORTANT method**. ⭐⭐⭐

## 🌟 Basic idea

The **Audio-Lingual Method (ALM)** became especially influential in the mid-20th century.

It is strongly associated with:

**Structural Linguistics + Behaviourist Psychology**

### Main idea:

> **Language learning is largely the formation of correct language habits through practice and repetition.**

---

# 🧠 The basic formula

### **Hear → Repeat → Practise → Form a habit**

For example:

Teacher:

> "I am going to school."

Students:

> "I am going to school."

Teacher:

> "He..."

Students:

> "He is going to school."

Teacher:

> "They..."

Students:

> "They are going to school."

This is **pattern practice**.

---

# ⭐ Main Features

### 1. Listening and speaking come first

The learner hears and speaks the language before focusing heavily on reading and writing.

---

### 2. Drills are very important

Students repeat patterns many times.

---

### 3. Habit formation

Correct language habits are developed through practice.

---

### 4. Errors are discouraged

The teacher tries to prevent incorrect habits from becoming established.

---

### 5. Dialogue is used

Students memorize and practise dialogues.

Example:

> A: Where are you going?
> B: I'm going to the market.

---

### 6. Grammar is taught through patterns

Instead of lengthy grammar explanations, learners practise structures.

---

# 🔄 Important Types of Drills

### 1. Repetition Drill

Teacher:

> She is a student.

Students repeat:

> She is a student.

---

### 2. Substitution Drill

Teacher:

> I like tea.

Teacher gives:

> coffee

Students:

> I like coffee.

---

### 3. Transformation Drill

Teacher:

> He is coming.

Teacher:

> Negative.

Students:

> He is not coming.

---

### 4. Question-Answer Drill

Teacher:

> Do you like English?

Student:

> Yes, I do.

---

# 🎓 Theoretical Foundation

This is **VERY IMPORTANT**. ⭐⭐⭐

### Structural Linguistics

Language is viewed as a system of:

**patterns + structures**

### Behaviourism

Learning is associated with:

**Stimulus → Response → Reinforcement**

Together:

**Structural Linguistics + Behaviourism → Audio-Lingual Method**

---

# 👍 Advantages

* Good pronunciation practice.
* Provides extensive oral practice.
* Useful for automatic use of basic structures.
* Gives students lots of controlled practice.
* Can help learners become familiar with common sentence patterns.

# 👎 Disadvantages

* Too much repetition can become boring.
* Learners may produce correct patterns without understanding deeply.
* Does not always develop spontaneous communication.
* Treating language learning mainly as habit formation is considered too limited by later cognitive approaches.

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**ALM = LISTEN + REPEAT + DRILL + HABIT**

---

# 5️⃣ Audio-Visual Method

Now this is particularly important because your syllabus specifically asks for:

* Features
* Sources and History
* Techniques
* Theoretical Assumptions

So let's study each one separately. ⭐

---

# 📺 What is the Audio-Visual Method?

The **Audio-Visual Method** teaches language through the combination of:

### 👂 Audio

**What learners hear**

*

### 👀 Visual

**What learners see**

The learner connects:

**Sound + Image + Meaning**

---

# 🌟 Simple Example

Teacher shows a picture of a classroom.

At the same time, students hear:

> "This is a classroom."

They see:

🏫 classroom

and hear:

**"This is a classroom."**

The visual situation helps students understand the meaning without necessarily translating every word.

---

# 📜 A. Sources and History

The Audio-Visual Method is especially associated with **French language teaching** in the mid-20th century.

It developed strongly at the **Centre de Recherche et d'Étude pour la Diffusion du Français (CREDIF)** and is associated with scholars such as **Georges Gougenheim, Paul Rivenc and Petar Guberina**.

It developed in the post-World War II period when there was a growing need for practical language teaching and efficient oral communication.

The method drew on developments in:

* structural linguistics
* spoken-language teaching
* audio technology
* visual materials
* situational teaching

### 🧠 Simple history line

**Post-war need → better language teaching → audio + visual materials → Audio-Visual Method**

---

# ⭐ B. Features of Audio-Visual Method

## 1. Audio + Visual together

This is the defining feature.

**Hear it + See it**

---

## 2. Meaning through situation

Language is presented within a meaningful situation.

For example:

Picture:

🚉 A railway station

Dialogue:

> "Where is the train to Paris?"

Students understand the language through the situation.

---

## 3. Oral language is important

Listening and speaking receive strong attention.

---

## 4. Dialogue is important

Learners hear and practise dialogues related to everyday situations.

---

## 5. Vocabulary is presented in context

Instead of:

> train = ___
> ticket = ___
> station = ___

students encounter:

> "Where can I buy a ticket?"

within a visual situation.

---

## 6. Visual materials are important

Teachers can use:

* pictures
* slides
* films
* charts
* objects
* visual sequences

---

# 🎬 C. Techniques

### 1. Presentation

Teacher presents a visual situation and corresponding language.

---

### 2. Listening

Students listen carefully to the recorded dialogue or teacher.

---

### 3. Repetition

Students repeat the language they hear.

---

### 4. Question-answer

Teacher asks questions about the visual situation.

---

### 5. Pattern practice

Students practise important structures.

---

### 6. Role-play

Students act out the situation.

Example:

One student = shopkeeper 🧑‍💼

Another = customer 🛍️

They practise:

> "How much is this?"

> "It's ten pounds."

---

### 7. Recombination

Learners use familiar language in slightly different combinations or situations.

---

# 🧠 D. Theoretical Assumptions

The Audio-Visual Method assumes that:

### 1. Language is primarily oral

Language is first encountered as **spoken communication**.

---

### 2. Language is learned in meaningful situations

Students understand language better when it is connected to a situation.

**Situation → Meaning → Language**

---

### 3. Visual context helps understanding

A picture or action can help learners understand without translation.

---

### 4. Language patterns are important

The method has a structural influence.

Learners practise useful language patterns.

---

### 5. Learning should involve the senses

The learner:

👂 hears

👀 sees

🗣️ speaks

This makes learning more concrete.

---

### 6. Communication is important

The goal is not simply to memorize grammar rules.

Learners should understand and use language in situations.

---

# 🆚 Audio-Lingual vs Audio-Visual

| Audio-Lingual                | Audio-Visual                                    |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Audio = hearing              | Audio = hearing                                 |
| Lingual = language/speech    | Visual = pictures/images                        |
| Strongly drill-based         | Strongly situation-based                        |
| Habit formation              | Meaning through context                         |
| Repetition                   | Visual + oral presentation                      |
| Structuralism + Behaviourism | Structural/situational + visual/oral principles |
| Hear → Repeat                | See + Hear → Understand → Use                   |

### 🧠 Easiest difference:

**Audio-Lingual = HEAR + REPEAT** 👂🔁

**Audio-Visual = SEE + HEAR + UNDERSTAND** 👀👂🧠

---

# 6️⃣ Cognitive Theory

Now we reach the next major shift.

The **Cognitive Theory** developed partly as a reaction against the idea that language learning is simply habit formation.

## 🧠 Main idea

> **Language learning involves thinking, understanding, mental processing and the active participation of the learner.**

Instead of seeing the learner as someone who simply:

**hears → repeats → forms habits**

Cognitive theory sees the learner as someone who:

**thinks → understands → processes → learns → creates**

---

# 🔥 Why was this important?

Behaviourist approaches often emphasized:

**Stimulus → Response → Reinforcement**

But cognitive approaches argued:

> Human beings don't simply imitate language. They understand and creatively use it.

---

# 🧠 Example

A student has learned:

> I played cricket yesterday.

The student can then create:

> I watched television yesterday.

> I visited my friend yesterday.

> I studied English yesterday.

They are not merely repeating one memorized sentence.

They have understood a **pattern** and can use it to create new sentences.

That is cognitive learning.

---

# ⭐ Main Features of Cognitive Theory

### 1. Learning is a mental process

The learner's mind is active.

---

### 2. Understanding is important

Students should understand what they are learning.

---

### 3. Meaning is important

Learning should not be meaningless repetition.

---

### 4. Learners are active

The student is not simply a passive receiver.

The learner:

* thinks
* analyses
* makes connections
* solves problems
* forms hypotheses
* uses language creatively

---

### 5. Errors can be meaningful

An error may show that the learner is developing an internal understanding of the language.

Therefore:

❌ Every error = bad habit

is too simplistic.

Instead:

✅ Error = possible evidence of the learner's developing language system.

---

### 6. Creativity is important

Learners can produce sentences they have never heard before.

---

# 👩‍🏫 Role of Teacher

The teacher becomes more of a:

* guide
* facilitator
* organizer
* explainer

rather than simply a drill instructor.

---

# 👨‍🎓 Role of Learner

The learner is:

* active
* thinking
* analysing
* problem-solving
* experimenting
* creating language

---

# 🆚 Behaviourism vs Cognitive Theory

| Behaviourism                  | Cognitive Theory                            |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Learning as habit formation   | Learning as mental processing               |
| Stimulus → Response           | Thinking → Understanding                    |
| Repetition important          | Understanding important                     |
| Drill-based                   | Meaning-based                               |
| Errors are undesirable habits | Errors can show learning/development        |
| Learner responds              | Learner actively processes                  |
| External behaviour emphasized | Mental processes emphasized                 |
| Audio-Lingual Method          | Influenced later learner-centred approaches |

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**BEHAVIOURISM = HABIT**

**COGNITIVISM = MIND**

# 📝 VERY IMPORTANT SHORT QUESTIONS

Prepare these:

### 1. What is the Grammar-Translation Method?

A method emphasizing grammar rules, translation, vocabulary, reading and written accuracy.

### 2. What is the Direct Method?

A method that teaches the target language directly, with little or no use of translation, and gives importance to oral communication.

### 3. What is the Reading Method?

A method that gives primary importance to developing reading ability and comprehension.

### 4. What is the Audio-Lingual Method?

A method based strongly on structural patterns and behaviourist ideas, using listening, repetition and drills to develop language habits.

### 5. What is the Audio-Visual Method?

A method that combines spoken language with visual materials to teach language through meaningful situations.

### 6. What is Cognitive Theory?

A theory that views language learning as an active mental process involving understanding, processing, thinking and creative use of language.
