# 📚 UNIT–V: LANGUAGE TESTING

This unit is about **how we measure a learner’s knowledge and ability in English**.

The basic idea is:

### **TEACH → LEARN → TEST → FIND RESULTS → IMPROVE**

Language testing is not only about giving marks. A good language test helps us understand:

* What a learner knows
* What a learner can do
* Where the learner has difficulties
* How much the learner has learned
* What the learner may be capable of learning

---

# 🌟 1. THE CONSTRUCTION AND USE OF LANGUAGE TESTS

## ⭐ What is a Language Test?

A **language test** is a systematic way of measuring a person's knowledge or ability in a language.

In simple words:

> **A language test is a planned assessment used to find out what a learner knows or can do in a language.**

For example, an English test may measure:

**Grammar + Vocabulary + Reading + Listening + Speaking + Pronunciation + Writing**

---

# ⭐ Why Do We Give Language Tests?

Tests can be used to:

### 1. Measure achievement

Find out how much students have learned.

### 2. Diagnose difficulties

Find out what students are struggling with.

### 3. Predict future learning

Estimate a learner's potential for learning a language.

### 4. Place students

Put students into appropriate classes or levels.

### 5. Evaluate teaching

Help teachers understand whether instruction has been effective.

---

# 🔹 Basic Principles of Test Construction

A good language test should be:

### 1. Valid

It should **measure what it is supposed to measure**.

Example:

If the purpose is to test speaking ability, the test should actually require students to speak.

### 2. Reliable

It should produce **reasonably consistent results**.

### 3. Practical

It should be possible to administer, score and manage.

### 4. Objective

Scoring should be as fair and consistent as possible.

### 5. Appropriate

The test should match:

* learner level
* purpose
* syllabus
* skills being tested

# 🌟 2. TECHNIQUES TO TEST THE PRODUCTION OF SOUND SEGMENTS

This sounds complicated, but it is simple.

## ⭐ What are Sound Segments?

Sound segments are **individual speech sounds**, mainly:

**Vowels + Consonants**

For example:

/p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /ɪ/, /iː/

So this section asks:

> **How can we test whether a learner can produce English sounds correctly?**

---

# 🔹 1. Imitation

The teacher gives a model.

Student listens and repeats.

**Teacher:** /p/

**Student:** /p/

The teacher checks whether the sound is produced correctly.

---

# 🔹 2. Word Production

Ask students to pronounce words containing a particular sound.

For example:

**/p/** → pen, paper, apple

**/b/** → book, baby, table

---

# 🔹 3. Minimal-Pair Test

This is **VERY IMPORTANT ⭐⭐⭐**.

Students pronounce pairs of words containing contrasting sounds.

Examples:

**ship — sheep**

**fan — van**

**rice — rise**

The teacher checks whether the learner can produce the difference.

---

# 🔹 4. Reading Aloud

Give students a list of words or sentences.

Students read aloud.

The teacher evaluates pronunciation.

---

# 🔹 5. Picture-Based Production

Show a picture and ask the learner to name it.

For example:

🖼️ Picture of a **fish**

Student says the word.

The teacher evaluates the relevant sound.

---

# ⭐ What is Being Tested?

The teacher may look at:

* correct sound production
* vowel quality
* consonant production
* voicing
* sound contrasts
* intelligibility

### 🧠 Memory:

**IMITATE → SAY WORD → MINIMAL PAIR → READ → PICTURE**

---

# 🌟 3. TECHNIQUES FOR TESTING INTONATION

## ⭐ What is Intonation?

**Intonation is the rise and fall of pitch in speech.**

Remember:

**↗ Rising**

**↘ Falling**

---

# 🔹 How can we test it?

## 1. Reading Sentences Aloud

Give students sentences and ask them to read them naturally.

Example:

**You are coming. ↘**

**Are you coming? ↗**

The teacher listens to the pitch movement.

---

## 2. Question and Answer

Ask students questions and observe their intonation.

Example:

**Are you ready?**

Student responds naturally.

---

## 3. Situational Tasks

Give a situation.

Example:

> You are surprised to hear that your friend has won a prize.

Student responds.

The teacher observes whether the learner uses appropriate intonation.

---

## 4. Dialogue Reading

Students read a dialogue.

The teacher checks whether the intonation matches:

* questions
* statements
* surprise
* emphasis
* attitude

---

# ⭐ What is Tested?

* Rising/falling intonation
* Appropriate pitch movement
* Sentence stress
* Expression of meaning/attitude
* Naturalness of speech

### 🧠 Memory:

**READ → ANSWER → SITUATION → DIALOGUE**

---

# 🌟 4. TECHNIQUES TO TEST THE PRODUCTION OF LEXICAL UNITS

## ⭐ What are Lexical Units?

**Lexical units are words or vocabulary items.**

So this section is basically:

> **How do we test whether learners know and can produce English vocabulary?**

---

# 🔹 1. Picture Naming

Show a picture.

Student names it.

🖼️ 🍎

Student:

**apple**

---

# 🔹 2. Word Completion

Give part of a word.

Example:

**teach____**

Student:

**teacher**

---

# 🔹 3. Sentence Completion

Give a sentence with a missing word.

> I drink ______ every morning.

Possible answer:

**tea**

---

# 🔹 4. Word Association

Give one word and ask students to produce a related word.

Example:

**School → teacher**

**Doctor → hospital**

---

# 🔹 5. Synonyms

Give a word and ask for a similar word.

**big → large**

---

# 🔹 6. Antonyms

Give a word and ask for its opposite.

**hot → cold**

---

# 🔹 7. Use the Word in a Sentence

Give the word:

**“generous”**

Student creates a sentence.

This tests whether the learner knows not only the meaning but also how to use the word.

---

# ⭐ What is Being Tested?

Vocabulary testing can measure:

* meaning
* pronunciation
* spelling
* word formation
* appropriate use
* collocation
* contextual use

### 🧠 Memory:

**PICTURE → COMPLETE → ASSOCIATE → SYNONYM → ANTONYM → USE**

---

# 🌟 5. TESTING AUDITORY COMPREHENSION

## ⭐ What is Auditory Comprehension?

**Auditory comprehension means understanding language that we hear.**

In simple words:

> **Can the learner listen to English and understand it?**

---

# 🔊 Example

Teacher/audio says:

> “The train will leave at six o'clock.”

Question:

**What time will the train leave?**

Student:

**Six o'clock.**

This tests listening comprehension.

---

# 🔹 Techniques

## 1. Listening and Answering Questions

Students listen to a passage and answer questions.

---

## 2. True / False

Students listen and decide whether statements are true or false.

---

## 3. Multiple Choice

Students listen and select the correct answer.

---

## 4. Following Instructions

Teacher says:

> “Open your book and underline the second sentence.”

Student follows the instruction.

This tests whether the learner understands spoken language.

---

## 5. Dictation

Teacher reads a passage or sentences.

Students write what they hear.

This can test:

**Listening + sound discrimination + vocabulary + spelling**

---

## 6. Note-Taking

Students listen to a lecture or passage and make notes.

Useful for advanced learners.

---

# ⭐ What is Tested?

* understanding main ideas
* understanding details
* identifying important information
* recognizing words and phrases
* following instructions
* making inferences

### 🧠 Memory:

**LISTEN → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND**

---

# 🌟 6. HOW TO TEST SPEAKING ABILITY

## ⭐ What is Speaking Ability?

Speaking ability is the learner's ability to **communicate orally in English**.

A speaking test should not test only pronunciation.

It may assess:

**Pronunciation + Grammar + Vocabulary + Fluency + Comprehensibility + Communication**

---

# 🔹 Techniques for Testing Speaking

## 1. Interview

Teacher asks questions.

Example:

> What is your favourite book?

Student answers.

---

## 2. Conversation

Teacher and student have a short conversation.

This tests natural interaction.

---

## 3. Picture Description

Give a picture.

Ask the learner to describe it.

Example:

> Describe what you can see in this picture.

---

## 4. Role Play

Give a real-life situation.

Example:

**Student = customer**

**Teacher = shopkeeper**

They interact in English.

---

## 5. Story Retelling

Give a short story.

Student retells it in their own words.

---

## 6. Topic Presentation

Give a topic.

Example:

**“Importance of Education”**

Student speaks for a few minutes.

---

# ⭐ What Should the Teacher Assess?

| Area                  | Meaning                                  |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Pronunciation**     | Are sounds understandable?               |
| **Fluency**           | Can the learner speak smoothly?          |
| **Grammar**           | Are structures reasonably accurate?      |
| **Vocabulary**        | Can the learner use suitable words?      |
| **Comprehensibility** | Can others understand the learner?       |
| **Interaction**       | Can the learner respond and participate? |

---

# 🧠 Speaking Memory

### **SPEAKING = P + F + G + V + C**

**P = Pronunciation**

**F = Fluency**

**G = Grammar**

**V = Vocabulary**

**C = Communication/Comprehensibility**

# 🌟 7. ACHIEVEMENT TESTING

## ⭐ What is Achievement Testing?

An **achievement test** measures **what a learner has learned after instruction**.

Simple definition:

> **Achievement testing measures the knowledge and skills that learners have gained from a course or period of instruction.**

---

# 🔹 Example

You study:

**Unit 1–5**

Then your teacher gives an examination based on those units.

That is an **achievement test**.

---

# ⭐ When is it Used?

Usually:

* after a lesson
* after a unit
* at the end of a course
* at the end of a semester/year

---

# ⭐ Purpose

To find out:

**How much has the student learned?**

---

# 🔹 Example

Teacher teaches:

**Past tense**

↓

Students practise it

↓

Test asks students to use past tense

↓

Teacher measures learning

= **Achievement Testing**

---

# 🧠 Memory

### **ACHIEVEMENT = WHAT HAVE I LEARNED?**

---

# 🌟 8. DIAGNOSTIC TESTING

## ⭐ What is Diagnostic Testing?

A **diagnostic test** identifies a learner's **strengths and weaknesses**.

Simple definition:

> **Diagnostic testing is used to find out specific areas in which a learner has difficulty.**

---

# 🔹 Example

A student gets:

**Grammar = good**

**Vocabulary = good**

**Reading = good**

**Pronunciation = weak**

The teacher can then provide extra pronunciation practice.

That is the purpose of diagnostic testing.

---

# ⭐ When is it Used?

It can be used:

* before instruction
* during instruction
* whenever difficulties need to be identified

---

# ⭐ Purpose

Not mainly:

**“How many marks did you get?”**

Instead:

### **“Where exactly is the problem?”**

---

# 🧠 Memory

### **DIAGNOSTIC = FIND THE PROBLEM**

---

# 🌟 9. APTITUDE TESTING

## ⭐ What is Aptitude Testing?

An **aptitude test** attempts to measure a learner's **potential or capacity for learning** something.

In language education:

> **Language aptitude testing estimates how readily a person may learn a new language under appropriate conditions.**

---

# 🔹 Important Difference

Aptitude is **not the same as achievement**.

### Achievement asks:

**What have you learned?**

### Aptitude asks:

**How well might you learn?**

---

# ⭐ Example

Imagine two students have never studied French.

They take a language aptitude test.

The test tries to identify which learner may have greater potential for learning the language.

That is **aptitude testing**.

---

# 🔹 What Can Language Aptitude Involve?

Aptitude tests may examine abilities related to:

* recognizing sound patterns
* remembering language material
* identifying grammatical patterns
* learning associations
* analysing language

---

# 🧠 Memory

### **APTITUDE = POTENTIAL TO LEARN**

---

# 🔥 VERY IMPORTANT: ACHIEVEMENT vs DIAGNOSTIC vs APTITUDE

| Test            | Main Question                                      | Purpose                     |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Achievement** | What has the learner learned?                      | Measure learning            |
| **Diagnostic**  | What is the learner having difficulty with?        | Find strengths/weaknesses   |
| **Aptitude**    | What potential does the learner have for learning? | Estimate learning potential |

### ⭐ BEST MEMORY:

**ACHIEVEMENT = LEARNED**

**DIAGNOSTIC = PROBLEM**

**APTITUDE = POTENTIAL**
