# 📘 UNIT–IV — PHONOLOGY, STRUCTURAL LINGUISTICS & MORPHOLOGY

---

# 🔊 1. PHONOLOGY

## 📖 Definition

> **Phonology is the branch of linguistics that studies the sound system of a language and the way sounds function and pattern within that language.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

**Phonetics** asks:

👉 *How are sounds physically produced?*

**Phonology** asks:

👉 *How do those sounds function and pattern in a particular language?*

### Example

English has the sounds /p/ and /b/.

* **pin** → /pɪn/
* **bin** → /bɪn/

Changing /p/ to /b/ changes the meaning.

Therefore, /p/ and /b/ have a meaningful contrast in English.

---

## 🔥 Phonetics vs Phonology

| Phonetics                                              | Phonology                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| Studies speech sounds physically                       | Studies sound systems                        |
| Concerned with production, transmission and perception | Concerned with function and pattern          |
| More universal                                         | Language-specific                            |
| Example: how /p/ is articulated                        | Example: why /p/ and /b/ contrast in English |

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**PHONETICS = SOUND ITSELF**
**PHONOLOGY = SOUND SYSTEM**

---

## 🔹 Main Concerns of Phonology

Phonology studies:

1. **Phonemes**
2. Sound contrasts
3. Distribution of sounds
4. Sound patterns
5. Syllable structure
6. Stress and intonation
7. Allophones
8. Phonological rules

### Flowchart

```text
PHONOLOGY
    ↓
Sound System of a Language
    ↓
Phonemes → Allophones → Sound Patterns
    ↓
Meaningful Sound Contrasts
```

# 🏛️ 2. STRUCTURAL LINGUISTICS

## 📖 Definition

> **Structural Linguistics is an approach to the study of language that views language as a structured system of interrelated elements.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

Language is **not a random collection of words and sounds**.

Its elements are connected and organized.

```text
Sounds
   ↓
Words
   ↓
Phrases
   ↓
Sentences
   ↓
Meaning
```

Structural linguists therefore study **relationships and patterns** within a language.

---

## ⭐ Ferdinand de Saussure

The development of modern structural linguistics is strongly associated with:

**Ferdinand de Saussure** 🇨🇭

He is often called one of the **founders of modern linguistics**.

### Important ideas associated with Saussure

#### 1. Langue and Parole

| Langue                | Parole            |
| --------------------- | ----------------- |
| Language system       | Actual speech     |
| Social/shared system  | Individual use    |
| Rules and conventions | Actual utterances |

### Example

English grammar and vocabulary shared by speakers = **langue**

A person's particular sentence = **parole**

---

#### 2. Signifier and Signified

A linguistic sign has two sides:

```text
LINGUISTIC SIGN
       ↓
 ┌───────────────┐
 │               │
Signifier     Signified
 │               │
Sound/form      Concept
```

### Example: “TREE”

* **Signifier** → the sound/form /triː/ or the written word *tree*
* **Signified** → the concept of a tree

---

#### 3. Language as a System

A linguistic element gets its value partly through its relationship with other elements.

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**Saussure = System + Sign**

# 🔤 3. THE PHONEME

## 📖 Definition

> **A phoneme is the smallest contrastive sound unit in a language that can distinguish meaning.**

### ⭐ Very Important

A phoneme is **not simply the smallest sound**.

It is the smallest **meaning-distinguishing** sound unit.

---

## Example

Consider:

**pin** → /pɪn/
**bin** → /bɪn/

Only one sound changes:

/p/ → /b/

But the meaning changes.

Therefore:

> /p/ and /b/ are separate phonemes in English.

---

## 🔥 Minimal Pair

A **minimal pair** consists of two words that differ in only one phoneme and have different meanings.

Examples:

| Word 1 | Word 2 | Contrast   |
| ------ | ------ | ---------- |
| pin    | bin    | /p/ vs /b/ |
| ten    | den    | /t/ vs /d/ |
| fan    | van    | /f/ vs /v/ |
| sip    | zip    | /s/ vs /z/ |
| cat    | bat    | /k/ vs /b/ |

### 🧠 Formula

```text
ONE PHONEME CHANGES
        ↓
MEANING CHANGES
        ↓
PHONEMIC CONTRAST
```

---

# 🔊 Phoneme and Allophone

This distinction is **very important for exams**. ⭐

### Phoneme

A sound unit that can distinguish meaning.

### Allophone

A different pronunciation/variant of a phoneme that **does not normally change meaning**.

### Example

The English phoneme /p/ can have different pronunciations:

* **pin** → [pʰɪn] (aspirated)
* **spin** → [spɪn] (generally unaspirated)

These are different phonetic realizations of the phoneme /p/.

### 🧠 Memory

**PHONEME = changes meaning**
**ALLOPHONE = changes pronunciation, not meaning**

---

# 🔄 4. FREE VARIATION

## 📖 Definition

> **Free variation occurs when two or more sounds can occur in the same environment without changing the meaning of the word.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

Two pronunciations are possible, but the meaning remains the same.

### Example

In some varieties of English, a word may have slightly different pronunciations without producing a difference in meaning.

The important point is:

```text
Same linguistic environment
        ↓
Different pronunciation
        ↓
No change in meaning
        ↓
FREE VARIATION
```

---

## Example

A speaker may pronounce a particular word slightly differently from another speaker, while both forms are accepted as the same word.

Thus:

> Variation in pronunciation ≠ necessarily variation in meaning.

### ⭐ Key Point

Free variation is often influenced by:

* speaker
* dialect
* speaking style
* social context
* degree of formality

---

# ⚔️ FREE VARIATION vs CONTRAST

| Free Variation                      | Contrast                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Different sounds/forms              | Different phonemes                 |
| Same environment may allow variants | Difference can distinguish meaning |
| Meaning remains same                | Meaning changes                    |
| Example: pronunciation variants     | pin/bin                            |

### Memory Trick

**FREE = same meaning**
**CONTRAST = different meaning**

---

# 🚫 5. NEUTRALIZATION

## 📖 Definition

> **Neutralization is the loss or suspension of a phonemic contrast in a particular phonological environment.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

Normally two sounds are different phonemes.

But in a particular environment, their contrast is **neutralized**.

```text
Normally:
Sound A ≠ Sound B
      ↓
Contrast exists

Special environment:
Sound A ≈ Sound B
      ↓
Contrast disappears
      ↓
NEUTRALIZATION
```

---

## Example

A classic example is found in some languages where a voiced/voiceless contrast disappears in a particular position, such as at the end of a word.

The important concept is:

> **A distinction that exists elsewhere is lost in a particular environment.**

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**NEUTRALIZATION = NO CONTRAST**

---

# 🔥 Free Variation vs Neutralization

| Free Variation              | Neutralization                                  |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Alternative forms can occur | A contrast disappears                           |
| Meaning remains unchanged   | Phonemic opposition is suspended                |
| Focus on variation          | Focus on loss of contrast                       |
| Often pronunciation choice  | Usually conditioned by phonological environment |

---

# 🧩 6. PATTERN CONGRUITY

This is a slightly more technical topic, so let's make it easy. 🌷

## 📖 Meaning

> **Pattern congruity refers to the regularity or consistency of relationships and patterns within the sound system of a language.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

If sounds behave in similar ways in similar environments, the pattern is **congruent** or regular.

Language tends to organize sounds systematically rather than randomly.

---

## Example

English has several voiced/voiceless pairs:

```text
/p/ ↔ /b/
/t/ ↔ /d/
/k/ ↔ /g/
/f/ ↔ /v/
/s/ ↔ /z/
```

These pairs show a systematic relationship.

The language therefore displays **pattern regularity**.

---

## Why Pattern Congruity Matters

It helps linguists identify:

* regular sound relationships
* phonological patterns
* contrasts
* predictable changes
* similarities between sounds

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**CONGRUITY = CONSISTENCY**

---

# 🧬 7. MORPHOLOGY

## 📖 Definition

> **Morphology is the branch of linguistics that studies the structure, formation and internal organization of words.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

If phonology studies **sound structure**, morphology studies **word structure**.

```text
PHONOLOGY
    ↓
Sound structure

MORPHOLOGY
    ↓
Word structure
```

---

# 🔹 WORDS AND MORPHEMES

## What is a Word?

A **word** is a linguistic unit that can function as a meaningful element in a sentence.

Examples:

* book
* teacher
* unhappy
* played
* cats

But words themselves can sometimes be divided into smaller meaningful units.

These smaller units are called **morphemes**.

---

# 🧩 8. MORPHEME

## 📖 Definition

> **A morpheme is the smallest meaningful unit of language.**

### Example

**cats**

can be divided into:

**cat + s**

* **cat** = meaning “a particular animal”
* **-s** = meaning “plural”

Therefore:

```text
CATS
 ↓
CAT + S
 ↓     ↓
Morpheme + Morpheme
```

---

## More Examples

### UNHAPPY

**un + happy**

* **un-** = not
* **happy** = happy

### REPLAYED

**re + play + ed**

* **re-** = again
* **play** = action
* **-ed** = past tense

### TEACHERS

**teach + er + s**

* teach = action
* -er = person who performs action
* -s = plural

---

# ⭐ Word vs Morpheme

| Word                              | Morpheme                                                 |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| May contain one or more morphemes | Smallest meaningful unit                                 |
| Can sometimes be divided          | Cannot normally be divided into smaller meaningful units |
| Example: teachers                 | teach + er + s                                           |
| May be complex                    | May be simple or complex                                 |

### Important

A word can contain **one morpheme**:

> book

Or **several morphemes**:

> books = book + s

---

# 🌱 9. FREE MORPHEMES

## 📖 Definition

> **A free morpheme is a morpheme that can occur independently as a word.**

### Examples

* book
* house
* boy
* teach
* kind
* run

These can stand alone.

```text
FREE MORPHEME
      ↓
Can stand alone
      ↓
Can function as a word
```

---

## Types of Free Morphemes

Free morphemes are commonly divided into:

### 1. Lexical Morphemes

These carry the main meaning.

Examples:

* boy
* book
* run
* beautiful
* house

### 2. Functional/Grammatical Morphemes

These express grammatical relationships.

Examples:

* and
* but
* the
* in
* on

### 🧠 Memory

**FREE = CAN FLY ALONE 🕊️**

---

# 🔒 10. BOUND MORPHEMES

## 📖 Definition

> **A bound morpheme is a morpheme that cannot normally occur independently and must be attached to another morpheme.**

### Examples

* un-
* re-
* -ed
* -s
* -ness
* -ly
* -er

They cannot normally stand alone as words in these uses.

```text
BOUND MORPHEME
      ↓
Cannot stand alone
      ↓
Must attach to another morpheme
```

---

## Examples

### UN + HAPPY

**un-** = bound morpheme

### KIND + NESS

**-ness** = bound morpheme

### WALK + ED

**-ed** = bound morpheme

### BOOK + S

**-s** = bound morpheme

---

# 🔥 Types of Bound Morphemes

## 1. Prefix

Added before the base/root.

Examples:

* **un** + happy
* **re** + write
* **dis** + agree

```text
PREFIX + BASE
```

---

## 2. Suffix

Added after the base/root.

Examples:

* kind + **ness**
* teach + **er**
* walk + **ed**

```text
BASE + SUFFIX
```

---

# ⚔️ FREE vs BOUND MORPHEMES

| Free Morpheme          | Bound Morpheme                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Can stand alone        | Cannot normally stand alone     |
| Can function as a word | Must attach to another morpheme |
| book                   | -s                              |
| happy                  | un-                             |
| teach                  | -er                             |
| kind                   | -ness                           |

### 🧠 Super Memory Trick

> **FREE = FREE TO STAND ALONE** 🕊️
> **BOUND = BOUND TO ANOTHER MORPHEME** 🔗

---

# 🔄 11. ALLOMORPHS

## 📖 Definition

> **Allomorphs are different forms or variants of the same morpheme that occur in different phonological or grammatical environments.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

One morpheme can have **different pronunciations/forms**, but it performs the **same grammatical function**.

```text
ONE MORPHEME
      ↓
Different forms
      ↓
Different environments
      ↓
ALLOMORPHS
```

---

# ⭐ Classic Example: English Plural

The English plural morpheme is usually written as:

**{-s}**

But it has different pronunciations.

### 1. /s/

After voiceless sounds such as /p, t, k/

Examples:

* cats → /kæts/
* books → /bʊks/

### 2. /z/

After voiced sounds such as /b, d, g, m, n, l, r, vowels/

Examples:

* dogs → /dɔːgz/
* bags → /bægz/
* boys → /bɔɪz/

### 3. /ɪz/ or /əz/

After sibilant sounds such as /s, z, ʃ, ʒ, tʃ, dʒ/

Examples:

* buses → /ˈbʌsɪz/
* wishes → /ˈwɪʃɪz/
* judges → /ˈdʒʌdʒɪz/

---

## 🔥 The Three Plural Allomorphs

```text
       PLURAL MORPHEME
              ↓
       ┌──────┼──────┐
       ↓      ↓      ↓
      /s/    /z/   /ɪz/
       ↓      ↓      ↓
     cats    dogs   buses
```

All three express:

> **PLURALITY**

Therefore, they are **allomorphs of the same plural morpheme**.

---

# ⭐ Another Important Example: Past Tense

The English past-tense morpheme **{-ed}** also has different pronunciations.

### /t/

Examples:

* walked
* kissed
* laughed

### /d/

Examples:

* played
* cleaned
* called

### /ɪd/ or /əd/

Examples:

* wanted
* needed
* started

```text
PAST TENSE
    ↓
 ┌───┼────┐
 ↓   ↓    ↓
/t/ /d/ /ɪd/
```

Again, the grammatical meaning is the same:

> **PAST TENSE**

So these are **allomorphs** of the past-tense morpheme.

---

# 🧠 Morpheme vs Allomorph

| Morpheme                    | Allomorph                         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Abstract meaningful unit    | Actual variant/form of a morpheme |
| Represents meaning/function | Represents different realization  |
| Example: plural {-s}        | /s/, /z/, /ɪz/                    |
| Example: past {-ed}         | /t/, /d/, /ɪd/                    |

### 🧠 Easy Formula

> **MORPHEME = ONE FUNCTION/MEANING**
> **ALLOMORPHS = DIFFERENT FORMS OF THAT MORPHEME**

---

# 🔥 PHONEME vs MORPHEME vs ALLOMORPH

This is a **very important exam distinction**.

| Term          | Meaning                         | Example        |
| ------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------- |
| **Phoneme**   | Smallest contrastive sound unit | /p/ in pin     |
| **Morpheme**  | Smallest meaningful unit        | cat + s        |
| **Allomorph** | Variant form of a morpheme      | /s/, /z/, /ɪz/ |

### 🧠 Memory Trick

```text
PHONEME → SOUND
MORPHEME → MEANING
ALLOMORPH → VARIANT FORM
```
