# 📖 UNIT–I: CHARACTERISTIC FEATURES OF HUMAN LANGUAGE

# 1. 🧩 DUALITY OF PATTERNING

### Definition

> **Duality of patterning means that human language is organized at two levels: a level of meaningless sounds and a level of meaningful units such as morphemes and words.**

In simple words:

> **Small meaningless sounds combine → meaningful words are created.**

---

## 🔹 Two Levels of Language

### LEVEL 1️⃣ — Pattern of Sounds 🔊

Individual speech sounds usually **do not carry meaning by themselves**.

For example:

**/k/ + /æ/ + /t/**

The individual sounds:

* /k/ ❌
* /æ/ ❌
* /t/ ❌

do not mean "cat" individually.

But when they are combined:

**/k/ + /æ/ + /t/ → CAT 🐈**

A meaningful word is produced.

---

### LEVEL 2️⃣ — Pattern of Morphemes and Words 🧩

Sounds combine to make **morphemes and words**, which carry meaning.

For example:

**un + happy**

```text
un → meaning "not"
happy → meaning "not sad/pleased"
        ↓
unhappy
```

Here, **un-** and **happy** are meaningful units.

Another example:

**teach + er**

```text
teach → a meaningful word/root
-er   → a person who performs an action
          ↓
       teacher
```

---

```text
          ↓                   ↓

          ↓                   ↓

          ↓
       COMBINE

```

**Sounds → Words → Meaning**

# ⭐ Why is Duality Important?

Duality makes language **economical and productive**.

Humans have a relatively limited number of speech sounds, but these sounds can be combined in many ways to create thousands of words and an enormous number of sentences.

### Example:

A small number of sounds:

```text
/k/ /æ/ /t/ /d/ /ɒ/ /g/
```

can be organized into different meaningful forms:

**cat 🐈**

**dog 🐕**

etc.

### 🔑 Remember:

> **Few sounds → Many words → Unlimited expressions**

# 2. 🎨 CREATIVITY

### Definition

> **Creativity is the ability of human beings to produce and understand an unlimited number of new and meaningful sentences.**

It is also called:

* **Productivity**
* **Generativity**

---

## 🤔 What does Creativity mean?

Imagine someone says:

> “The blue elephant is dancing on the roof.”

You may have **never heard this sentence before**.

Yet you can understand it.

Why?

Because human language allows us to combine words according to grammatical rules and create **new expressions**.

---

# 🌱 Example of Creativity

Start with:

> The girl sings.

Add information:

> The young girl sings.

Add more:

> The young girl sings beautifully.

Add even more:

> The young girl in the blue dress sings beautifully.

We can keep expanding.

```text
The girl sings.
       ↓
The young girl sings.
       ↓
The young girl sings beautifully.
       ↓
The young girl in the blue dress
sings beautifully.
       ↓
MORE + MORE + MORE...
```

There is no fixed list of sentences that humans can produce.

---

# 🧠 Creativity = Limited Elements + Rules = Unlimited Expressions

```text
       LIMITED SOUNDS
             +
       LIMITED WORDS
             +
          RULES
             ↓
     INFINITE POSSIBILITIES
             ↓
         CREATIVITY
```

---

## 🐕 Human Creativity vs Animal Communication

| Human Language 👨                  | Animal Communication 🐕 |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Highly productive                 | Generally limited      |
| Can create new sentences          | Restricted repertoire  |
| Can combine elements in many ways | Usually less flexible  |
| Can discuss imaginary situations  | Much more limited      |
| Can express abstract ideas        | More restricted        |

### ⭐ Exam Point

> **The creative nature of language allows human beings to generate an unlimited number of novel utterances from a finite set of linguistic elements and rules.**

# 3. ⏳ DISPLACEMENT

### Definition

> **Displacement is the ability of human language to refer to things, persons, places, events and situations that are absent from the immediate context.**

### Simple meaning:

> **We can talk about things that are NOT HERE and NOT NOW.**

---

## 📅 Examples

### Past

> “I visited Delhi last year.”

The visit happened in the past.

### Future

> “I will visit Delhi next month.”

The visit has not happened yet.

### Imaginary

> “A dragon lives on the moon.”

There may be no such dragon.

Yet language allows us to discuss it.

---

# 🧠 Displacement = Beyond Here and Now

```text
                    DISPLACEMENT
                         ↓
          ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
          ↓              ↓              ↓
         PAST          FUTURE        IMAGINARY
          ↓              ↓              ↓
      Yesterday       Tomorrow       Dragons
                                      🐉
```

# 🐕 Context-Bound Animal Communication

Many animal signals are strongly connected to the **immediate situation**.

For example:

🐒 A monkey may give an alarm call when a predator is nearby.

🐕 A dog may bark when it sees a stranger.

🐝 A bee may communicate information about the location of food.

These signals are often connected to the **present context**.

---

# 👨 Human Language = Context-Free / Less Context-Bound

Humans can communicate without the object or event being physically present.

Example:

> “Yesterday I saw a tiger in the forest.”

The tiger is not present.

We can still talk about it.

---

# ⭐ Context-Bound vs Context-Free

| Context-Bound Animal Communication 🐕          | Human Language 👨                                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Strongly connected to immediate situation     | Can refer beyond immediate situation               |
| Usually related to present circumstances      | Can refer to past and future                       |
| Limited displacement                          | Extensive displacement                             |
| Immediate context is often important          | Context can be absent                              |
| Signals often relate to particular situations | Language can discuss abstract and imaginary things |

### 🧠 Golden Line

> **Animal communication is often context-bound, whereas human language possesses a remarkable capacity for displacement.**

⚠️ **Exam caution:** It is better to say **“many animal communication systems are strongly context-bound”** rather than claiming that every animal communication system is completely context-bound.

# 4. 🛡️ REDUNDANCY

This is a slightly more difficult concept, but don't worry — it is actually easy once you see the examples. 😊

### Definition

> **Redundancy refers to the presence of extra or repeated information in language that is not strictly necessary for conveying the basic message but helps make communication clearer and more reliable.**

### Simple meaning:

> **Redundancy = Extra information that helps prevent misunderstanding.**

---

# 🔊 Example 1: Repetition

Someone says:

> “I saw him with my own eyes.”

The phrase **“with my own eyes”** is somewhat redundant because seeing already involves the eyes.

But it adds **emphasis**.

---

# 🔊 Example 2: Grammatical Information

Consider:

> **The boys are playing.**

The sentence contains several signals:

* **boys** → plural
* **are** → plural agreement
* **playing** → ongoing action

Several parts reinforce the same grammatical information.

---

# 🔊 Example 3: Everyday Speech

> “Please come back again.”

“Come back” already contains the idea of returning, while “again” repeats that idea.

But this can make the intended meaning stronger or clearer.

---

# 🛡️ Why is Redundancy Useful?

Imagine communication happening in a noisy room:

```text
SPEAKER
   ↓
Speech
   ↓
NOISE 🔊🔊🔊
   ↓
Some information is lost
   ↓
REDUNDANCY helps
   ↓
LISTENER understands
```

Redundancy can help the listener **recover the intended message** even when part of the signal is missed.

---

# ⭐ Functions of Redundancy

| Function                    | How it helps                                     |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 🛡️ Prevents misunderstanding | Gives additional clues                           |
| 👂 Helps in noisy situations | Missing information may be reconstructed         |
| 🔁 Reinforces meaning        | Repetition emphasizes information                |
| 🧠 Helps comprehension       | Provides additional grammatical/contextual clues |
| 📢 Improves communication    | Makes messages more robust                       |

### 🧠 Memory Trick

> **REDUNDANCY = EXTRA INFORMATION FOR SAFETY**

Think:

### 🛡️ **Redundancy = Communication Safety Net**

# 5. 🌍 CULTURE-PRESERVING AND CULTURE-TRANSMITTING FEATURES

This is an important connection between **language and culture**. ⭐⭐⭐

Language is not merely a tool for exchanging information.

It also carries:

* 🏛️ Traditions
* 📜 History
* 🎵 Songs
* 📖 Stories
* 🧠 Knowledge
* 👨‍👩‍👧 Customs
* 💭 Values
* 🎭 Beliefs
* 🌾 Practices

---

# 🌱 Language as a Carrier of Culture

Every society has knowledge that is passed from one generation to another.

For example:

👵 Grandmother tells a traditional story.

↓

👩 Mother learns it.

↓

👧 Child learns it.

↓

👶 Next generation remembers it.

Language makes this transmission possible.

---

# 📚 Culture-Preserving Feature

### Meaning

Language helps **preserve the cultural knowledge of a community**.

For example:

Traditional:

* stories 📖
* songs 🎵
* proverbs
* folk tales
* rituals
* historical memories
* traditional knowledge

can be preserved through language.

### Example

A proverb such as:

> “Where there is a will, there is a way.”

preserves and communicates a cultural idea about determination.

---

# 🔄 Culture-Transmitting Feature

Language also helps **transfer culture from one generation to another**.

```text
        OLDER GENERATION 👵
                 ↓
          Language
                 ↓
       Stories / Proverbs
                 ↓
         YOUNGER GENERATION 👧
                 ↓
          Cultural Knowledge
                 ↓
        NEXT GENERATION 👶
```

---

# 🌳 Culture + Language

```text
       PRESERVES CULTURE    TRANSMITS CULTURE
```

---

# ⭐ Culture-Preserving vs Culture-Transmitting

| Culture-Preserving 📚           | Culture-Transmitting 🔄              |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Keeps cultural knowledge alive | Passes knowledge to new generations |
| Preserves stories              | Teaches stories                     |
| Preserves traditions           | Teaches traditions                  |
| Preserves proverbs             | Passes values                       |
| Preserves historical memory    | Transfers knowledge                 |
| Maintains cultural identity    | Continues cultural identity         |

### 🧠 Easy Difference

> **Preserving = KEEPING**

> **Transmitting = PASSING ON**
