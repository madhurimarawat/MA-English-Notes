# 📚 UNIT–III — PHONETICS

---

# 🔊 1. WHAT IS PHONETICS?

### Definition

> **Phonetics is the scientific study of speech sounds, including how they are produced, transmitted and perceived.**

In simple words:

> 🗣️ **Phonetics = Study of human speech sounds**

It asks three main questions:

```text id="phonet1"
How is a sound PRODUCED?
          ↓
ARTICULATORY PHONETICS

How does a sound TRAVEL?
          ↓
ACOUSTIC PHONETICS

How is a sound HEARD?
          ↓
AUDITORY PHONETICS
```

---

# 🌳 THREE BRANCHES OF PHONETICS

```text id="phonet2"
                    PHONETICS
                       🔊
                       ↓
          ┌────────────┼────────────┐
          ↓            ↓            ↓
    ARTICULATORY     ACOUSTIC     AUDITORY
          ↓            ↓            ↓
      Production    Transmission   Perception
          ↓            ↓            ↓
      SPEAKER       SOUND WAVE    LISTENER
```

### 🧠 Easy Memory Trick

> **A – A – A**

**Articulatory = Action/Production**
**Acoustic = Air/Sound wave**
**Auditory = Audio/Hearing**

Or:

### 👄 **Produce → 🌊 Travel → 👂 Hear**

---

# 1.1 👄 ARTICULATORY PHONETICS

### Definition

> **Articulatory phonetics studies how speech sounds are produced by the movement and positioning of the speech organs.**

It studies:

* 👄 Lips
* 👅 Tongue
* 🦷 Teeth
* 🫁 Lungs
* 🗣️ Vocal cords
* 👃 Nasal cavity
* 👄 Oral cavity
* Soft palate

### Example

To produce **/p/**:

1. Lips come together.
2. Air pressure builds up.
3. Lips open.
4. Air is released.

Therefore, articulatory phonetics studies **how /p/ is physically produced**.

---

# 1.2 🌊 ACOUSTIC PHONETICS

### Definition

> **Acoustic phonetics studies the physical properties of speech sounds as sound waves.**

It examines:

* Frequency
* Amplitude
* Duration
* Intensity
* Sound waves

### Simple idea:

When we speak:

```text id="acoustic1"
SPEAKER 👄
    ↓
Speech sound
    ↓
Sound waves 🌊
    ↓
AIR
    ↓
LISTENER 👂
```

Acoustic phonetics studies the **sound wave travelling through the air**.

---

# 1.3 👂 AUDITORY PHONETICS

### Definition

> **Auditory phonetics studies how speech sounds are received and perceived by the listener's auditory system.**

It studies:

* Hearing
* Perception
* Recognition of speech sounds
* Differences between sounds

### Example:

You hear:

> **“ship” 🚢**

rather than:

> **“sheep” 🐑**

Your auditory system identifies the difference between the vowel sounds.

---

# ⭐ COMPARISON OF THREE BRANCHES

| Branch             | Main Question          | Focus              | Remember         |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------ | ---------------- |
| 👄 **Articulatory** | How is sound produced? | Speech organs      | **Production**   |
| 🌊 **Acoustic**     | What happens to sound? | Sound waves        | **Transmission** |
| 👂 **Auditory**     | How is sound heard?    | Hearing/perception | **Reception**    |

### 🔥 Golden Formula

> **ARTICULATORY = SPEAKER**
> **ACOUSTIC = SOUND WAVE**
> **AUDITORY = LISTENER**

# 🫁 2. THE ORGANS OF SPEECH

### Definition

> **The organs of speech are the parts of the human respiratory and vocal apparatus involved in the production of speech sounds.**

Speech requires a coordinated system involving:

```text id="organs1"
LUNGS 🫁
   ↓
Air
   ↓
TRACHEA
   ↓
LARYNX
   ↓
PHARYNX
   ↓
ORAL / NASAL CAVITY
   ↓
SPEECH SOUND 🗣️
```

---

# 🌳 MAJOR ORGANS OF SPEECH

## 1. 🫁 Lungs

The lungs provide the **air stream** required for speech.

### Function:

> They supply the air that powers most speech sounds.

Think:

### 🫁 **Lungs = Power source**

---

# 2. 🗣️ Larynx

The larynx is commonly called the **voice box**.

It contains the:

### 🎵 Vocal cords / vocal folds

The vocal folds are extremely important for **voicing**.

---

# 3. 🎵 Vocal Cords

When the vocal folds vibrate:

> **Voiced sound**

When they do not vibrate:

> **Voiceless sound**

### Examples:

**/b/** → voiced
**/p/** → voiceless

**/d/** → voiced
**/t/** → voiceless

---

# 4. 👄 Oral Cavity

The oral cavity is the **mouth space**.

It contains or involves:

* Tongue
* Teeth
* Alveolar ridge
* Hard palate
* Soft palate
* Lips

It is one of the main spaces where speech sounds are shaped.

---

# 5. 👃 Nasal Cavity

The nasal cavity is involved in producing **nasal sounds**.

Examples:

* /m/
* /n/
* /ŋ/

---

# 6. 👅 Tongue

The tongue is the **most important articulator** for many speech sounds.

Important parts include:

```text id="tongue1"
            TONGUE 👅
                ↓
      ┌─────────┼─────────┐
      ↓         ↓         ↓
    TIP       BLADE      BODY
                         ↓
                       BACK
```

The tongue changes position to produce different vowels and consonants.

---

# 7. 🦷 Teeth

The teeth help produce sounds such as:

**/θ/** → think
**/ð/** → this

---

# 8. 👄 Lips

The lips are important in sounds such as:

**/p/**
**/b/**
**/m/**

They also play an important role in vowel production.

---

# 9. 🦴 Alveolar Ridge

The alveolar ridge is the raised area just behind the upper front teeth.

Sounds articulated there include:

* /t/
* /d/
* /s/
* /z/
* /n/
* /l/

---

# 10. 🏠 Hard Palate

The hard palate is the hard, bony part of the roof of the mouth.

---

# 11. 🌙 Soft Palate / Velum

The soft palate is the soft part toward the back of the roof of the mouth.

It helps control whether air passes through:

* 👄 Mouth
* 👃 Nose

# 🔥 3. PLACES OF ARTICULATION

### Definition

> **Place of articulation refers to the location in the vocal tract where the airflow is obstructed or modified to produce a consonant sound.**

### Simple meaning:

> **Where in the mouth/throat is the sound made?**

---

# 🌳 IMPORTANT PLACES OF ARTICULATION

| Place               | Articulators                        | Examples           |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| 👄 **Bilabial**      | Two lips                            | /p, b, m/          |
| 🦷 **Labiodental**   | Lip + teeth                         | /f, v/             |
| 👅 **Dental**        | Tongue + teeth                      | /θ, ð/             |
| 👅 **Alveolar**      | Tongue + alveolar ridge             | /t, d, s, z, n, l/ |
| 👅 **Post-alveolar** | Tongue + area behind alveolar ridge | /ʃ, ʒ, tʃ, dʒ/     |
| 👅 **Palatal**       | Tongue + hard palate                | /j/                |
| 👅 **Velar**         | Tongue + soft palate                | /k, g, ŋ/          |
| 🗣️ **Glottal**       | Vocal folds/glottis                 | /h/                |

---

# 3.1 👄 BILABIAL

### Meaning:

**Bi = two**

**Labial = lips**

So:

> **Bilabial = two lips**

Examples:

**/p/** → pen
**/b/** → bat
**/m/** → man

```text id="bilab1"
Upper lip 👄
     ↓
    ↕
Lower lip 👄
     ↓
BILABIAL
```

---

# 3.2 🦷 LABIODENTAL

**Labio = lip**

**Dental = teeth**

One lip + teeth.

Examples:

**/f/** → fan
**/v/** → van

---

# 3.3 🦷 DENTAL

Tongue + teeth.

Examples:

**/θ/** → think

**/ð/** → this

---

# 3.4 👅 ALVEOLAR

Tongue touches or approaches the **alveolar ridge**.

Examples:

**/t, d, s, z, n, l/**

Memory:

> **T D S Z N L = ALVEOLAR**

---

# 3.5 👅 POST-ALVEOLAR

Produced just behind the alveolar ridge.

Examples:

**/ʃ/** → ship
**/ʒ/** → measure
**/tʃ/** → chair
**/dʒ/** → judge

---

# 3.6 👅 PALATAL

Tongue approaches the hard palate.

English example:

**/j/** → yes

---

# 3.7 👅 VELAR

Tongue + soft palate.

Examples:

**/k/** → cat
**/g/** → go
**/ŋ/** → sing

---

# 3.8 🗣️ GLOTTAL

Produced at the glottis/vocal-fold area.

Example:

**/h/** → hat

# 4. 🔊 VOWELS AND CONSONANTS

This is one of the **most important phonetics topics**. ⭐⭐⭐

---

# 🟢 VOWELS

### Definition

> **A vowel is a speech sound produced with relatively free passage of air through the vocal tract, without significant obstruction.**

Examples:

**/iː/** as in *see*
**/ɪ/** as in *sit*
**/æ/** as in *cat*
**/ʌ/** as in *cup*

---

# 🔴 CONSONANTS

### Definition

> **A consonant is a speech sound produced with complete or partial obstruction of the airflow in the vocal tract.**

Examples:

**/p/**
**/t/**
**/k/**
**/m/**
**/s/**

---

# ⭐ VOWEL vs CONSONANT

| Vowels 🟢                                     | Consonants 🔴                      |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Relatively free airflow                      | Airflow is obstructed or narrowed |
| Usually form the centre/nucleus of syllables | Usually occur around vowels       |
| Tongue/lips shape the sound                  | Articulators create obstruction   |
| Example: /iː/, /æ/                           | Example: /p/, /t/, /k/            |

---

# 🧩 CLASSIFICATION OF CONSONANTS

Consonants can be classified according to:

### 1. Place of articulation

**Where?**

### 2. Manner of articulation

**How?**

### 3. Voicing

**Are vocal folds vibrating?**

```text id="cons1"
CONSONANTS
     ↓
 ┌───┼────────┐
 ↓   ↓        ↓
PLACE MANNER  VOICING
 ↓     ↓       ↓
WHERE HOW?    VIBRATION?
```

---

# 🔊 VOICED vs VOICELESS

### Voiced

Vocal folds vibrate.

Examples:

**/b, d, g, v, z, ʒ/**

### Voiceless

Vocal folds do not vibrate.

Examples:

**/p, t, k, f, s, ʃ/**

---

# 🧠 EASY PAIRS

| Voiceless | Voiced |
| --------- | ------ |
| /p/       | /b/    |
| /t/       | /d/    |
| /k/       | /g/    |
| /f/       | /v/    |
| /θ/       | /ð/    |
| /s/       | /z/    |
| /ʃ/       | /ʒ/    |
| /tʃ/      | /dʒ/   |

### 🔥 Memory:

> **If the vocal folds vibrate → VOICED.**

# 5. 🌈 DIPHTHONGS

### Definition

> **A diphthong is a vowel sound in which the tongue moves from one vowel position toward another within the same syllable.**

### Simple meaning:

> **Diphthong = two vowel qualities gliding together in one syllable.**

Think:

### 🛝 **One vowel position → glide → another vowel position**

---

# 🔊 Examples in English

### /eɪ/

**day**

```text id="diph1"
/e/ → /ɪ/
  \____/
   glide
```

### /aɪ/

**my**

### /ɔɪ/

**boy**

### /aʊ/

**now**

### /əʊ/ or /oʊ/

**go**

### /ɪə/

**near** *(in varieties of English where this distinction is maintained)*

### /eə/

**hair** *(in varieties where this is a diphthong)*

### /ʊə/

**tour** *(variety-dependent)*

---

# 6. 🧱 CLUSTERS

### Definition

> **A consonant cluster is a sequence of two or more consonant sounds occurring together without an intervening vowel sound.**

### Examples:

**play** → /pl/

**street** → /str/

**spring** → /spr/

**texts** → /ksts/ in a phonetic analysis of the final sequence

---

# 🌳 TYPES OF CLUSTERS

### Initial Cluster

At the beginning of a syllable/word.

Examples:

**play** /pl/
**tree** /tr/
**school** /sk/

---

### Final Cluster

At the end.

Examples:

**desk** /sk/
**help** /lp/
**asked** /skt/ (depending on pronunciation)

---

### Medial Cluster

Within a word.

Example:

**extra**
**doctor**

---

# ⭐ Cluster vs Single Consonant

> **Cluster = two or more consonants together**

```text id="cluster1"
C
↓
single consonant

CC
↓
two-consonant cluster

CCC
↓
three-consonant cluster
```

# 7. 🔤 SYLLABLES

### Definition

> **A syllable is a unit of spoken language organized around a vowel or syllabic sound.**

In simple words:

> **A syllable is a beat or unit of pronunciation within a word.**

---

## Examples

### One syllable

**cat** 🐈

### Two syllables

**teacher**

**teach – er**

### Three syllables

**beautiful**

**beau – ti – ful**

---

# 🧩 STRUCTURE OF A SYLLABLE

A syllable can contain:

### Onset + Nucleus + Coda

```text id="syll1"
           SYLLABLE
              ↓
      ┌───────┼───────┐
      ↓       ↓       ↓
    ONSET   NUCLEUS  CODA
      ↓       ↓       ↓
 consonants  vowel  consonants
```

### Example:

**cat**

/k æ t/

```text id="syll2"
/k/     /æ/     /t/
 ↓       ↓       ↓
Onset  Nucleus  Coda
```

### ⭐ Remember:

> **Onset = beginning**

> **Nucleus = centre**

> **Coda = ending**

# 🔥 SYLLABLE MEMORY TRICK

# What does “Suprasegmental” mean?

### Definition

> **Suprasegmental features are features of speech that extend over individual speech sounds or segments, such as stress, pitch, intonation and duration.**

The word:

### **SUPRA = ABOVE**

So:

> **Suprasegmental = features operating over or across individual sounds.**

# 8.1 💪 STRESS

### Definition

> **Stress is the relative prominence given to a syllable or word in speech.**

A stressed syllable is usually:

* Louder 🔊
* Longer ⏱️
* More prominent
* Often associated with a change in pitch 🎵
* Sometimes has a clearer vowel quality

---

## Example

### `REcord` 📖 — noun

### `reCORD` 📝 — verb

The position of stress changes the meaning or grammatical category.

```text id="stress1"
REcord → noun
reCORD → verb
```

---

# 🔥 Word Stress

Stress within a word.

Example:

**TAble**

**beGIN**

---

# 🔥 Sentence Stress

Stress on important words in a sentence.

Example:

> **I BOUGHT a NEW BOOK.**

The stressed words carry important information.

---

# 🧠 Stress Memory

> **STRESS = PROMINENCE**

Think:

### 💪 **One syllable stands out.**

---

# 8.2 🎵 PITCH

### Definition

> **Pitch is the perceived height or lowness of a person's voice, primarily related to the frequency of vocal-fold vibration.**

Simple:

> **Pitch = How high or low the voice sounds.**

### High pitch:

🎵 “Really?”

### Low pitch:

🎵 “Really.”

---

# 📈 Pitch and Frequency

```text id="pitch1"
Higher frequency
       ↓
Higher pitch 🎵

Lower frequency
       ↓
Lower pitch 🎵
```

### Important:

> **Pitch is perceptual; frequency is its main physical correlate.**

---

# 8.3 🎶 INTONATION

### Definition

> **Intonation is the pattern of pitch movement across a phrase or sentence.**

In simple words:

> **Intonation = Rise and fall of the voice while speaking.**

---

# 📈 Rising Intonation

Voice rises toward the end.

Often associated with:

### Yes/No questions

> “Are you coming? ↗️”

---

# 📉 Falling Intonation

Voice falls toward the end.

Often associated with:

### Statements

> “I am coming. ↘️”

And many WH-questions:

> “Where are you going? ↘️”

⚠️ Intonation patterns depend on context, speaker intention and variety of English, so these are useful general patterns rather than absolute rules.

---

# 🧠 PITCH vs INTONATION

| Pitch 🎵                                  | Intonation 🎶                |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Height/lowness of voice                  | Pattern of pitch movement   |
| Can occur on a particular sound/syllable | Extends across an utterance |
| Related to frequency                     | Involves changes in pitch   |
| High/low                                 | Rising/falling patterns     |

### Easy memory:

> **PITCH = HIGH / LOW**

> **INTONATION = RISE / FALL PATTERN**

---

# 8.4 ✂️ JUNCTURE

### Definition

> **Juncture refers to the boundaries or transitions between sounds, syllables, words or larger units in speech.**

Simple:

> **Juncture = how we divide or connect speech units.**

---

## Example

Consider:

> **ice cream**

versus

> **I scream**

The sequence of sounds can be similar, but the **boundary/segmentation** differs.

```text id="junct1"
ICE CREAM
   ↓
word boundary

I SCREAM
   ↓
different word boundary
```

Juncture helps listeners understand **where one linguistic unit ends and another begins**.

---

# ⭐ Why is Juncture Important?

It can help distinguish meaning by signalling boundaries.

### Think:

> ✂️ **Juncture = boundary**

---

# 8.5 🎼 RHYME

### Definition

> **Rhyme is the repetition or similarity of sound, especially at the ends of words or syllables, often involving the stressed vowel and following sounds.**

Examples:

**cat — bat**

**light — night**

**day — say**

---

# 🎵 Rhyme in Poetry

```text id="rhyme1"
Twinkle, twinkle, little STAR ⭐
How I wonder what you ARE.
```

**star — are**

These words rhyme.

---

# 🧠 Why is Rhyme Important?

Rhyme contributes to:

* Rhythm 🎵
* Memorability 🧠
* Poetry 📖
* Songs 🎶
* Sound patterning

# 📝 EXAM-READY DEFINITIONS

### 🔊 Phonetics

> **Phonetics is the scientific study of speech sounds, including their production, transmission and perception.**

### 👄 Articulatory Phonetics

> **Articulatory phonetics studies how speech sounds are produced by the movement and positioning of the speech organs.**

### 🌊 Acoustic Phonetics

> **Acoustic phonetics studies the physical properties of speech sounds as sound waves.**

### 👂 Auditory Phonetics

> **Auditory phonetics studies the perception and reception of speech sounds by the listener.**

### 📍 Place of Articulation

> **Place of articulation refers to the location in the vocal tract where the airflow is obstructed or modified to produce a speech sound.**

### 🟢 Vowel

> **A vowel is a speech sound produced with relatively free passage of air through the vocal tract.**

### 🔴 Consonant

> **A consonant is a speech sound produced with complete or partial obstruction of the airflow.**

### 🌈 Diphthong

> **A diphthong is a vowel sound involving a glide from one vowel quality toward another within a single syllable.**

### 🧱 Consonant Cluster

> **A consonant cluster is a sequence of two or more consonant sounds occurring together without an intervening vowel sound.**

### 🔤 Syllable

> **A syllable is a unit of spoken language organized around a vowel or syllabic sound.**

### 💪 Stress

> **Stress is the relative prominence given to a syllable or word in speech.**

### 🎵 Pitch

> **Pitch is the perceived height or lowness of the voice, primarily associated with the frequency of vocal-fold vibration.**

### 🎶 Intonation

> **Intonation is the pattern of pitch movement across a phrase or sentence.**

### ✂️ Juncture

> **Juncture refers to the boundaries or transitions between linguistic units in connected speech.**

### 🎼 Rhyme

> **Rhyme is the repetition or similarity of sound, especially at the ends of words or syllables.**

### 🌟 The whole unit in one sentence:

> **Phonetics studies speech sounds through their production, physical transmission and perception; these sounds are produced by the organs of speech at different places of articulation and are organized into vowels, consonants, diphthongs, clusters and syllables, while suprasegmental features such as stress, pitch, intonation, juncture and rhyme add important patterns to spoken language.**
