# 📘 UNIT–V — SYNTAX, WORD CLASSES & PHRASE STRUCTURE

This unit is especially important because it introduces **Syntax**, **Immediate Constituent (IC) Analysis**, and **Phrase Structure Grammar**, which are fundamental for understanding how sentences are constructed.

---

# 🔵 1. SYNTAX

## 📖 Definition

> **Syntax is the branch of linguistics that studies the rules and principles governing the arrangement of words into phrases, clauses and sentences.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

Syntax studies:

👉 **How words are arranged to form meaningful sentences.**

Compare:

✅ **The boy opened the door.**

❌ **Door the boy opened the.**

The same words are present, but their arrangement is different.

Therefore, syntax is concerned with **sentence structure and word order**.

---

## 🌟 Example

Consider:

> **The little girl opened the window.**

Syntax tells us how the words are organized:

```text id="x1syntax"
The + little + girl
        ↓
      Noun Phrase

opened
   ↓
  Verb

the + window
      ↓
   Noun Phrase
```

So:

```text id="syntaxflow"
WORDS
  ↓
PHRASES
  ↓
CLAUSES
  ↓
SENTENCES
  ↓
MEANING
```

---

# ⭐ What Does Syntax Study?

Syntax studies:

1. Word order
2. Sentence structure
3. Phrases
4. Clauses
5. Grammatical relationships
6. Subject and predicate
7. Agreement
8. Grammatical rules
9. Sentence patterns
10. Constituent structure

---

# 🔥 Example of Syntax

Consider:

> **The cat chased the mouse.**

The structure is:

```text id="syntaxcat"
The cat       chased       the mouse
   ↓             ↓             ↓
Subject        Verb        Object
   ↓             ↓             ↓
   NP            V             NP
```

Therefore:

> **Sentence = NP + VP**

---

# 🧠 Memory Trick

### **SYNTAX = SENTENCE STRUCTURE**

Remember:

> **Syntax asks: “How are words put together?”**

# 🟣 2. WORD CLASSES

## 📖 Definition

> **Word classes are groups of words classified according to their grammatical function, form and distribution in a sentence.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

Words belong to different grammatical categories.

For example:

> **The clever boy quickly opened the door.**

Here:

* **the** → determiner
* **clever** → adjective
* **boy** → noun
* **quickly** → adverb
* **opened** → verb
* **the** → determiner
* **door** → noun

---

# ⭐ Major Word Classes

There are two broad categories:

```text id="wordclasses"
                WORD CLASSES
                     ↓
          ┌──────────┴──────────┐
          ↓                     ↓
     OPEN CLASSES          CLOSED CLASSES
          ↓                     ↓
  Noun, Verb, Adjective    Determiner,
  Adverb                   Pronoun,
                           Preposition,
                           Conjunction,
                           Auxiliary etc.
```

---

# 🟢 A. OPEN WORD CLASSES

They are called **open** because new words can easily be added.

## 1. Noun

### Definition

> A noun is a word that typically names a person, place, thing, animal, idea or concept.

Examples:

* teacher
* Delhi
* book
* dog
* freedom

### Example

> **The teacher** entered the room.

---

## 2. Verb

### Definition

> A verb expresses an action, event, process or state.

Examples:

* run
* eat
* write
* sleep
* know
* become

Example:

> She **writes** every day.

---

## 3. Adjective

### Definition

> An adjective typically describes or modifies a noun.

Examples:

* beautiful
* tall
* intelligent
* red
* difficult

Example:

> She is a **beautiful** girl.

---

## 4. Adverb

### Definition

> An adverb typically modifies a verb, adjective, another adverb or sometimes an entire clause.

Examples:

* quickly
* slowly
* very
* carefully
* yesterday

Example:

> He ran **quickly**.

---

# 🔵 B. CLOSED WORD CLASSES

These classes have a relatively fixed membership and do not normally accept new words easily.

## 1. Pronouns

Examples:

* I
* you
* he
* she
* they
* it

> **She** is reading.

---

## 2. Determiners

Examples:

* the
* a
* an
* this
* that
* some
* my

> **The** book is interesting.

---

## 3. Prepositions

Examples:

* in
* on
* under
* beside
* with
* from
* to

> The book is **on** the table.

---

## 4. Conjunctions

They connect words, phrases or clauses.

Examples:

* and
* but
* or
* because
* although

> I stayed home **because** it was raining.

# 🟠 3. IMMEDIATE CONSTITUENT (IC) ANALYSIS

This is one of the **most important topics of Unit-V**. ⭐⭐⭐

## 📖 Definition

> **Immediate Constituent Analysis is a method of analyzing a sentence by dividing it into its immediate constituent parts and then continuing to divide those parts into smaller constituents.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

A sentence is not just a string of separate words.

It is made up of **groups of words**, and those groups can be further divided.

---

# 🔥 What is a Constituent?

A **constituent** is a word or group of words that functions as a unit within a larger structure.

Consider:

> **The little boy opened the door.**

We can divide it as:

```text id="icbasic"
The little boy | opened the door
       ↓                ↓
      NP                VP
```

Then:

```text id="icbasic2"
The little boy
      ↓
   The | little boy
           ↓
       little | boy
```

And:

```text id="icbasic3"
opened the door
      ↓
 opened | the door
            ↓
          the | door
```

These are the **immediate constituents** at each stage.

---

# 🌳 IC Analysis Example

Sentence:

> **The boy opened the door.**

### Step 1

```text id="icstep1"
The boy | opened the door
   NP   |       VP
```

### Step 2

```text id="icstep2"
The | boy       opened | the door
 D  |  N          V    |    NP
```

### Step 3

```text id="icstep3"
                    S
                  /   \
                NP     VP
               / \     / \
              D   N   V   NP
              |   |   |   / \
             The boy opened D  N
                            |  |
                           the door
```

This is an **IC structure**.

---

# ⭐ Why is it called "Immediate"?

Because at each stage we identify the **nearest / immediate parts** of a larger constituent.

For example:

> **The boy**

Its immediate constituents are:

* **The**
* **boy**

Not individual sounds.

---

# 🧠 Important Principle

```text id="icprinciple"
SENTENCE
   ↓
IMMEDIATE CONSTITUENTS
   ↓
SMALLER CONSTITUENTS
   ↓
WORDS
```

---

# 🟡 4. MODELS OF IC ANALYSIS

IC analysis has been represented in different ways.

The most useful models for examination are:

1. **Bracketing**
2. **Tree Diagram**
3. **Labeled Bracketing / Labeled Tree**

---

# ① Bracketing

In this model, constituents are shown using brackets.

Sentence:

> **The boy opened the door.**

Can be represented as:

```text id="bracket1"
[The boy] [opened [the door]]
```

More formally:

```text id="bracket2"
[S [NP The boy] [VP opened [NP the door]]]
```

### 🧠 Advantage

Brackets clearly show **which words belong together**.

---

# ② Tree Diagram

A tree diagram represents the hierarchical structure visually.

```text id="tree1"
                 S
               /   \
             NP     VP
            / \     / \
           D   N   V   NP
           |   |   |   / \
          The boy opened D   N
                       |   |
                      the door
```

### ⭐ Important

A tree diagram shows:

* hierarchy
* constituents
* relationships
* sentence structure

---

# ③ Labeled Bracketing

Here, the constituents are shown with grammatical labels.

Example:

```text id="labelbracket"
[S
   [NP [D The] [N boy]]
   [VP [V opened]
       [NP [D the] [N door]]
   ]
]
```

This tells us not only **where the boundaries are**, but also **what each constituent is**.

---

# 🌟 Models — Comparison

| Model                  | How it represents structure        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Bracketing**         | Uses brackets                      |
| **Tree Diagram**       | Uses branches/nodes                |
| **Labeled Bracketing** | Uses brackets + grammatical labels |

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**B–T–L**

> **Brackets → Tree → Labels**

# 🟢 5. INTRODUCTION TO PHRASE STRUCTURE GRAMMAR

## 📖 Definition

> **Phrase Structure Grammar is a grammatical theory that describes how words combine to form phrases and how phrases combine to form larger syntactic structures.**

### 🧠 Simple Meaning

Phrase Structure Grammar explains:

> **How smaller units combine to make larger units.**

```text id="psgflow"
WORDS
  ↓
PHRASES
  ↓
CLAUSES
  ↓
SENTENCES
```

---

# ⭐ Basic Idea

A sentence can be divided into major constituents.

For example:

> **The girl read a book.**

The sentence can be represented as:

```text id="psg1"
S
├── NP
│   ├── Det → The
│   └── N   → girl
│
└── VP
    ├── V  → read
    └── NP
        ├── Det → a
        └── N   → book
```

Therefore:

> **S → NP + VP**

This is called a **phrase structure rule**.

---

# 🔥 Common Phrase Structure Rules

### 1. Sentence

```text id="rule1"
S → NP VP
```

Meaning:

> A sentence consists of a Noun Phrase + Verb Phrase.

---

### 2. Noun Phrase

```text id="rule2"
NP → Det N
```

Example:

> **the boy**

---

### 3. Verb Phrase

```text id="rule3"
VP → V NP
```

Example:

> **opened the door**

---

### 4. Adjective Phrase

```text id="rule4"
AP → Adv A
```

Example:

> **very beautiful**

---

### 5. Prepositional Phrase

```text id="rule5"
PP → P NP
```

Example:

> **in the room**

---

# 🌳 Complete Example

Sentence:

> **The small boy opened the door.**

Phrase structure:

```text id="psgtree"
                         S
                       /   \
                     NP     VP
                   /  \    /  \
                 Det   N  V    NP
                  |   / \ |   /  \
                 The small boy opened Det N
                                      |   |
                                      the door
```

A simplified phrase structure is:

```text id="psgsimple"
S
↓
NP + VP
↓
Det + Adj + N + V + NP
↓
The + small + boy + opened + the door
```

---

# 🔵 Constituency and Phrase Structure

One of the central ideas of Phrase Structure Grammar is **constituency**.

### Example:

> **The intelligent student**

This group behaves as a unit.

We can replace it with:

> **She**

So:

> The intelligent student passed.

→ **She** passed.

This shows that **The intelligent student** functions as a constituent.

---

# 🧠 Constituency Test: Substitution

A group of words can often be tested by replacing it with a pronoun or another constituent.

Example:

> **The boy in the blue shirt** is sleeping.

Replace the entire group:

> **He** is sleeping.

This suggests that:

> **The boy in the blue shirt**

is functioning as an NP.

# 🟣 6. LIMITATIONS OF PHRASE STRUCTURE GRAMMAR

This is another **important long-answer topic**. ⭐⭐⭐

Phrase Structure Grammar is useful, but it has limitations.

---

## ❌ 1. It Can Become Too Rigid

Phrase Structure Grammar often represents sentences using fixed structural rules.

But natural languages can have:

* variation
* exceptions
* alternative word orders
* complex constructions

Therefore, simple rules may not capture everything.

---

## ❌ 2. Difficulty with Transformations

Some sentences have related structures even though their surface forms are different.

Compare:

> **The boy opened the door.**

and

> **The door was opened by the boy.**

Their surface structures are different, but they express closely related meanings.

A purely phrase-based description may not fully explain this relationship.

---

## ❌ 3. Long-Distance Relationships

Some grammatical relationships extend over long distances.

Example:

> **The book that the student bought was expensive.**

The relationship between:

> **book**

and

> **was**

is not immediately obvious from simple adjacent word grouping.

---

## ❌ 4. Ambiguous Sentences

Some sentences can have more than one structural interpretation.

Classic example:

> **I saw the man with a telescope.**

Two possible meanings:

### Meaning 1

I used a telescope to see the man.

### Meaning 2

I saw a man who had a telescope.

The same words can therefore have different structures.

```text id="ambiguity"
SAME WORDS
    ↓
DIFFERENT STRUCTURES
    ↓
DIFFERENT INTERPRETATIONS
```

---

## ❌ 5. Meaning Is Not Fully Explained

Phrase Structure Grammar primarily focuses on **syntactic structure**.

But language also involves:

* meaning
* context
* speaker intention
* discourse

Therefore, syntax alone cannot explain every aspect of language.

---

## ❌ 6. Coordination Can Be Difficult

Consider:

> **John likes tea and Mary likes coffee.**

Coordination may involve larger and more complex structures.

Simple phrase rules can sometimes struggle to represent such structures elegantly.

---

## ❌ 7. Does Not Fully Explain Context

Compare:

> **Can you open the window?**

Syntactically, this looks like a question about ability.

But pragmatically, it is often a **request**.

Phrase Structure Grammar alone does not fully explain this communicative meaning.

---

# ⭐ Limitations — Quick Table

| Limitation                  | Problem                                           |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rigidity**                | Natural language is flexible                      |
| **Transformations**         | Related structures may look different             |
| **Long-distance relations** | Some relationships are not local                  |
| **Ambiguity**               | Same words can have different structures          |
| **Meaning**                 | Syntax alone cannot explain meaning               |
| **Coordination**            | Complex coordination can be difficult             |
| **Context**                 | Social/pragmatic meaning is not fully represented |

---

# 🔥 IC ANALYSIS vs PHRASE STRUCTURE GRAMMAR

These two topics can look similar, so remember the distinction.

| IC Analysis                                    | Phrase Structure Grammar                 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| A method of analyzing constituents             | A grammatical framework/theory           |
| Divides structures into immediate constituents | Uses rules to generate/build structures  |
| Focuses on constituent divisions               | Focuses on phrase formation              |
| Often uses brackets/tree diagrams              | Uses phrase structure rules              |
| Example: S → NP + VP as an analysis            | Example: S → NP VP as a grammatical rule |

### 🧠 Simple Memory

> **IC = BREAK IT DOWN** 🔪
> **PSG = BUILD IT UP** 🧱
