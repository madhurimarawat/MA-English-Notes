# 📚 CULTURAL STUDIES

## 🌟 First: What is Cultural Studies?

Very simply:

> **Cultural Studies = the study of culture as a site of power, ideology, identity, resistance, and everyday life.**

It does **not** study only “great literature.”

It studies things like:

📚 Literature
🎬 Films
📺 Television
🎵 Music
📰 Newspapers
📱 Social media
👗 Fashion
⚽ Sports
🍔 Popular culture
🏫 Education
🌍 Race
👩 Gender
🏳️‍🌈 Sexuality
💰 Class
🏛️ Politics
🛍️ Consumer culture

### The BIG question:

> **Who has the power to decide what culture means?**

---

# 🧠 THE GOLDEN CULTURAL STUDIES CHAIN

Memorize this first:

> **MARX → FRANKFURT SCHOOL → WILLIAMS → HOGGART → BIRMINGHAM → HALL → HEGEMONY → IDEOLOGY → REPRESENTATION → IDENTITY → RACE/GENDER/CLASS → GLOBALIZATION**

🔥 If you understand this chain, the whole topic becomes much easier.

# ⭐ HEGEMONY

This is one of the most important words in Cultural Studies.

### Simple meaning:

> **Hegemony = dominant groups maintain power partly by making their ideas appear natural, normal, and common-sense.**

Not just:

❌ FORCE

But:

✅ CONSENT

---

## 🧠 Example

Suppose society repeatedly tells people:

> "Success means becoming rich."

People begin to accept this as normal.

They don't necessarily need someone to force them.

The idea becomes:

### "COMMON SENSE."

🔥 That is the kind of process Gramsci helps us understand.

---

# 1️⃣3️⃣ ENCODING / DECODING

One of Stuart Hall's most famous models.

### 📅 1973

Essay:

# 📖 "Encoding and Decoding in the Television Discourse"

---

# 🧠 SIMPLE EXPLANATION

A media producer creates a message.

### STEP 1

📺 Producer

**ENCODES**

meaning into a media text.

Then:

### STEP 2

👀 Audience

**DECODES**

the message.

But audiences don't necessarily interpret it exactly as the producer intended.

---

# ⭐ THREE READING POSITIONS

Hall identifies three major positions:

### 1️⃣ Dominant-Hegemonic Reading

Audience accepts the preferred meaning.

👍

### 2️⃣ Negotiated Reading

Audience partly accepts and partly modifies the meaning.

🤝

### 3️⃣ Oppositional Reading

Audience understands the intended meaning but rejects it.

✊

---

# 🧠 Example

Advertisement says:

> "Buying this expensive product = success."

### Dominant:

"Yes! This means success."

### Negotiated:

"It may help, but money isn't everything."

### Oppositional:

"This advertisement is manipulating consumers."

🔥 That's Hall's model.

---

# 🧠 MEMORY

### HALL =

> **ENCODE → DECODE**

And:

> **DOMINANT → NEGOTIATED → OPPOSITIONAL**

---

# 5️⃣0️⃣ CULTURAL STUDIES vs LITERARY THEORY

Think of it this way:

### Literary theory asks:

> How do we interpret texts?

### Cultural Studies asks:

> How do texts and cultural practices operate within society, history, ideology and power?

# 🔥 CULTURAL STUDIES IN ONE STORY

Imagine society as a huge cultural system.

### MARX 💰

asks:

> Who owns the means of production?

⬇️

### GRAMSCI 🧠

asks:

> How does domination become common sense?

⬇️

### FRANKFURT SCHOOL 📺

asks:

> What happens when culture becomes an industry?

⬇️

### HOGGART 👷

asks:

> What does working-class everyday culture look like?

⬇️

### WILLIAMS 🏠

says:

> Culture is ordinary.

⬇️

### BIRMINGHAM 🏛️

studies:

> everyday culture + youth + media + class + race

⬇️

### HALL 📺

asks:

> How are messages encoded and decoded?

⬇️

### BARTHES 📰

asks:

> How do everyday things become ideological myths?

⬇️

### FOUCAULT 🧠

asks:

> How do power and knowledge produce "truth"?

⬇️

### MULVEY 👁️

asks:

> How does cinema construct the male gaze?

⬇️

### SAID 🌍

asks:

> How does Western culture represent the East?

⬇️

### BHABHA 🌀

asks:

> What happens when cultures meet and mix?

⬇️

### GILROY 🌊

asks:

> How does diaspora create transnational Black culture?

⬇️

### DIGITAL CULTURAL STUDIES 📱

asks:

> Who controls meaning in the age of algorithms, platforms and social media?

## 🏆 FINAL EXAM FORMULA

**Culture is not just art.**

**Culture = meaning + everyday life + media + identity + ideology + power + resistance.**

And the central Cultural Studies question is:

> **"Who produces meaning, who controls it, who benefits from it, who is excluded by it, and how can people resist or reinterpret it?"** 🔥
