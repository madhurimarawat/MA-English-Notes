# Awards of Indian English Writers: UGC NET English Literature Master List

One important distinction: the Nobel Prize in Literature, Booker Prize, International Booker Prize, Pulitzer Prize, Sahitya Akademi Award, Jnanpith Award, JCB Prize, and DSC Prize are different awards. Also, winner, shortlisted, and longlisted are not interchangeable.

## 1. Booker Prize: Indian and Indian-origin writers

The Booker Prize is for fiction written in English and published in the UK or Ireland under the prize’s eligibility rules. These are the most important Indian-related winners and shortlisted works for exam preparation.

| Year | Writer              | Work (creation)                   | Award result                                                       |
| ---- | ------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1971 | V. S. Naipaul       | In a Free State                   | Booker winner                                                      |
| 1981 | Salman Rushdie      | Midnight’s Children               | Booker winner                                                      |
| 1993 | Rohinton Mistry     | A Fine Balance                    | Booker shortlisted                                                 |
| 1997 | Arundhati Roy       | The God of Small Things           | Booker winner                                                      |
| 2001 | Rohinton Mistry     | Family Matters                    | Booker shortlisted                                                 |
| 2006 | Kiran Desai         | The Inheritance of Loss           | Booker winner                                                      |
| 2008 | Aravind Adiga       | The White Tiger                   | Booker winner                                                      |
| 2012 | Jeet Thayil         | Narcopolis                        | Booker shortlisted                                                 |
| 2014 | Neel Mukherjee      | The Lives of Others               | Booker shortlisted                                                 |
| 2015 | Anuradha Roy        | Sleeping on Jupiter               | Booker longlisted                                                  |
| 2022 | Shehan Karunatilaka | The Seven Moons of Maali Almeida  | Booker winner; Sri Lankan writer, included for South Asian context |
| 2025 | Kiran Desai         | The Loneliness of Sonia and Sunny | Booker shortlisted                                                 |

Important distinction: Naipaul was born in Trinidad and Tobago and was of Indian ancestry. Rohinton Mistry was born in India and later settled in Canada. Kiran Desai is an Indian-born author who has lived abroad. These distinctions can matter in questions about nationality, diaspora, and postcolonial literature.

## 2. International Booker Prize

This prize recognises fiction translated into English, with the author and translator sharing the award.

| Year | Work                       | Original author        | English translator   | Result                              |
| ---- | -------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 2015 | The Vegetarian             | Han Kang (South Korea) | Deborah Smith        | Winner; useful for world literature |
| 2022 | Tomb of Sand (Ret Samadhi) | Geetanjali Shree       | Daisy Rockwell       | Winner                              |
| 2023 | Pyre (Pookkuzhi)           | Perumal Murugan        | Aniruddhan Vasudevan | Longlisted                          |
| 2025 | Heart Lamp                 | Banu Mushtaq           | Deepa Bhasthi        | Winner                              |

The 2022 win for Tomb of Sand was a landmark for an Indian novel originally written in Hindi. In 2025, Heart Lamp became the first Kannada-language work to win the International Booker Prize.

## 3. Pulitzer Prize: Indian-origin and diaspora writers

The Pulitzer Prize is American. It is not an award restricted to Indian writers. For Indian English literature, the key name to remember is Jhumpa Lahiri.

| Year | Writer               | Work                            | Award                                                               |
| ---- | -------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2000 | Jhumpa Lahiri        | Interpreter of Maladies         | Pulitzer Prize for Fiction                                          |
| 2003 | Kiran Desai          | Hullabaloo in the Guava Orchard | Betty Trask Award, not Pulitzer                                     |
| 2013 | Siddhartha Mukherjee | The Emperor of All Maladies     | Pulitzer Prize for General Nonfiction; medical history, not fiction |

For exam purposes, remember that Interpreter of Maladies is a short-story collection, while The Emperor of All Maladies is nonfiction.

## 4. Nobel Prize in Literature

| Year | Writer              | Work or contribution            | Exam point                                                     |
| ---- | ------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1913 | Rabindranath Tagore | Gitanjali and his literary work | First Asian Nobel laureate in Literature                       |
| 2001 | V. S. Naipaul       | Body of literary work           | Nobel laureate of Indian ancestry; born in Trinidad and Tobago |

Remember: Tagore's Nobel was awarded for his body of work, with the English Gitanjali especially associated with the award. Naipaul was a Trinidadian-born British writer of Indian descent, not an Indian citizen when awarded the Nobel.

## 9. Indian English drama and playwrights

International prizes such as the Booker and Pulitzer generally do not recognise plays in the same way they recognise novels or collections. For drama, UGC NET often tests the playwright, the play, its language, and its literary or theatrical importance.

### Drama-related awards and distinctions

* Sangeet Natak Akademi Award: A major national recognition for performing artists, including theatre practitioners and playwrights.

* Sahitya Akademi Award: Can recognise literary works in drama, as well as poetry, fiction, and criticism. The award is language-specific.

* Jnanpith Award: A major Indian literary honour, generally awarded for outstanding contribution to literature in recognised Indian languages.

* Pulitzer Prize for Drama: Primarily an American theatre award, not a standard award for Indian English dramatists.
