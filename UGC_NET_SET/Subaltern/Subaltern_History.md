# 🧠 SUBALTERN THEORY — FROM BEGINNING TO PRESENT

## 🌟 First: What does “Subaltern” mean?

**Subaltern = people who are socially, politically, economically, or culturally pushed to the margins and have little access to institutional power.**

Very simple:

> **Subaltern = the people below the dominant power structure.**

Examples can include:

* colonized people 🌍
* peasants 🚜
* workers 👷
* lower castes
* indigenous communities
* marginalized women
* racial minorities
* people excluded from political representation

⚠️ **Important NET point:**
Subaltern does **not simply mean “poor people.”** The concept is about **power, exclusion, representation, and access to political/social institutions**.

# 1️⃣ ANTONIO GRAMSCI — THE STARTING POINT

### 👤 Antonio Gramsci

🇮🇹 Italian Marxist thinker

**Born:** 1891
**Died:** 1937

He is the most important starting point for the modern theoretical use of **subaltern**.

---

## 📅 When did Gramsci develop the concept?

Mainly during his imprisonment under Fascist Italy.

He wrote his famous:

### 📖 *Prison Notebooks*

Written mainly **1929–1935**
Published posthumously in Italian in the years after WWII.

Because of prison censorship, Gramsci often used terms such as **“subaltern”** in place of more politically dangerous Marxist terminology.

---

# 🧠 What did Gramsci mean?

For Gramsci, **subaltern groups are subordinate groups that lack political power and are dominated by ruling groups.**

Think:

### RULING GROUP

⬇️
controls institutions / culture / political power

### SUBALTERN GROUP

⬇️
excluded from meaningful political power

---

# 🔥 KEY GRAMSCI CONCEPT: HEGEMONY

You MUST connect:

> **GRAMSCI → HEGEMONY → SUBALTERN**

### What is hegemony?

**Hegemony = domination maintained not only through force, but also through consent.**

Very simple:

Suppose a powerful group tells society:

> “This is the natural order of things.”

People gradually accept it as normal.

That's **hegemony**.

### Example:

If society repeatedly teaches:

> “Rich people naturally deserve to rule.”

and people accept this without questioning it,

👉 ideology is helping maintain domination.

---

# 🧠 Easy formula

### Gramsci:

**POWER + CULTURE + CONSENT = HEGEMONY**

And:

**HEGEMONY → DOMINANT GROUPS vs SUBALTERN GROUPS**

---

# 🎯 NET MEMORY

### GRAMSCI = HEGEMONY

Whenever you see:

* consent
* cultural domination
* ruling ideology
* dominant/common sense
* subordinate groups

👉 Think **Antonio Gramsci**.

# 3️⃣ SUBALTERN STUDIES — 1980s

### 📅 Beginning

The **Subaltern Studies** project began in the early **1980s**.

The first major volume appeared in:

### 📖 *Subaltern Studies I*

**1982**

Edited by **Ranajit Guha**.

Full title:

### *Subaltern Studies I: Writings on South Asian History and Society*

🔥 **NET FACT: 1982 = Subaltern Studies begins**

---

# 🧠 WHY DID GUHA START THIS PROJECT?

Traditional colonial history often presented history from the viewpoint of:

### British rulers 👑

and

### Indian elite leaders 🏛️

Guha argued:

> What about ordinary people?

What about:

* peasants 🚜
* workers
* tribal communities
* lower social groups
* rebels
* marginalized communities?

Their historical agency was often ignored.

---

# 💥 GUHA'S MAIN IDEA

Traditional history:

> **ELITE → makes history**

Guha:

> **SUBALTERN PEOPLE ALSO MAKE HISTORY**

This is called:

# ⭐ SUBALTERN AGENCY

---

# 🧠 Very simple example

Imagine a history book about Indian independence that discusses only:

* Gandhi
* Nehru
* Jinnah
* British administrators

But says almost nothing about:

* peasants
* tribal rebellions
* workers
* local resistance
* ordinary people.

Guha asks:

> **Where are the voices and actions of the ordinary people?**

🔥 That's the central impulse of Subaltern Studies.

# 5️⃣ WHAT IS "HISTORY FROM BELOW"?

This phrase is very useful.

Traditional history often focuses on:

👑 Kings
🏛️ Governments
⚔️ Political leaders
📜 Elite institutions

History from below focuses on:

🚜 Peasants
👷 Workers
👩 Women
🧑 Indigenous groups
✊ Rebels
🌾 Rural communities

So:

> **Subaltern Studies = an attempt to recover the history and agency of marginalized groups.**

# 7️⃣ RANAJIT GUHA'S MAJOR CRITIQUE

Guha criticized both:

### 🇬🇧 Colonial historiography

and

### 🇮🇳 Elite nationalist historiography

Why?

Because both could marginalize ordinary people's independent political activity.

---

# 🧠 Important phrase

### "Elite politics" vs "subaltern politics"

Guha wanted scholars to examine:

> **autonomous domain of subaltern politics**

Meaning:

Subaltern groups were not simply puppets controlled by elites.

They could have:

* their own goals
* their own political consciousness
* their own forms of resistance
* their own collective action.

🔥 **AUTONOMY / AGENCY = important Guha ideas**

# 9️⃣ SPIVAK — "CAN THE SUBALTERN SPEAK?"

### 📅 1988

This is probably the **single most important text** for NET questions on subaltern theory.

### 📖 "Can the Subaltern Speak?"

Published in **1988**.

🔥 MEMORIZE:

> **SPIVAK → 1988 → CAN THE SUBALTERN SPEAK?**

---

# 🧠 What is Spivak asking?

At first glance the question sounds simple:

> Can marginalized people speak?

But Spivak's argument is much more complicated.

She asks:

> **Even if the oppressed person speaks, does the dominant system allow that voice to be heard, recognized, represented, and understood?**

🔥 That's the key.

---

# 🎯 SPEAKING ≠ BEING HEARD

This is one of the most important ways to remember Spivak.

### Person speaks 🗣️

BUT

### Institution controls representation 🏛️

Therefore:

> **Having a voice does not automatically mean having political or epistemic power.**

---

# 🔥 SPIVAK'S BIG IDEA: REPRESENTATION

Spivak asks:

### Who represents the subaltern?

And:

### Who gets represented?

And:

### Who has the power to speak for them?

---

# 🧠 Easy example

Imagine a poor rural woman.

She has her own experiences.

But then:

👨‍🎓 academic speaks about her
🏛️ government speaks about her
📰 journalist speaks about her
🌍 Western scholar speaks about her

The woman may disappear behind all these representations.

So Spivak asks:

> **Who is actually speaking?**

And:

> **Who is being spoken for?**

---

# 1️⃣0️⃣ "SUBALTERN CANNOT SPEAK" — BE CAREFUL! ⚠️

This is a very common NET trap.

Students sometimes write:

> "Spivak says oppressed people literally cannot speak."

❌ Too simplistic.

Her argument is about **structural conditions of representation and recognition**.

The problem is not simply:

> "Their mouths don't work."

😂

The problem is:

> **Can their speech enter the dominant structures of knowledge, power and representation on equal terms?**

# 1️⃣2️⃣ SATI AND SPIVAK

Spivak discusses the colonial and nationalist representations surrounding:

# 🔥 SATI

The historical practice in which a widow was expected or compelled in some contexts to die on her husband's funeral pyre.

Spivak examines how:

### British colonial discourse

and

### Indian patriarchal/nationalist discourse

could both speak **about** women.

But where is the woman's own voice?

# 🧠 Famous conceptual tension

### British:

> "We are saving Indian women."

versus

### Indian patriarchal discourse:

> "Indian tradition determines what women should do."

Spivak asks:

### Where is the woman's autonomous voice?

🔥 This is an excellent way to remember her argument.

# 1️⃣4️⃣ SPIVAK'S "EPISTEMIC VIOLENCE"

🔥 VERY IMPORTANT TERM

### Epistemic = related to knowledge

So:

> **Epistemic violence = violence done through systems of knowledge, representation, classification, and discourse.**

Simple example:

A colonial power describes a colonized population as:

> primitive
> irrational
> backward
> uncivilized

These descriptions aren't neutral.

They create a system where the colonized are positioned as inferior.

That's a form of:

### 🧠 KNOWLEDGE-BASED POWER

---

# 🎯 MEMORY

### SPIVAK =

**SUBALTERN + SPEAK + REPRESENTATION + EPISTEMIC VIOLENCE**

---

# 1️⃣5️⃣ SPIVAK'S "STRATEGIC ESSENTIALISM"

Another important term.

### Essentialism

means treating a group as if everyone in it has the same fixed identity.

For example:

> "All women are the same."

❌ That's essentialist.

Or:

> "All Indians think alike."

❌ Essentialist.

Spivak became associated with:

# ⭐ STRATEGIC ESSENTIALISM

Very simplified:

Sometimes politically marginalized groups may temporarily organize around a shared identity for political purposes.

For example:

> "We, as women, demand this right."

Even though women are internally diverse.

---

# ⚠️ NET CAUTION

Don't reduce strategic essentialism to:

> "Spivak believes all women are the same."

❌ Wrong.

It is about the **strategic political use of collective identity despite internal differences**.

# 🧠 What does "Provincializing Europe" mean?

Western European history has often been treated as:

> the universal model of modern history.

Chakrabarty asks:

> Why should European historical experience be treated as the universal standard for everyone?

So he tries to:

### decentralize Europe

and recognize different historical experiences.
