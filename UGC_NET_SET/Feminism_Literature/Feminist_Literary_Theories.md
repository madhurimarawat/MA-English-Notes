# 🌸 FEMINIST THEORY — FROM BEGINNING TO NOW

## 🧠 First: What is Feminist Theory?

Very simply:

> **Feminist theory studies how societies create, maintain and justify inequalities based on sex/gender, and asks how those inequalities can be challenged.**

It asks questions like:

* Why have women historically had less political power? ⚖️
* Why is women's work undervalued? 💼
* Who controls women's bodies? 🧍‍♀️
* Why are certain literary works considered "great" while women's writing is ignored? 📚
* How does language represent women?
* How do **race + class + sexuality + caste + colonialism** change women's experiences?
* Is "woman" even one universal category?
  
> **Woman → Equality → Patriarchy → Woman's writing → Difference → Race/Class → Gender → Sexuality → Intersectionality → Queer/Trans → Digital/Global feminism**

# 🌱 STAGE 1 — MARY WOLLSTONECRAFT

## 👩 Mary Wollstonecraft

### 📅 1792

### 📕 Major book

**A Vindication of the Rights of Woman**

This is one of the **most important starting points in feminist intellectual history**.

### Her basic argument

Society says:

> "Women are naturally inferior."

Wollstonecraft's response:

> **No. Women appear inferior because they are denied proper education.**

💡 Her solution:

**Education → Reason → Independence → Equality**

She wanted women to be educated as rational human beings rather than trained merely to please men.

### Easy memory

🧠 **WOLLSTONECRAFT = EDUCATION**

Think:

> **"Give women education, and you discover their reason."**

### NET facts

* Mary Wollstonecraft
* **A Vindication of the Rights of Woman**
* **1792**
* Enlightenment
* Rationality
* Education
* Equality
* Criticism of women's dependence

---

# ⚖️ STAGE 2 — JOHN STUART MILL

## 👨 John Stuart Mill

### 📅 1869

### 📕 **The Subjection of Women**

Mill argued that women's social position was not a natural necessity.

He attacked:

* Legal inequality
* Political inequality
* Women's exclusion from professions
* Marriage as a system of domination

His wife **Harriet Taylor Mill** was also extremely important to his feminist thinking.

### Easy memory

**Wollstonecraft = Education**

**Mill = Legal/social equality**

# 🔥 STAGE 3 — SIMONE DE BEAUVOIR

Now we reach one of the **MOST IMPORTANT names for UGC NET**.

## 👩 Simone de Beauvoir

### 📅 1949

### 📕 **The Second Sex**

This book changes feminist theory.

Her famous idea:

> **"One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman."**

### What does that mean?

Beauvoir distinguishes between:

**Biological sex** 🧬

and

**social construction of womanhood** 👩‍🦰

Society teaches girls:

* how to behave
* how to dress
* how to speak
* how to be feminine
* how to become wives/mothers
* what jobs are "appropriate"

Therefore:

> **Womanhood is socially produced.**

### Key concepts

* Woman as **Other**
* Man = Subject
* Woman = Other
* Immanence
* Transcendence
* Social construction

### 🔥 VERY IMPORTANT

Beauvoir says:

> **Man is treated as the universal human; woman becomes the "Other."**

### Easy memory

🧠 **BEAUVOIR = OTHER**

Remember:

> **MAN = SUBJECT 👨**
> **WOMAN = OTHER 👩**

# 📖 STAGE 5 — FEMINIST LITERARY CRITICISM

Now feminism enters **literature directly**.

The question becomes:

> "Why are women absent or misrepresented in literary history?"

and:

> "What happens when women become writers and critics?"

Two important approaches emerge.

---

# 🔍 FEMINIST CRITIQUE

Ask:

> **How are women represented in literature written within patriarchal culture?**

Look at:

* Female stereotypes
* Male gaze
* Objectification
* Patriarchal narratives
* Misogyny
* Representation

Example:

If a novel repeatedly portrays women as:

❌ passive
❌ irrational
❌ dependent
❌ beautiful objects

feminist criticism asks:

> **Why? Who benefits from this representation?**

---

# ✍️ GYNOCRITICISM

## 👩 Elaine Showalter

### 📅 1970s–1980s

### Major book

**A Literature of Their Own — 1977**

This is **MASSIVE for NET**.

Showalter distinguishes between:

### Feminist critique

🔍 Study how women are represented.

and

### Gynocriticism

✍️ Study **women as writers**.

---

# 🌸 GYNOCRITICISM

The question becomes:

> **"What is the tradition of women's writing?"**

Instead of studying women only in relation to male authors, Showalter wants to establish a female literary tradition.

### Memory

**GYNO = WOMEN**

So:

> **Gynocriticism = Study of women writers**

---

# 📚 SHOWALTER'S THREE PHASES OF WOMEN'S WRITING

Extremely useful for NET.

## 1️⃣ Feminine Phase

### approx. 1840–1880

Women writers imitate the dominant male tradition.

They often use:

* Male pseudonyms
* Conventional forms
* Accepted literary styles

Example:

**George Eliot**

(real name Mary Ann Evans)

---

## 2️⃣ Feminist Phase

### approx. 1880–1920

Women openly challenge:

* Patriarchy
* Women's oppression
* Social inequality

---

## 3️⃣ Female Phase

### 1920 onward

Women increasingly focus on:

> **Women's experience itself**

Rather than simply:

> "We want to be equal to men."

### 🔥 MEMORY

**F → F → F**

**Feminine → Feminist → Female**

> **"Women's writing"**

Women should write their bodies and experiences into language.

# 🌐 STAGE 9 — INTERSECTIONALITY

This is one of the **most important modern feminist concepts**.

## 👩 Kimberlé Crenshaw

### 📅 1989

### Major paper

**"Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race and Sex"**

She introduced the term:

# INTERSECTIONALITY

Imagine:

👩 Woman
+
✊ Black
+
💰 Working class
+
🌈 LGBTQ
+
♿ Disabled

A person can experience multiple systems of discrimination simultaneously.

### Example

A Black woman may experience:

**racism + sexism**

But those experiences aren't necessarily simply:

racism + sexism separately.

They can **intersect**.

That's intersectionality.

### 🔥 MEMORY

**CRENSHAW = INTERSECTION 🚦**

Imagine roads crossing:

> Race ↘
> Gender → **INTERSECTION** ← Class
> Sexuality ↗

# ⚧️ STAGE 10 — JUDITH BUTLER

Now feminism takes another huge turn.

## 👩 Judith Butler

### 📅 1990

### 📕 **Gender Trouble**

One of the most important texts in gender theory.

Butler questions:

> What exactly is "woman"?

And:

> Is gender naturally fixed?

Her answer challenges the idea that gender is simply a natural inner identity.

---

# 🎭 GENDER PERFORMATIVITY

This is Butler's famous concept.

**Gender is performed repeatedly.**

Through:

* Clothing
* Behaviour
* Speech
* Gestures
* Social roles
* Repeated practices

Think:

🎭 **PERFORMANCE**

Not:

> "I put on a costume occasionally."

But:

> Society repeatedly produces and reinforces gender through acts and norms.

### Important distinction

**Gender performativity ≠ simply pretending.**

That's a common exam trap.

---

# 🧠 BUTLER

### Key ideas

* Gender performativity
* Heteronormativity
* Gender instability
* Sex/gender questioning
* Queer theory
* Compulsory heterosexuality

### Memory

**BUTLER = GENDER IS PERFORMED**

🎭

🇮🇳 India

🇰🇪 Kenya
🇨🇱 Latin America

* Colonial history
* Race

Very important for UGC NET.

> **Who gets to speak?**

> **Who gets heard?**

# 👁️ THE MALE GAZE

### 📅 1975

### Essay

**"Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema"**

Very important.

She uses psychoanalysis and film theory to argue that mainstream cinema often positions women as:

👀 **objects to be looked at**

👁️ **viewer/active subject**

### Memory

**MULVEY = MALE GAZE 👁️**

### Male gaze

Woman → object 👩
