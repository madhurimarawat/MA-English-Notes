# 🕰️ PERIOD 1

# ENGLISH ARRIVES IN INDIA

## 1600s–1800s

English came to India initially through **trade and colonial expansion**, not through literature.

---

# 1️⃣ EAST INDIA COMPANY

### 1600

The **English East India Company** was established by royal charter under Queen Elizabeth I.

Initially its main purpose was:

💰 trade

not:

📚 English education.

---

# 2️⃣ EARLY ENGLISH CONTACT

English trading settlements developed in places such as:

* Surat
* Madras
* Bombay
* Calcutta

As British political control increased, English became increasingly associated with:

🏛️ administration
💼 commerce
⚖️ law
🎓 education

---

# 🧠 IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

### Early phase

> **English = language of traders and colonial administration**

### Later phase

> **English = language of education + administration + elite mobility**

---

This is **VERY IMPORTANT for NET**.

---

# 3️⃣ CHARTER ACT OF 1813 ⭐⭐⭐

### Date:

**1813**

This is one of the earliest major landmarks in British educational policy in India.

The Charter Act provided money for the promotion of education in India.

### Famous controversy

A major debate emerged:

## Orientalists 🆚 Anglicists

### Orientalists

Favoured:

* Sanskrit
* Arabic
* Persian
* traditional Indian learning

### Anglicists

Favoured:

* English
* Western science
* European literature
* Western education

### 🧠 NET FORMULA

> **1813 = education money + Orientalist/Anglicist debate**

---

# 4️⃣ THOMAS BABINGTON MACAULAY ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 1800–1859

One of the most important names in the history of English education in India.

---

# 📜 MACAULAY'S MINUTE

### 1835

**Macaulay's Minute on Indian Education — 1835**

This strongly supported:

> **English-medium Western education**

Macaulay argued for educating Indians through English and Western knowledge.

---

# 🧠 THE FAMOUS “CLASS” IDEA

Macaulay wanted to create a class of Indians who would act as intermediaries between British rulers and the wider Indian population.

This is often summarized as:

> **“Indian in blood and colour, but English in tastes…”**

⚠️ For NET, remember the **idea**, but don't confuse it with the entire educational policy.

---

# 🚨 NET TRAP

### Macaulay

❌ 1813

# 🇮🇳 PERIOD 8

# ENGLISH AFTER INDEPENDENCE

## 1947 onwards

Now comes the BIG QUESTION:

### Why did English survive after British rule ended?

Because English had already become deeply established in:

* administration
* higher education
* judiciary
* science
* technology
* interstate communication
* elite employment
* international communication

Therefore India did **not simply remove English** after 1947.

---

# 📜 CONSTITUTION OF INDIA

### Article 343

The Constitution provided for:

> **Hindi in Devanagari script as the official language of the Union**

while English continued for official purposes during the transition.

But English continued far beyond that initial period.

---

# ⚖️ OFFICIAL LANGUAGES ACT

## 1963

English continued to be used for official purposes of the Union.

The **Official Languages (Amendment) Act, 1967** further secured its continued use.

### 🧠 NET sequence

> **1950 Constitution → 1963 Official Languages Act → 1967 Amendment → English continues**

# 🎓 PERIOD 9

# THREE-LANGUAGE FORMULA

Associated with the:

## Education Commission / Kothari Commission

### 1964–66

The Three-Language Formula became an important framework for Indian language education.

Broadly:

1. Regional language / mother tongue
2. Hindi or another Indian language
3. English

The exact implementation has varied across states.

> **Kothari Commission → 1964–66 → Three-Language Formula**

This is a very important conceptual shift.

English in India is not simply British English spoken by Indians.

It develops distinctive features through contact with:

* Hindi
* Bengali
* Tamil
* Telugu
* Malayalam
* Marathi
* Punjabi
* Urdu
* Gujarati
* Kannada
* Assamese
* etc.

This creates:

# 🇮🇳 INDIAN ENGLISH

---

# 🗣️ WHAT IS INDIAN ENGLISH?

Indian English may show distinctive:

### Vocabulary

Examples:

* preponed
* lakh
* crore
* crore rupees
* goonda
* jugaad
* lakh/crore
* tuition
* batchmate
* outstation

### Grammar

Some Indian English constructions differ from British/American norms.

Example:

> “I am having two brothers.”

Standard international English would generally prefer:

> “I have two brothers.”

But Indian English has its own established usage patterns.

---

# 🧠 IMPORTANT

Don't think:

> Indian English = “wrong English”

Instead:

> **Indian English = a legitimate variety of World English shaped by Indian multilingualism and cultural contexts.**

# 🗣️ INDIAN ENGLISH ONLINE

One of the most interesting developments is:

## Hinglish

> **Hindi + English**

Examples:

> “Let's meet after lunch, phir movie dekhte hain.”

Or:

> “Mujhe assignment submit karna hai.”

This is not simply linguistic failure.

It can be understood as:

> **code-switching / code-mixing**

---

# 🔄 CODE-SWITCHING

A speaker moves between languages depending on:

* context
* audience
* identity
* topic
* social setting

Example:

> “Kal presentation hai, so please time pe aa jana.”

English + Hindi.

---

# 🧠 IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

### Code-switching

Changing between languages.

### Code-mixing

Mixing elements of different languages within speech.

### Translanguaging

Using one's complete multilingual linguistic repertoire rather than treating languages as completely isolated systems.

This is an increasingly important contemporary concept.
